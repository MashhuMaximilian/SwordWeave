# SwordWeave product marketing context

**Document version:** v1
**Last updated:** 2026-10-19
**Status:** Working draft. Product facts come from the repository and founder instructions; audience, positioning and outcome targets below are hypotheses until validated with players.

## Product overview

**One-liner:** SwordWeave is a free modular tabletop roleplaying game with browser tools for building characters and abilities from shared rules.

**What it does:** Players compose primitives, effects, capabilities, heritages and items. Groups agree fictional intent, scope, costs and consequences at the table. The website provides character tools, a shared library, rules readers and downloadable books.

**Category:** Tabletop roleplaying game; modular RPG; character and ability creation toolkit. It is its own game, not a claimed D&D-compatible character builder.

**Stage:** v0.1 alpha, founder-led. Public readers and core-book PDFs exist. Availability does not establish independent playtest success.

**Business model:** Free digital alpha. Printed PHB and GM Guide editions are a future possibility. Prices, fulfilment and sales channels are undecided. No paid advertising is planned.

## Target audience

**Primary hypothesis:** GMs and small groups who enjoy creating unusual characters and adapting rules together, and are willing to try an alpha.

**Secondary hypothesis:** Indie RPG creators, homebrewers and reviewers interested in modular character/ability construction.

**First participant to recruit:** A willing GM who can bring a table, run a session and report where the group struggled.

**Jobs to be done:**
- Express a character concept through understandable mechanics.
- Learn enough to run a first session without mastering the whole authoring engine.
- Create and share reusable game material.
- Discuss a ruling with the group and understand its consequences.

## Personas

Consumer/hobby project: no B2B buying committee. Distinguish GM, player and creator needs without forcing a corporate persona model.

## Problems and pain points

These are research hypotheses, not collected customer testimony:
- Preset options may not express the character someone imagines.
- Open-ended powers can require lengthy negotiation without useful examples.
- A flexible new system can be hard to learn or explain to a group.
- An alpha can be difficult to evaluate without a complete first-session package.

Test these through interviews and session reports. Do not assume every table wants greater mechanical freedom.

## Competitive landscape

Research references, not established superiority claims:
- Modular RPG systems, including Cortex.
- Point-based character systems and build tools.
- Free/open indie RPGs and their quickstarts.
- A table continuing with the game it already knows.

Compare learning effort, table procedures, supported tools and first-session experience before writing comparison content. Do not invent competitor weaknesses.

## Differentiation

Repository-supported capabilities:
- One shared primitive vocabulary across multiple kinds of game content.
- Browser tools to inspect and compose those pieces.
- Public rules and three core publications.
- Table discussion of scope, costs and consequences.

**Positioning hypothesis:** Creative freedom supported by inspectable rules and worked examples.

Avoid untested claims such as fastest, easiest, perfectly balanced, beginner-proof, rules-light or universally compatible. Validate whether “classless” is the right public description before making it a main search target.

## Objections and fit

| Likely objection (hypothesis) | What must answer it |
| --- | --- |
| “Will my group understand it?” | Short demo, ready characters and first-session material |
| “Does flexibility create arguments?” | Actual procedures and examples of agreeing scope and cost |
| “Can I rely on an alpha?” | Clear version, limitations, changelog and feedback route |
| “How was this made?” | Accessible founder credit and AI-use disclosure |

**Poor initial fit:** Groups seeking a finished, fully supported commercial product or a drop-in replacement for another game's tooling.

## Switching dynamics

**Push hypothesis:** A character idea does not fit available options.
**Pull hypothesis:** Try that idea in a system with flexible construction and browser support.
**Habit:** Existing group knowledge, campaigns and purchased material.
**Anxiety:** Learning time, unfinished rules, balance and ongoing support.

Reduce the first commitment to one prepared session. Do not require a campaign migration.

## Customer language

No independent player interviews or approved testimonial corpus has been collected in this chat.

**Founder language:** “A game friends would play”; “flexible and decided at a table, not rigid” (existing About page).

**Useful plain terms:** tabletop RPG, character concept, create an ability, play a scene, run a session, free rules, alpha playtest.

**Explain before using:** primitives, BU/Build Units, capabilities, Manifest, Atelier, Council.

**Avoid:** Corporate growth language in player-facing copy, unsupported superiority, “rules-light” without evidence, claims of D&D compatibility, ranking/AI-citation guarantees.

## Brand voice

Warm, imaginative, direct and practical. The founder is present as a person. Evocative names should have clear descriptions. Welcome beginners without implying that an unfinished alpha has no learning curve. Respect creative criticism and different table preferences.

## Proof points

**Available evidence:** Public website, tools, rules readers, books and worked examples in the repository.

**Not established:** Number of independent playing groups, retention, comparative balance, time to learn, testimonials or paid demand. Do not turn intended outcomes into claims.

**Reuse/disclosure:** Publication notices distinguish rules text/diagrams from brand, artwork and font rights. Refer to the actual notices instead of promising unrestricted rights to every asset. A founder-approved AI disclosure exists in `scripts/phb_ai_disclosure.py`.

## Goals

**Primary alpha outcome:** Independent groups complete a session and supply useful feedback.

**Suggested first milestone, pending founder review:** Five independent groups complete a session; record which choose to play again.

**Supporting actions:** Start guide read, book download click, first character completion, Discord participation, opt-in release subscription, session report.

**Measurement constraints:** Clicks are proxies. Discord invite clicks do not establish joins; downloads do not establish play. Do not send private character text or email addresses to analytics.

**Operating constraints:** Founder handles creator outreach personally. No paid ads. No automatic outreach, mass DMs or newsletters to account holders without appropriate subscription choice. Printed editions are a separate milestone.
