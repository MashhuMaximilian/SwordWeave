# Create & Publish

## You are welcome to create and sell
Make your own primitives, heritages, items, creatures, adventures, settings and compatible supplements. Free distribution, paid downloads and print products can all use appropriately licensed SwordWeave material. The open-system license requires no SwordWeave royalty for that use.

This guide explains the existing license and recorded product policy. It does not replace CC BY 4.0, change the repository's licenses, grant rights to unrelated assets or establish a storefront contract. Edition: **21 October 2026**, for **v0.1 alpha**.

## Begin with one useful promise
Say who the product helps and what they can do with it: run one situation, use a creature, adopt an upbringing or build a particular kind of working. State the rules version and whether any table variations are required.

Make playable content complete on the page. Include the actual permissions, values, conditions, targets, stacking and ending terms needed to use it. A website link adds convenience; it should not hide essential paid-product rules behind a missing catalogue entry.

## Build from the shared framework
Use **Create** for authoring and accounting, **Play** for resolution and **Run** for monsters/encounters. Preserve the distinction between core rules, defaults, your authored examples and optional variations.

Use personal stories and new arrangements freely within those rules. State a new acquisition's proposed price for table review. Your adventure can introduce situations and creative material without pretending every example is a universal system rule.

**Example product:** “Lockside Paths” offers a canal encounter, a local upbringing and three tools. It reproduces the definitions needed to run them, identifies their sources, and labels its proposed prices and optional variations. The plot can have separate terms from the openly licensed rules it includes.

## Keep a small provenance ledger
For each reused contribution record **title/file; author; source; license/permission; changes; required credit**. Include text, maps, artwork, fonts, icons, commissioned work and community contributions. A public download or visible Library entry is not by itself a blanket reuse license.

## Understand the material boundaries
| Material | What to check |
| --- | --- |
| Designated SwordWeave rules and explanatory game text | CC BY 4.0. Preserve attribution, notices and changes information. Commercial use is allowed. |
| New original contributions you control | Choose and identify their terms. Keep inherited CC rights intact. Contributors retain their own rights unless an agreement says otherwise. |
| SwordWeave instructional vector diagrams/maps | The illustrated alpha designates original instructional vectors CC BY 4.0. Confirm the particular source notice. |
| AI illustrations, other images, maps and third-party assets | Read the specific asset notice. The text license is not blanket asset permission. |
| Name, wordmark, logo and branding | CC BY does not grant trademark rights or official endorsement. Use factual compatibility wording. |
| Software source | Its existing MIT notice is separate from the game-text license. Retain required notices when reusing that code. |

The repository's existing CC BY scope includes designated rules, primitives, canonical mechanics and descriptive lore text. Do not relabel already-open material as newly exclusive. Identify any separately protected new plot, art or other contribution precisely.

## Make compatibility a clear claim
Suggested wording: **“Compatible with SwordWeave v0.1 alpha. Independently created; not an official SwordWeave product.”** This is descriptive wording, not a newly granted compatibility-logo license.

Do not imply certification, sponsorship or endorsement. Do not package the official wordmark or other branding as your own. If you want to reuse a particular logo or press asset, check its actual permission first.

**Official reference:** [swordweave.quest](https://www.swordweave.quest/). Link the source edition used where practicable. An alpha product should state its date/version so readers can distinguish your variation from later system changes.

## What CC BY 4.0 permits
You may copy and adapt the designated material, including commercially. Credit the supplied author, preserve applicable notices, link the source and license, and identify modifications. Keep supplied earlier change information and disclaimer notices where applicable. Attribution must not imply endorsement.

You may choose terms for your own contributions, but those terms must not obstruct compliance with CC BY or remove recipients' rights in inherited licensed material. Do not add legal restrictions or technical locks that prevent its licensed reuse. CC BY does not require every separate new contribution to use the same license.

See the [license summary](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/) and [complete legal terms](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/legalcode.en). The legal terms govern; a summary or example notice is not a replacement.

## Attribution you can adapt
The repository requests this credit statement:

> This work is based on the SwordWeave SRD, a product of Marius Ion, licensed under the Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International License.

Add the actual source/version, license link and a truthful changes statement. For a modified compatible example:

> Source: SwordWeave v0.1 alpha by Marius Ion, https://www.swordweave.quest/. License: CC BY 4.0, https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/. Changes: condensed the encounter procedure and added original canal situations. Independently created; no official endorsement.

These are reusable wording examples. Replace the example changes with your actual changes, or say the reused text is unchanged. Retain any additional supplied attribution or third-party notices relevant to the material you actually used.

## Separate your license scopes
In your credits, identify inherited open text, your original contributions and separately licensed assets. Give each its actual author/source/terms. Explain where those categories occur so a reader can tell what they may reuse.

For “Lockside Paths,” a notice could distinguish its quoted/adapted SwordWeave rules under CC BY 4.0 from the author's separately licensed new plot and commissioned map. The actual map agreement must permit your intended sale; naming it in the credits alone grants no rights.

## Selling your compatible product
Choose your own selling channel, price and format subject to that service's terms and the rights in your materials. Selling a compatible product does not transfer your original contributions to SwordWeave or create a system-use royalty under CC BY.

A future SwordWeave bookstore is a separate arrangement. Hosting permission, selling/delivery rights, commission, payouts, moderation and removal terms still need agreement with the creator. No commission rate or platform publishing contract is established by this guide.

The recorded official policy keeps **every official playable option freely accessible**, even if introduced in a future paid adventure. That policy concerns official releases; it does not add a new noncommercial restriction to CC BY or require unrelated creators to give away all their original products.

## Sharing official books and adventures
The three complete digital corebooks are free. Their notices permit complete-book noncommercial sharing with friends and personal/table printing, while designated open text retains its broader CC BY rights. A coffee contribution is voluntary.

Before selling or extracting material from a complete illustrated book, check separately identified assets. The open text license alone does not authorize commercial reuse of every illustration, font or branding element in that PDF.

**Future official paid-adventure preference, not finalized terms:** private sharing with friends, including onward private sharing, with an optional coffee invitation. Separate protected story/maps/art would not receive commercial resale permission through that sharing policy. Exact adventure notices remain to be drafted. Existing open grants and statutory physical-resale rights are not retracted.

## Artwork, AI and third-party rights
Use assets you made or have permission to distribute in your intended product. Keep contributor agreements and specific license records. Commissioning, finding or generating an image does not by itself settle every underlying right.

For SwordWeave AI art, use the particular asset notice and generation provenance; do not assume exclusive copyright or a blanket extraction license. Credit generation, direction, human edits and references truthfully. Other publishers' AI-disclosure requirements depend on their current rules; check them when choosing a channel.

Fonts have separate licenses. Embedding a font in a PDF and redistributing its font file can have different conditions. Keep the notices and permissions applicable to the actual files used.

## A practical release checklist
1. **Identify the edition.** Title, author/contact, SwordWeave rules version, product revision and date are present.
2. **Check the rules.** Actual ingredients, acquisitions, recipients, conditions, costs, stacking and endings are readable. Separate player, creature and Item BU. Identify optional variations and pending alpha rules.
3. **Run the examples.** Recalculate bonuses, DC, Vitality, prices, quantities and encounter totals. Test normal use, a counter-approach and a changed dependency.
4. **Check rights by asset.** Preserve required notices and source links. Confirm commercial distribution/embedding permissions. State changes and avoid implied endorsement.
5. **Inspect the final PDF.** Selectable text, readable type, embedded fonts, intact tables, correct links, page order and complete edges. Read at ordinary viewing/printing size.
6. **Keep a source checkpoint.** Preserve editable text, necessary licensed assets and a release manifest. Record filename, version, byte count, SHA-256 and source revision.
7. **Give readers a useful route back.** Provide correction/contact information and explain actual update/support arrangements. Claims about testing, contributors and AI use must match what happened.

## A note from the creator / AI and this alpha
AI was used in creating the SwordWeave platform, these books and their artwork. I am not a programmer by trade. The system’s design, and the platform’s UI and UX, are my own. For implementation, AI was mostly a set of extra arms: a tool I could work with, like a hammer or a sword. It also reasoned through and solved programming problems; that contribution deserves acknowledgement.

For the book illustrations and heritage artwork, AI took the lead in art direction as well as creating the images. The aesthetic of the profile images was its proposal, and it clicked for me on the first try. I explored some alternatives, but kept returning to that first direction because I loved it. I want to give credit where it is due.

This alpha is a one-person project. I chose to get it into people’s hands for feedback and play rather than spend many more months producing the books. I do not currently have the funds to hire artists. My hope for v1.0 is to involve more people across the system, platform, writing, design and artwork. For now, this is what I can make and share, with AI’s help. Thank you for giving it a place at your table.

## Keep the distinctions clear
This creator acknowledgement carries the approved note from the books. It describes the wider project's use of AI; these compact sheets introduce no new generated illustrations. Acknowledgement and reuse permission serve different purposes. The material notices and actual license determine reuse.

**For your product:** identify its real authors, contributors, tools and supplied assets. If you include an AI-use statement, describe what AI actually did and what humans actually did. Do not describe hoped-for future human collaboration as completed work.

**Next step for a creator:** take one complete, tested creation, attach its true provenance and permission notices, and publish only the files you have checked. The open framework is an invitation to make a personal contribution.
