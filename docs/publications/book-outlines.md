# Three book outlines

Date: 10 October 2026. Proposed architecture; chapter lengths follow writing and testing.

Shared references: `rules-reconciliation.md` and [the full-system audit](full-rules-audit-2026-10-10.md). The eight-page proof's visual direction is approved; it demonstrates voice and layout, not the finished books. Apply the audit's six editorial labels consistently across all manuscripts.

## Shared rules and different reader needs

The PHB must contain every rule a player needs to create, develop and play a character: composition, targeting, checks, combat/movement/reactions, scaling/cost, upkeep, damage/conditions/recovery, equipment, progression and mirrors. Players must not need to consult the SRD or GM Guide to obtain missing core procedures. The SRD repeats these same mechanics in a concise reference form and adds the necessary creature/GM framework and openly designated game content. Repeated coverage is intentional; contradictions are not.

The GM Guide explains applying those rules to situations, preparing encounters/adventures, choosing consequences, authoring opposition and running campaigns. Essential adjudication is also stated in the SRD so the open reference can support a complete game.

The current manuscripts are uneven partial drafts: the PHB now has all nine main teaching chapters drafted, while the GM Guide has its first chapter and the SRD still needs full base-family definitions. This is a writing-progress difference, not the intended division of rules. Treat each shared subject as incomplete until both its PHB teaching version and SRD reference version exist and agree.

The creator's latest scope is construction-led: base primitive families and tiers, complete play/construction rules and a few worked examples. Teach creating lineages/upbringings/manifests, capabilities, items and creatures. Do not invent a class subsystem to imitate another game's chapter labels or require the 100-monster set and finished heritage catalogue as book appendices. All necessary base definitions must be readable offline. Platform links are supplementary. See [comparative publication research](comparative-publication-research.md).

## Player's Handbook

| Chapter | Reader outcome | Content and rules |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Begin a shared story | A first-time player understands the people, dice, scene and play loop. | Roles, expectations, intent, a complete check and an original scene. SW-PLAY-01, SW-ROLL-01. |
| 2. Make someone you want to play | Build a usable character from a concept and explain each purchase. | Attributes, proficient attribute, practices, verb/domain foundation, lineage/upbringing/manifest, 25-BU ledger, optional items and drawbacks. SW-BUILD, SW-BU. |
| 3. Read and compose your abilities | Distinguish permissions, numerical rules, recipes and outcomes. | Primitives, effects, capabilities, conditional rules, stacking, reusable purchases, improvised actions, sensory targeting/area alternatives/authored exceptions and mirroring. SW-BUILD-01, SW-BU-04, SW-ITEM-01. |
| 4. Resolve uncertainty | Choose an appropriate check and read a complete numerical example. | Ten practices, PB, expertise, advantage/disadvantage, attacks, single DC, three saves and rounding. SW-ROLL. |
| 5. Act together in combat | Coordinate one complete shared round. | Council, tracks, split/independent movement across the round, clashes, contests, pivoting and reactions. Mark surprise treatment as optional advice. SW-COMBAT. |
| 6. Push the limits | Negotiate an unusual action without confusing BU with effort. | Scope, permissions, scale, impact, complexity, urgency, Strain, Cost and optional CV explanation. SW-SCALE, SW-STRAIN. |
| 7. Keep a working alive | Track duration and maintenance accurately. | One capability/one upkeep track, multiple workings, mechanical/restrictive/narrative upkeep and contextual interruption. Leave an explicit draft placeholder for the deferred Vitality damage-trigger/reset policy. SW-UPKEEP. |
| 8. Survive and carry consequences | Apply damage and explain rescue and recovery. | Vitality, source/domain defenses, collapse, massive damage, contextual conditions, healing, stabilization, agreed short/long rests and actual-used short-rest carryover. SW-DAMAGE, SW-VIT, SW-COND. |
| 9. Equipment and growth | Track Item BU, equipment and purchases made during play. | Six slots, Load, sizes, cover, rewards, remaining BU, progression table and mirror ceilings. SW-ITEM, SW-BU. |
| Base primitive reference and construction examples | Understand the building blocks and build without needing the website. | Main primitive families, actual base definitions, family-specific tiers and suggested prices; a few complete character/heritage/capability/item constructions with ledgers and demonstrated uses. No catalogue of forks or finished heritages. |
| References | Find a needed rule during play. | Glossary, practice table, one-round reference, progression, formulas, index and a brief guide to reading a creature's public rules when relevant. |

A short optional website companion explains sheets and builders; the main handbook remains playable offline. No class progression, spell slots, initiative rolls or fixed XP schedule is imported.

## Game Master's Guide

| Chapter | GM outcome | Content and rules |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Your first session | Prepare a short adventure and run it with new players. | Expectations, boundaries, objective, a few locations, people, threats and an annotated session example. |
| 2. Make rulings understandable | State scope, stakes, resolution and cost before commitment. | Intent/access checklist, clarification questions, scale reduction, time trade-offs, failures that change the scene and bounded example rulings. SW-PLAY, SW-STRAIN. |
| 3. Run exploration and conversation | Support several approaches without turning practice names into permission gates. | Evidence, obstacles, uncertainty, opposing motives, player plans and contextual consequences. SW-ROLL-01, SW-COND. |
| 4. Run the combat rhythm | Coordinate opposition, intent, track and reactions without initiative. | Annotated multi-participant round, clashes versus contests, pivots, visible telegraphing and an encounter worksheet. SW-COMBAT. |
| 5. Judge ambitious workings | Apply owned access, scaling, Strain and maintenance consistently. | Paired examples at different scope/time pressure, selective outcomes, CV as optional analysis, interruption windows and ongoing costs. SW-SCALE, SW-STRAIN, SW-UPKEEP. |
| 6. Design creatures and encounters | Build useful opposition and understand what BU does and does not compare. | Shared construction grammar, base/spent/remaining creature BU, separate Item BU, quantities, roles, terrain, objectives, tactical concentration and retreat. SW-ENCOUNTER. |
| 7. Rewards and campaign growth | Agree pacing without a mandatory reward schedule. | Milestones, smaller BU awards, separate treasure value/Item BU/Load and clearly marked optional loot guidance. SW-BU, SW-ITEM. |
| 8. Author and review new rules | Turn a concept into a comprehensible component. | Recipients, conditions, stacking, prose permissions, access versus output, mirrors and edge-case review. |
| 9. Build adventures for your table | Prepare playable choices and situations. | Locations, clues, objectives, NPC motivations, consequences, scene transitions and adaptable preparation. |
| Worked creatures and scenes | Build opposition and understand its role in play. | A few fully checked creature constructions with actual abilities/equipment, ledgers, practical tactics and an encounter situation. Show how to adapt them. The seeded bestiary remains an optional platform resource; no full bestiary appendix is required. |
| Toolbox | Reach a practical answer quickly. | Ruling worksheet, round tracker, upkeep ledger, consequence record, monster template and cost examples. |

Use real procedures and contrasting worked situations. Optional numerical scaffolding is labelled as guidance, not a hidden mandatory balance formula.

## System Reference Document

| Section | Reference purpose |
| --- | --- |
| 1. Release and use | Rules version, material scope, license, attribution, errata and terminology conventions. |
| 2. Core procedure | Participants, scene, intent, access, agreement, checks and consequences. Essential GM adjudication remains included. |
| 3. Character foundation | Attributes, practice mapping, PB, proficiency/expertise, creation requirements, heritages and budget ledger. |
| 4. Progression and mirrors | Literal threshold table, item separation, awards, purchase reuse, ownership, credit/debt constraints and compatible operations. |
| 5. Construction grammar | Primitive classifications, effects, capabilities, recipients, operations, conditions and stacking. Explain adaptation/forking without reproducing a fork catalogue. Descriptive rules remain valid. |
| 6. Resolution | Checks, attacks, one DC, three saves, bias, rounding, clashes and contests. |
| 7. Combat | Council and tracks, movement, reaction defaults, timing and changed intent. |
| 8. Scale and cost | Access, declared dials, Strain guidance, agreed costs and clearly distinguished optional CV analysis. |
| 9. Persistence | Track, duration, upkeep, interruption and maintained-capability accounting. |
| 10. Survival and equipment | Vitality, damage defenses, conditions, collapse/recovery, Load, slots, sizes and cover. |
| 11. Base primitive family/tier reference | Principal Market families and actual base definitions: verb/domain/structure tiers, range/output ladders, attribute/practice grants, modifiers/conditions/stacking, movement/senses, equipment and other necessary foundation rules. Include suggested prices and restrictions. Access tiers, output ladders and cost guidance are distinct. Exclude derived catalogue variants and forks. |
| 12. Heritage and capability construction | How to compose lineages/upbringings/manifests and capabilities/effects, with ingredient definitions, purchases, conditions and scope. Explain reuse, authoring and suggested pricing. Include a few checked constructions rather than a finished-option catalogue. |
| 13. Item construction and accounting | How to create/use an item; Item BU, final slot requirements, Load and equipped capabilities/modifiers. One worked item can demonstrate the procedure; an exhaustive equipment catalogue is unnecessary. |
| 14. Creature construction and encounter reference | Complete construction/resolution formulas, reusable record structure, quantity and separate creature/Item BU appraisal, essential adjudication and a few usable worked creature/encounter examples. No 100-monster appendix or Monster Manual requirement. |
| 15. Examples and design reference | A few complete characters and worked ledgers/rounds/rests; required adjudication and creator terminology. |
| Appendices | Glossary, tables, rule IDs and changes. |

Publish a coherent, independently usable reference. A legal notice, abstract grammar or a collection of links is not enough. Include the base definitions and construction procedures sufficient to create characters and creatures and run scenes without access to the platform. The seeded monsters, finished heritages and public/community forks remain separate resources. Select only example material with established reuse rights, designate the open mechanical/text scope and keep artwork/other asset notices explicit.

The creator has clarified rest, bias, targeting/range, slots and movement. Keep PUB-01's numerical upkeep question as an explicitly deferred placeholder while writing; settle it before final release. Apply PUB-04's editorial conventions throughout.

## Writing and review order

1. Use the completed full-system audit to expand the register and resolve the remaining core details, preserving recovered rest/stabilization sources.
2. Retain the approved eight-page aesthetic; refine voice, clarity and density using real chapter drafts.
3. Write PHB chapters while maintaining matching SRD definitions.
4. Write the GM guide using those definitions and original situations.
5. Assemble and review the complete SRD.
6. Test with a new player and a GM using only the manuscript; verify formulas and worked examples.
7. Copyedit, index, typeset and release versioned web/PDF editions.

Before typesetting, check coverage in both directions: every SRD player rule appears in the PHB, every PHB rule has a matching reference definition, and every example/creature has its required dependencies and checked arithmetic. Teaching prose can be longer; mechanics must agree.
