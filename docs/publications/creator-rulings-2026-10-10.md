# Creator rulings after the full-system audit

10 October 2026. Explicit creator clarifications supersede older advice and audit candidates. This register records accepted wording separately from questions still being answered. The full books are not yet written.

## Upkeep and consequences

- Upkeep is an ongoing consequence, not necessarily damage or a Vitality payment. It can be a numerical modifier, restriction, loss of access until an agreed condition, task, resource requirement or narrative consequence; combinations are possible.
- Maintaining a capability generally spreads out its cost or makes it cheaper upfront than forcing an increased expression of that same capability in one round. There is no mandatory percentage discount.
- Use an explicit agreed maintenance point within the shared rhythm. Council was proposed and accepted in principle; exact Vitality damage-trigger/reset wording is being clarified below.
- Lost access or other consequences need their own duration and ending condition; a new round does not silently erase them.

**Explicitly deferred by the creator:** leave a placeholder for Vitality-only damage-triggered repayment and counter reset; continue the manuscripts without resolving it. The current app wording requests immediate additional repayment once incoming damage reaches the upkeep amount. That historical wording is not approval of the proposed reset/excess policy. Do not automate it or present the proposal as a settled publication rule. Normal agreed continuation upkeep and contextual consequences remain available.

## Rest, recovery and rescue

- A short rest is a short pause, perhaps 15 or 30 minutes. A long rest is overnight sleep or an equivalent rest, not a mandatory eight-hour duration.
- DM and players agree whether the circumstances earn the effects of either rest; elapsed time alone never applies them automatically.
- Rest can provide partial recovery. Example: the table may reduce recovery after a poor five-hour night to 70%. Agree the amount and consequence rather than surprise the players with an unstated automatic rule.
- The proposed short-rest allowance between long rests was accepted, with unused points carrying forward. Only actual Vitality restored consumes it. Long-rest healing is not charged against that short-rest pool; an agreed long rest refreshes the pool.
- **Confirmed in a subsequent direct reply:** all ordinary rounding is upward; the original 5-point example was arithmetic error. Half maximum 13 rounds up to an allowance of 7. At 10/13, heal 3 to full and retain 4 for later short rests after further damage.
- Unused recovery persists across short rests until a completed long rest refreshes it. DM adjustment remains available by table agreement. Long-rest healing is independent of the short-rest pool.
- Stabilization halts the rescue countdown without automatically granting Vitality or consciousness. Healing above zero ends the dying state and restores consciousness unless another consequence prevents it. Lingering consequences use their stated recovery conditions. These proposals were accepted along with recovery.

## Targeting and range

- Specific-target actions require locating the target through an applicable sense: vision, blindsight, tremorsense or another granted method. Ordinary eyesight is not the only valid method.
- Without that location, propose a known spot or area and resolve the uncertainty at the table. Choosing an area does not guarantee the desired creature is inside it.
- Specific workings may establish different methods: scrying, widespread weather influence or a stated “any within range” permission. Explain access, targeting and consequences explicitly.
- Range is an authored permission and practical scope. The DM can agree an extension, including Extreme beyond three miles. No sensory permission is gained merely by extending range.
- Current catalogue values remain Very Far 120 ft and Extreme 240 ft–3 miles. The creator recalled 300/600 ft as possible alternatives but did not choose a replacement. Do not silently rewrite existing versioned entries or adopt a remembered value.
- The creator accepted the range proposal while emphasizing sensory targeting and table-agreed extensions. Book wording uses continuous Extreme reach up to approximately three miles, including the 121–239-ft transition, with agreed extensions possible. Preserve Very Far 120 ft as the established reference until a different number is explicitly chosen. The old catalogue's 240-ft lower-bound phrase remains historical data to reconcile when updating entries; it is not a minimum targeting distance in the manuscript.

## Stacking, bias and primitive authoring

- Applicable sources stack by default unless their authored stacking rules specify otherwise. Repeated copies must still obey their stated uniqueness, scope, conditions and other restrictions.
- Advantage and disadvantage cancel one-for-one, leaving a net stack count. Two advantages and one disadvantage leave one advantage.
- **Confirmed in a subsequent direct reply:** one extra resolution die per remaining stack. N advantage stacks roll N + 1 dice and keep the highest; N disadvantage stacks keep the lowest. Zero net stacks rolls one die.
- Suggested Library/builder BU prices are authoring guidance. The creator selects a price; the DM may accept it or request a cheaper/more expensive version. Scope, restrictions and stacking can justify a different price.

## Equipped slots

- Six universal base equipment slots; primitives can extend them.
- Ordinary equipped item: 1 slot. Two-handed item: 2 slots.
- An unusual permitted item can have a greater authored requirement or another agreed consequence. Example: a small character wielding a large sword can require 3–4 slots.
- Size alone is not a compulsory slot multiplier. Load remains a different calculation.
- The old engine did multiply item size automatically when stored slot cost equalled its baseline. That calculation has been corrected to respect this current ruling, retaining explicitly higher costs and quantity.

## Movement and surprise

- Speed is the ordinary movement allowance across a whole round. Spend some or all, split among tracks or valid reactions; do not get another allowance at each track.
- Movement may accompany an action/intent or occur independently as the scene permits. Reaction triggers/scope and actual interference still matter.
- Further movement requires an appropriate speed primitive or an agreed cost/consequence. The Rule of Cool and a coherent, engaging, cinematic scene guide case-by-case rulings.
- No universal surprise mechanic. **Optional advice:** the prepared enemies may complete one round before surprised participants respond. The circumstances or an agreed d20 check may change this treatment. It is not an automatic ambush entitlement.
