# SwordWeave: licensing and revenue, in plain language

10 October 2026. Decision aid, not a replacement license or a revenue forecast. No prices, checkout, membership or site notices have been published by this document.

**Current scope:** the PHB, GM Guide and SRD will be complete free digital books. [Their focused rights policy](book-rights-policy.md) takes precedence over earlier recommendations here. Revenue options remain later possibilities; native platform selling may be considered later. For future adventures, the creator explicitly wants private sharing with friends (including onward private sharing), a voluntary coffee invitation for each recipient, and no commercial resale permission for separately protected creative material.

## 1. Three separate questions

| Question | What it controls | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Price | What the customer pays | A free rulebook, a paid adventure, a voluntary tip. |
| License | What someone may copy, change and publish | They may reuse the SRD with credit; they may not automatically redistribute your separately protected adventure PDF. |
| Visibility | Who sees a platform record | Public, followers only, or private. |

A free book can have reserved rights. A paid book can contain open rules. A public collection is not automatically open licensed. Followers-only access is not a copyright license. Buying a book does not normally transfer its copyright.

You can choose different terms for different future books, atlases and adventures, provided you own or have the necessary rights. Any reused material keeps its existing obligations.

## 2. What each proposed label means

### CC BY 4.0: rules people can build on

People can copy the designated material, translate it, adapt it and sell products using it. They must give appropriate credit, link to the license and identify changes; they cannot imply your endorsement. They can also sell a straight copy of that licensed material. You can sell your own edition too. Compliant permissions cannot later be withdrawn. [Creative Commons deed](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/).

For SwordWeave, use this for the SRD and expressly designated mechanical rule text/entries. “Free to read” by itself would not give creators these reuse permissions. CC BY does. It does not require creators to give you a cut or to release their own additions under CC BY.

### MIT: reusable software code

People may use, modify and sell the designated code, including in closed products, while retaining the copyright and license notice. It does not mean that your running website, hosting or support is free for you to provide. The actual [repository notice](../../LICENSE) currently applies broadly to software source; it does not yet establish a separate proprietary platform boundary.

### Reserved rights: separately identified new creative material

For new adventure plots, original setting prose, maps, illustrations and branding, you can reserve the rights you control while permitting table use, personal/table printing and private sharing of the complete PDF with friends. Onward private sharing is also intended to be allowed. Each recipient is invited to buy a coffee voluntarily. The intended permission excludes commercial resale of separately protected adventure material; public uploading and other reuse will need explicit later terms. Existing open mechanical content keeps its license.

This scope needs to be explicit. Already licensed text does not become closed because it appears in a paid adventure. Third-party assets and creator submissions need their own permission records. A reserved-rights label is not a guarantee that every element is independently protectable.

## 3. A concrete adventure example

Suppose you publish **The Bell Beneath the Harbour**; this is a hypothetical title.

| Part | Suggested treatment | What another creator can do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| New primitive/capability/heritage rule text designated open | CC BY 4.0, linked from its public mechanical collection | Reuse it in their own compatible product, including commercially, with credit. |
| Previously open rules repeated in the adventure | Keep their existing license | Continue exercising those permissions. |
| New story, secrets, location descriptions and owned maps/art | Separate adventure notice, drafted later | Use at their table and share privately with friends under the intended permission; no commercial resale permission for these protected parts. Other uses follow the eventual terms and applicable law. |
| Logo and official endorsement | Separate brand policy | Describe compatibility under the policy; do not claim official endorsement. |
| Community material | Its creator's actual license | Follow that creator's permission, not a blanket platform assumption. |

The product includes a PDF, player handouts, maps and links to ready-to-copy encounter/monster collections. The mechanical collection stays free. The paid value is the adventure, artwork, editing and preparation already done for the GM.

Keep spoiler text and protected downloadable files out of a public collection if they are intended only for buyers. A public R2 URL is publicly downloadable; a secret-looking link is not access control. The free SRD can use public R2 delivery. Paid files need the storefront's delivery or a private authenticated download path.

## 4. Recommended book policy

- **SRD:** free access; clearly designated CC BY 4.0 text; attribution and version information.
- **Player's Handbook and GM Guide:** complete free digital editions. Designate original game-rule explanations/examples with the open text; identify separately licensed artwork, branding and third-party material in the credits. See the focused book policy for the proposed release notice and asset permission checks.
- **New adventures and atlases:** choose free, pay-what-you-want or fixed-price per product. Reserve new creative material where intended, with generous personal table-use permission.
- **Linked mechanical collections:** free access to designated rules, with per-entry license and provenance. Collection ownership, saving and cloning do not replace rights notices.
- **Community works:** their authors choose their permitted terms. SwordWeave needs permission to host/show them; it does not automatically own them.

A product page and its credits should identify what is open, what is reserved, who owns it, which collection/version it uses, what buyers may do with handouts/assets, and which third-party notices apply. This is a proposed notice structure, not a license already granted.

The existing repository has already included descriptive lore under CC BY and source under MIT. We must inventory those grants before changing future notices. This is about preserving past permissions, not asking you to charge for races or mechanics.

Blades in the Dark provides a useful precedent: its open SRD is distinguished from setting, artwork and maps. Its specific license version/policy is its own; SwordWeave would use its own explicit scope. [Publisher licensing page](https://bladesinthedark.com/licensing).

## 5. Revenue options that fit your preferences

These are recommendations and tradeoffs, not evidence that a particular income level will follow.

| Model | What people buy/support | Why it fits | Main cost/tradeoff | My priority |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| One-time adventure packs | Finished adventures, maps, handouts, ready-made encounters and linked collections | Gives a GM a concrete reason to pay while mechanics remain accessible | Writing, playtesting, editing and delivery | First paid product |
| Pay-what-you-want starter | A complete short introduction to playing SwordWeave | Lets people try it and support voluntarily | Many downloads may pay nothing | First acquisition product |
| Optional supporter membership | Development support, progress posts, credits, optional cosmetic rewards | More regular support without a mechanics subscription | Recurring expectations; keep promised rewards sustainable | Optional, simple tier |
| Premium print editions/table tools | Physical books, cards, reference screens or illustrated atlases | Tangible convenience and presentation | Print proofs, shipping, support and unit economics | After digital demand |
| Preorders/crowdfunding | A defined, scoped adventure or print run | Can fund a known production target | Delivery obligations and campaign work | After a finished sample and interested audience |
| Commissions or paid hosted sessions | Your time creating a custom adventure or running a game | Can earn from a small audience | Revenue scales with your available time | Occasional optional service |
| Community marketplace commission | Optional sales by other creators | Could help a creator ecosystem later | Payments, moderation, creator rights and support overhead | Much later |

Optional paid platform conveniences are another possible future model, but should be evaluated after we know actual demand and operating costs. Keep creation, rules access and gameplay viable without a subscription under your current policy. There is no need to build a paid account system now.

Ironsworn is a concrete example of free complete digital rules alongside purchasable print books and additional products. This demonstrates that the offerings can coexist; it does not establish SwordWeave's potential revenue. [Official Ironsworn page](https://tomkinpress.com/pages/ironsworn).

## 6. Where to sell

Fees below were checked on 10 October 2026 and can change. Processor, currency, payout and tax effects mean a platform fee is not your total selling cost.

| Channel | Current published terms/features | SwordWeave use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Buy Me a Coffee | 5% platform fee; its help page also lists processor/payout and possible extra transaction charges. Supports tips, memberships and shop purchases. | Keep the existing support link; simplest place to add optional support without another community to maintain. |
| itch.io | Creator-selected platform revenue share, plus payment-provider costs. Supports minimum-zero/pay-what-you-want products and paid digital products. | Recommended first digital adventure storefront linked from your website. |
| DriveThruRPG | Publisher help states 65% non-exclusive / 70% exclusive royalties for digital sales; actual agreement needs checking before signup. | Optional additional RPG storefront; evaluate the lower net receipt against its audience and sales tools. |
| Patreon | New creator pages after 4 August 2025 use a 10% standard platform plan, plus relevant fees/taxes. | Only if its community/membership workflow is worth maintaining separately. |

Sources: [Buy Me a Coffee fees](https://help.buymeacoffee.com/en/articles/8105744-how-to-calculate-charges-on-your-payment), [support/shop features](https://help.buymeacoffee.com/en/articles/8347891-supporter-dashboard-explained), [itch.io payments](https://itch.io/docs/creators/payments), [itch.io pricing](https://itch.io/docs/creators/pricing), [DriveThru publisher royalty statement](https://help.drivethrupartners.com/hc/en-us/articles/33322793014423-Partner-Shipping-Program-PSP), [Patreon creator fees](https://support.patreon.com/hc/en-gb/articles/11111747095181-Creator-fees-overview).

Do not change storefront solely because its headline fee is lower. Product delivery, receipts, regional availability and required tax/customer handling must be checked for the actual seller setup before launching. No new seller account has been created.

## 7. Suggested starting offer

1. Finish the three rules books as accessible digital publications.
2. Publish one complete short starter adventure at pay-what-you-want, with a visible optional support amount and public mechanical collection.
3. Release one substantially richer paid adventure pack: searchable PDF, player-safe handouts, maps, encounter preparations and collection links. Make its preview show a real playable scene.
4. Keep Buy Me a Coffee. An optional “support development” recurring tier can come later without a promised monthly adventure treadmill.
5. Measure completed purchases, net receipts, use/playtest feedback and production time before making more products or starting print production.

Pricing is not settled. A price experiment should follow the actual scope, quality, production cost and audience feedback. Downloads are not the same as paying customers. Contribution per sale is the receipt after transaction fees/refunds/taxes and direct fulfillment costs; break-even also needs the project's production cost.

## 8. Website changes after you choose

- A compact **Books & adventures** area: free rules, a playable starter and clearly priced products.
- A **Support SwordWeave** page describing what contributions fund; preserve the FAB support action.
- Each product shows sample pages, contents, delivery format, linked mechanical collection, rights notice and a purchase link to the selected storefront.
- Rules and character builders remain easy to use without purchase prompts interrupting play.
- Paid story/map files use controlled delivery. Public mechanics keep their normal library availability.

We can start with external checkout and delivery. Native purchases, account entitlements and a creator marketplace are separate later projects.
