## Your first scene, step by step

You can learn SwordWeave by playing one small situation. This chapter follows three players through a rescue, including their questions, a useful roll, an unsuccessful approach, and the record they carry forward. Read it as a conversation you can imitate. Its DCs and consequences belong to this example; they are not a universal rescue system.

:::art bridge

### The situation before the dice

The party reaches a mill above a canal. A worker is trapped on a balcony. A wooden support has cracked. There is a trough beside the road, a coil of rope, and a narrow maintenance stair. The balcony is 20 ft from the trough and 10 ft above the lower landing. Nobody is attacking the party. The urgency comes from the moving water and weakening building.

The DM gives those visible facts without requiring a check. The players know enough to make choices. They can ask about anything that would be evident to someone standing there: the rope's condition, the nearest window, the depth of the water, or whether the worker can move.

Mira, the water courier, can reshape existing water. Torren, a practical gatekeeper, has strong Physical approaches and training. Ilex, a records keeper, reads structures and speaks calmly to frightened people. Their complete builds appear in the next workshop. The scene is not designed to require all three special abilities. They can succeed through tools, cooperation, or a different plan.

> **At the table**
>
> DM: “The support shifts. The worker is holding the balcony rail. What do you do?”
>
> Torren: “Is the stair still connected to the balcony?”
>
> DM: “Yes. The lower half is intact. The upper section leans away from the wall.”
>
> Ilex: “Can the worker hear us?”
>
> DM: “If you speak loudly, yes.”
>
> Mira: “I want to support the upper stair with water while Torren brings them down.”

Each question has changed the plan's reliability. None has spent an action merely because someone spoke. If speaking would itself create a risk in another scene, establish that risk there. Here the conversation supports a shared intent.

### Identify the extraordinary part

Mira describes shaping the trough's existing water into a support. Water domain access identifies the subject; Verb II supports changing its form. Near permits the 20-ft reach. She is not creating water from nothing, permanently turning it into stone, or giving the worker a new sense.

The DM asks how the proposed support works. Mira wants a narrow moving channel to take some of the stair's weight while Torren braces it. That is more specific than “I fix the mill.” It also leaves the ordinary rope, strong landing, and worker's cooperation relevant.

The table agrees that the water can assist a short rescue under these circumstances. The support is temporary: Mira must keep directing it. They choose a Magical check to establish it before another shift. Failure means the water arrives too late to brace the upper stair, which then becomes unusable; the worker still has the balcony rail and the rope remains an alternative. The table has not agreed automatic death on a failed setup roll.

Mira can accept the attempt, narrow it further, or take the rope route instead. She accepts. Her combined Magical bonus is +6. A d20 result of 7 gives 13 against the agreed DC 12. The narrow support forms. Her acquired d8 output is not rolled: this construction is neither an attack nor a healing effect.

### Let support change the next question

Torren moves to the landing. He does not need a second roll to notice the support Mira just created. He wants to climb the stairs and bring the worker down. The remaining uncertainty is whether he can keep the warped handrail and the worker steady while the water shakes the stairs.

The DM first asks what Torren uses. He anchors the rope on the intact lower post and gives the worker its free end. That preparation changes the possible failure. A slip can now leave them hanging from an anchored rope rather than falling immediately into the canal. Agree that change before the roll, not as a reward invented afterward.

The table selects Prowess for controlling the weight of the worker and rail. Torren has +7: Physical 5 and PB 2. The agreed DC is 13. He rolls 4, totaling 11. He does not bring the worker safely down yet. The upper rail breaks. They remain supported by the rope and Mira's channel, but the stair route has changed.

Torren's player has options. He can hold position while Ilex directs the worker toward the window. He can propose greater exertion with a consequence. He can release the damaged rail and trust the rope. The failed roll has produced a new decision, rather than asking him to roll the same unchanged check until it works.

### Propose a narrower success

Torren asks to pull the worker onto the landing immediately. The DM offers a bounded consequence: he can do it, but his shoulder will be strained, imposing the agreed restriction that he cannot carry another person until treated and rested. This is an example ruling, not a fixed injury penalty attached to every Prowess failure.

Torren asks whether bracing the rope against the post would reduce that cost. The DM agrees, but someone must guide the worker so the rope does not cut across a broken edge. Ilex can do that from the safe bank. Ilex has not acquired a “rescue instruction” primitive; ordinary communication is available.

The group chooses the supported version. Ilex explains the movement clearly, Torren draws the worker in, and Mira keeps the water directed. The remaining situation is secure enough that the DM does not require another roll. The worker reaches the landing. Torren has accepted a lesser consequence: aching hands, requiring clean dressings before precise rope work becomes comfortable again. They record its actual meaning together.

### Record what survived the scene

The party's record now includes a rescued worker, a damaged stair, a rope that needs inspection, and Torren's agreed hand consequence. Mira releases the water support; that maintenance restriction ends. Her future Water access remains hers. A temporary support disappearing does not erase the acquisition that permitted it.

The worker promises to introduce them to the lock keeper. This is a relationship created in play. It is not automatic BU, a purchased capability, or a mandatory reward. The table can separately agree an advancement award if the rescue completes an important campaign objective.

Before proceeding, the players check current Vitality and any actually paid costs. This scene's consequences were restrictive and environmental; nobody invents a damage bill after the rescue. Torren's hands remain affected until the agreed care occurs. A new scene does not clear the record automatically.

### Learn from a different approach

The same scene can play differently. Ilex might ask the worker to crawl through the window while Torren holds a ladder. Mira might divert the pressure below the mill instead of supporting the stair. The party could evacuate the lower landing and wait for specialists if it is safe to do so.

Those choices use the same core loop: establish the result, check what supports it, set the scope and stakes, resolve meaningful uncertainty, and record the changed situation. The DM does not need to protect the water solution. Players do not need to imitate this conversation word for word.

**Try it with your group.** Change one fact: the worker cannot hear; the trough is empty; the balcony is 50 ft away; or the rope anchor is unstable. Ask which part of the plan changes. A missing water supply matters to a permission for reshaping existing water. Greater distance matters to Near. Neither is solved simply by declaring more damage dice.

## Three people, three starting builds

These workshops show how a concept becomes a readable record. They use the default 25-BU level 1 foundation, a total of 10 base attributes, PB 2, and reference acquisition prices. Their unusual capabilities are explicitly authored examples. Review them with your own table before adopting them.

They are not classes. Nothing locks a gatekeeper into armor, a courier into water, or a records keeper into a career. Their purchases can develop in different directions. Keep the underlying ingredients when you reorganize a heritage; a title is not a second ledger.

:::art workshop

### Torren, the gatekeeper

**Person first.** Torren spent years keeping flood barriers working. He is physically strong, good at noticing what will fail, and reluctant to abandon someone behind a closed gate. His immediate goal is to deliver a warning to the next settlement. His connection to Mira is practical: her river knowledge helps him understand the warning's cause.

His base attributes are Physical 5, Mental 4, Magical 1. Physical is proficient. At level 1 that gives PB 2 to its three practices and its saving throw. Mental and Magical remain useful without training. He has not traded ordinary thinking for the right to lift things.

| Acquisition | Bound rule | BU |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| Verb I | Direct physical/perceptual interactions and simple force within the owned subject | 4 |
| Metal domain I | Grounded metal is an eligible subject; no control of minds, all matter, or every Tier I domain | 4 |
| d8 output | Permit d8 output for an appropriate authored damage/healing expression | 4 |
| Awareness proficiency | Add PB to relevant Awareness checks; does not train every Mental practice | 4 |
| Steady Hands | Add +2 to Finesse checks to thread or tie a rope while both hands are free | 4 |
| Defensive Save Upgrade: Mental | Grant PB training to the Mental saving throw | 4 |
| Total | Six acquisitions; no mirror credit | 24 |

The last two 4-BU entries are table-reviewed choices. Steady Hands is the authored example in the reference, not a universal price for every +2. The save-training grant applies to the saving throw, not to Reason, Knowledge, or Influence.

**Derived record.** Maximum Vitality 12; short-rest allowance 6; Physical-selected DC 12. Prowess, Finesse, and Fieldcraft are +7. Awareness is +6. Reason, Knowledge, and Influence are +4. Mysticism, Communion, and Intuition are +1. Physical save +7; Mental save +6; Magical save +1. When Steady Hands qualifies, the relevant Finesse check becomes +9; the ordinary Finesse line stays +7.

**Gate Crew Upbringing** organizes Awareness proficiency and Steady Hands: 8 BU already counted. **Iron Hand Manifest** organizes Verb I, Metal I, and d8: 12 BU already counted. Mental save training remains another acquisition. Torren can describe a lineage without assigning it mechanics; the absence of a purchased lineage does not leave his character incomplete.

**Iron Knock, authored capability.** At Touch, use eligible direct force through existing metal to strike a located target. Example expression: one attack using Physical training, +7, against the target's DC; on a hit, 1d8 Physical Metal damage. This direct expression does not reshape an entire gate, create metal, gain reach, or automatically disarm the recipient. The table appraises unusual output or extra recipients separately. It has no ongoing upkeep in this example.

**Working gear.** A Medium two-handed canal hook has Item BU 0, Load 2, and two equipped slots. A Medium rope bundle has Item BU 0, Load 2, and one slot when actively equipped. If the table treats a worn working coat as an equipped item, it has Item BU 0, Load 2, and one slot. These are three explicit ordinary item records, not grants of armor or damage reduction. Total Load 6; equipped use four of six slots. Capacity is 40 + 5 × Physical 5 = 65. Tools have setting availability or prices separately from Item BU.

**Three test uses.** Iron Knock can strike a metal obstacle at contact under its actual rule. It cannot reach a guard 30 ft away using Touch alone. It does not quietly become Verb II reshaping when Torren wants to turn a lock into a key. The party can still use ordinary tools or ask for a bounded exception.

### Mira, the river courier

Mira's full record appears in Worked records. Here the important construction decision is restraint. Her concept suggests many abilities, but she begins with a small reusable vocabulary: Verb II, Water I, Near, d8, and Knowledge proficiency. She spends 24 BU and leaves one unspent.

Her attributes are Physical 3, Mental 3, Magical 4, with Magical proficient. Maximum Vitality 12; short-rest allowance 6; Magical-selected DC 11. Her Physical practices are +3. Mental practices are +3 except trained Knowledge +5. Magical practices are +6. Saves are Physical +3, Mental +3, Magical +6.

She arranges Water, Verb II, and Near into Guided Water, then River Hand. She can also define **Canal Impact**, a separate authored example using those same acquisitions plus d8: drive existing located water along an actual projected path up to 30 ft, attack +6 against DC, and deal 1d8 Magical Water damage on a hit. A projected path is affected by its actual obstruction; direct manifestation requires a separately supported delivery arrangement.

Both capabilities reuse the same acquisitions: River Hand uses Water, Verb II and Near, while Canal Impact also uses d8. Her ledger stays 24. She does not gain two Water purchases or two simultaneous outputs because Water appears in two capability descriptions. Whether she can maintain one channel while launching another working depends on their agreed requirements and actual scope; there is no universal one-concentration limit.

**Choice she postpones.** Mira wants to restore a drowned person's breath. d8 output is not a healing permission. Grounded Water I does not automatically manipulate life or reverse death. She can rescue through ordinary aid, acquire a reviewed relevant construction later, or ask the table for a clearly scoped exceptional possibility. A sympathetic title such as “River's Mercy” supplies no missing behavior.

**Her useful weakness.** Dry surroundings matter because her present workings reshape existing water. This is a consequence of their recorded scope, not a mirrored drawback that automatically refunds BU. She carries a flask and asks where water is available. A companion who notices resources can be more useful than another die type.

### Ilex, the records keeper

**Person first.** Ilex restores damaged maps and mediates disputes about old boundaries. They want to discover why two villages possess incompatible copies of the same canal charter. They are curious, careful, and willing to put themselves between angry people. Their unusual skill helps read physical documents; it does not decide whose rights are just.

Attributes: Physical 2, Mental 5, Magical 3. Mental proficient. Their foundation has 25 BU. They acquire a narrow grounded subject instead of an abstract information domain: Paper I concerns existing paper as material. Reading secret memories or rewriting belief would need a different subject and behavior.

| Acquisition | Bound rule | BU |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| Verb II | Reshape, redirect, combine, separate, transfer, or stabilize eligible existing states | 8 |
| Paper domain I | Grounded existing paper is an eligible subject; no control of all recorded knowledge | 4 |
| Mysticism proficiency | Add PB to Mysticism checks | 4 |
| Fieldcraft proficiency | Add PB to Fieldcraft checks | 4 |
| Steady Hands | Add +2 to Finesse checks to thread or tie a rope while both hands are free | 4 |
| Total | No mirror credit; 1 BU unspent | 24 |

Maximum Vitality 12; allowance 6; Mental-selected DC 12. Prowess and ordinary Finesse are +2; Fieldcraft +4. Mental practices are all +7. Mysticism +5; Communion and Intuition +3. Physical save +2; Mental save +7; Magical save +3. Mysticism proficiency does not train the Magical saving throw.

**Archive Upbringing** groups Paper I, Verb II, and Mysticism proficiency: 16 existing BU. Fieldcraft and Steady Hands are individual acquisitions. Their lineage is descriptive. They can later build a Manifest for a new role without losing their archive training.

**Dry the Record, authored capability.** At Touch, reshape or stabilize existing paper so a soaked sheet can be handled without immediately tearing. State what material is present and which damage is reversible in the scene. The capability does not reconstruct words that no longer exist, authenticate a lie, reveal invisible ink through walls, or grant Near reach. Time, tools, and uncertainty remain relevant. The free d4 baseline is available for suitable output constructions but is not needed for this task.

**Play connection.** Ilex can notice repeated marks through Awareness, compare accounts through Reason, recall a charter through Knowledge, or negotiate through Influence. All four have +7 from Mental proficiency, but they answer different questions. A good comparison may make a conclusion secure with no roll. A persuasive argument cannot force a frightened villager to adopt a belief against all evidence.

### Check the three ledgers together

Each character has 24 positive acquired BU, 1 ordinary BU left, no mirror credit, and separately recorded equipment. The party's default progression reference is 3 × 25 = **75 Party BU**, not 72 because three points are unspent. Spent acquisitions describe builds; Party BU represents their progression budgets for encounter comparison. Add actual resolved Item BU separately.

Torren and Mira can deal d8 output through their recorded expressions. Ilex can be useful without an extraordinary damage capability. Ordinary actions, resources, and relevant senses remain available to everyone. These records demonstrate a varied party rather than a mandatory frontline/healer/damage roster.

Before using them, ask each player to name one thing they can do, one limit, one relevant combined bonus, and one reason to cooperate. If someone cannot explain a capability, simplify its prose. A longer list is not automatically a better character.

### Adapt a workshop without losing its accounting

Start with the desired change. Swapping Water for grounded Air changes Mira's subject, so rewrite the effect and tests; do not merely change the icon. Replacing Knowledge proficiency with a different 4-BU practice grant changes the affected checks while leaving the acquisition total unchanged. Replacing a 4-BU grant with a proposed 12-BU attribute increment changes the ledger and possibly the derived sheet.

A fork is a new authored definition. You can keep references to unchanged ingredients, record what you changed, and agree which version belongs to the character. Existing ownership does not imply a free unrelated upgrade simply because the new creation descended from an old one. Discuss replacement or retraining with the table; SwordWeave does not prescribe a universal fee.

## A construction workshop

Use this chapter when an idea does not fit a ready-made entry. You can begin with a sentence and turn it into a primitive, effect, capability, or heritage. Every layer should make the result easier to understand. You do not need a nested container merely to make the card look complete.

### Choose the smallest useful record

“Add +2 to Finesse checks to thread or tie a rope while both hands are free” is one primitive. Its amount, recipient, check, and condition are clear. It does not need an effect and capability to become valid.

“Guide existing water around an obstacle within 30 ft” uses several acquired ingredients. An effect can gather its recurring arrangement. A capability can explain how the character applies it. A heritage can connect those permissions to the character's history. These layers organize the same purchases; they do not invent additional power.

Use a direct primitive when one readable grant is enough. Use an effect when a group of ingredients is repeatedly useful together. Use a capability when the table needs a complete working, trigger, or use. Use a heritage when the character's ancestry, experience, or current expression gives those mechanics context.

### Separate five kinds of statement

| Statement | What it establishes | What it does not establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “Water is an eligible subject” | Bound domain permission | Every grounded domain; healing; automatic success |
| “Reshape existing states” | A kind of change within owned subject access | Infinite quantities; every causal rewrite |
| “Up to 30 ft” | Maximum permitted reach | Sight; passage through barriers; target knowledge |
| “Use d8 output” | Eligible output die type | A compulsory attack; dice count; extra recipients |
| “While singing” | A condition or continuation requirement | Automatic action economy; immunity to silence |

When a sentence mixes several categories, split it into readable clauses. “Sing to all allies, reveal hidden enemies, and restore everyone” might combine targeting, information, healing, recipients, and persistence. Review each contribution, rather than assigning one broad word a package of powers.

### Write a useful condition

A condition should be something the table can identify. “While blessed” requires a definition of blessed. “While touching the marked stone” provides a clear event, but you must still decide whether a glove counts and what happens if contact ends during execution.

Choose conditions because they express the concept and create understandable decisions. A fragile condition that nobody can verify becomes an argument. A condition that is always true contributes no practical restriction. Neither automatically establishes a fixed discount: price is an authored proposal.

**Workshop example.** A player writes “Gain +2 Awareness near water.” The table asks what near means. They choose within 10 ft of an open body of water large enough to immerse a hand, while conscious and able to perceive it. The bonus applies to Awareness checks concerning changes in that water, not every Awareness check made beside a puddle. That is a narrower authored example; its price must be proposed and accepted separately.

The revised rule is easier to use. It also makes the player's desired scene clear. If they intended every observation beside water, they should say so and review that broader scope.

### Choose an operation at the right level

An attribute bonus can affect its practices, trained save, selected DC, and other formulas that actually use the attribute. A bonus to one check affects that check only. A result floor constrains a result at its stated stage; it does not replace every contributing attribute. A grant establishes permission or training; repeating the same training grant does not stack PB endlessly.

Ask the author to show a before-and-after example. For Ilex, adding +1 Mental changes Mental from 5 to 6 if that grant is accepted within the actual limits. Their Mental-trained check total would become 8 before further modifiers. Adding +1 only to Knowledge leaves Reason, Awareness, Influence, and the selected DC unchanged. These are mechanically different proposals even if both have “clear thinking” in their names.

The app may support a precise numerical operation. Use it when it faithfully expresses the rule. If a rule is contextual, readable prose remains authoritative for the table. Automation should not fabricate an unconditional number for a conditional benefit.

### Make stacking legible

The default is that applicable contributions stack unless their actual rules say otherwise. First check whether each source qualifies. Then check uniqueness, acquisition identity, and any authored cap. A primitive referenced twice in the same composition is not necessarily two acquired occurrences.

For probability bias, cancel opposing stacks and roll one extra resolution die per remaining stack. For static bonuses, apply the qualifying numerical contributions at their actual scope. For training, multiple grants of the same proficiency establish the same trained state rather than repeatedly adding PB. Those three behaviors should not be described with one vague “stacks” counter.

**Example.** Torren has trained Finesse +7. Steady Hands supplies +2 during qualifying rope work, giving +9. A second effect referencing the very same acquired Steady Hands does not add another +2. If he deliberately acquires an additional permitted occurrence, the table checks its actual stacking/uniqueness rule before applying it. A separate source of advantage changes the dice selected, not the +9 static bonus.

### Test a trigger without inventing an extra action

“When an ally falls” defines an event. It does not, by itself, grant an unlimited free response. Write how the response interacts with the shared rhythm, available Reaction Slot, immediate scope, senses, and actual distance.

**Example authored wording.** “When a conscious ally within ordinary reach begins to fall, you may spend an available independent Reaction Slot to catch them with a free hand. Resolve the actual interference or weight through the table's chosen procedure.” This is a readable immediate response. It gives no remote rescue, extra slot, or automatic success.

An author who wants an additional reaction must state that economy change separately. An author who wants a distant catch needs relevant reach and behavior. A powerful story image can be supported by a clear construction; it does not need to hide those decisions.

### Give a transformation boundaries

“Become a wolf” leaves important questions unanswered. Which size, speed, senses, defenses, and capabilities change? What remains from the character? Is gear carried, merged, dropped, or unchanged? What ends the form? Which actual acquisitions supply the changes?

Write the form's readable record. If it is only a cosmetic change, say so. If it grants a movement mode or sense, identify its scope. Do not silently copy every rule from a creature with a similar appearance. A small photograph or portrait cannot define a build.

For a beginner, a narrow form can be easier: fur, posture, and voice change; existing attributes and equipment remain; no new attacks or senses are granted. The player can add reviewed mechanics later. This gives them a playable starting point without pretending the full fantasy is already encoded.

### Turn the construction into a heritage

A **Lineage** can express inherited or bodily features. An **Upbringing** can express learned skills, customs, and work. A **Manifest** can express a present vocation, awakened gift, or self-defined role. These are organizing meanings, not rules that every benefit must fit exactly one biological category.

Suppose Ilex develops **Keepers of the Fold**, an upbringing of map restorers. It contains their existing Paper I, Verb II, and Mysticism proficiency. Its description explains careful copying and why they mistrust a clean map without a date. The upbringing costs 16 of the already recorded BU. It grants no automatic language list, legal authority, or new sense because its members keep archives.

Another character can adapt it with Knowledge training or a different subject. Name the changed ingredients and ledger. Stories connect the mechanics to a person; the mechanics explain what the story lets that person actually do.

### The three-use review

Test a **normal use**, a **stretched use**, and an **outside-scope use**. A normal use demonstrates the working at its intended scale. A stretched use checks how the table will negotiate effort, recipients, time, and consequences. An outside-scope use reveals missing access rather than pricing it as more of the same.

For River Hand, pour a small stream from a nearby trough, redirect a flood across a street, then restore a drowned person's life. The first may be secure. The second raises serious scope and consequences. The third introduces a different behavior; more water or more d8s does not establish it.

Record ambiguities revealed by these tests. If a player and DM interpret the same clause differently, settle it before a tense scene. You do not need a universal answer for every possible future use. You need an intelligible rule and a reliable conversation for the next one.

## Adventures beyond a fight

SwordWeave's construction tools should expand your choices in conversation, investigation, travel, and work. A character who cannot deal spectacular damage can still carry an adventure. Start by asking what the situation needs and what your character can contribute.

### Investigate through evidence

Distinguish what you can see from what you infer. A broken seal is a visible fact if you can inspect it. Who broke it can be uncertain. A check should answer a specific question: find a trace before it washes away, recognize a tool mark, or connect two accounts under time pressure.

Ask where the information might come from. People, documents, materials, and granted senses can supply different evidence. A magical impression can establish something its rule actually detects; it does not automatically become a complete account of the past.

Share discoveries with the party. A useful clue may let someone else act. If you fail one approach, try a different source or revise the question. Do not assume every failed check means the answer is forever unavailable, and do not assume repeating the same words gives endless safe attempts.

### Negotiate with a person

Listen for what the other participant wants and can afford. Offer something relevant. A Watcher protecting a spawning bed may accept a flow plan that preserves it. A worker who needs immediate safety may care little about an elegant explanation of water mechanics.

Your Influence bonus helps resolve uncertainty in an actual approach. It does not replace the offer, erase another person's motives, or grant supernatural control. If a capability changes agency, read its exceptional behavior and agreed boundaries explicitly.

A successful negotiation can be a limited agreement. “We will open this route for one crossing if you repair the bank” is different from permanent allegiance. Record the terms. This avoids treating every good roll as a universal relationship upgrade.

### Travel through a changing place

Ask what route offers, what it risks, and what the group knows. Distance, terrain, time, weather, and supplies can matter. Your table may use a travel procedure, but its ration costs, intervals, and weather rolls are campaign choices rather than hidden core rules.

Contribute through an approach: scout, read a map, negotiate passage, maintain equipment, or protect a route with a suitable working. Extraordinary mobility changes some obstacles while leaving others. Flight does not automatically find a hidden destination, carry the whole party, or protect companions from weather.

Tell the DM which uncertainty you want to engage with. A player interested in exploration may enjoy route choices. A group trying to reach the next scene quickly can summarize secure travel. The same rules support both when the table's intent is clear.

### Work with ordinary tools

A ladder, lever, rope, cart, or reliable witness can solve a problem without BU. Their availability, material, strength, and use still matter. Ordinary permission is not a guarantee that a tool accomplishes an impossible task.

Torren can brace a gate with a hook. Ilex can copy a map by hand. Mira can carry water in a bucket. Their extraordinary acquisitions add possibilities; they do not revoke everyday competence. A saved capability name is not required before a player can propose an ordinary action.

**Example.** To move a heavy crate, the party uses rollers and a rope rather than buying telekinesis. The DM may ask for a check if the floor is unstable or time is short. If there is ample time and no meaningful uncertainty, they move it. The rolls serve the actual question, not a tax on creativity.

### Share the spotlight deliberately

After explaining your approach, look for a place another player can contribute. A water channel needs a guide; a negotiation needs credible terms; a map needs someone who recognizes the terrain. Do not invent a compulsory team bonus. Let actual help change access, circumstances, stakes, or the chosen resolution when it makes sense.

You can also choose to step aside. Another character's specialist scene does not need your competing check. Ask a useful question, carry a consequence, or make their route safer. Your contribution can change the fiction without appearing as a large number on the sheet.

## Consequences you can understand

A consequence is a change people can apply. Its name is shorthand for a rule or situation, not an imported package. This chapter contrasts several agreed records and follows them through recovery. These examples have no universal BU price or mandatory Strain grade.

:::art camp

### Record a mechanical consequence

**Example: trembling hands.** After forcing a difficult mechanism, Torren accepts −2 to Finesse checks requiring delicate hand work until he receives care and completes an agreed short rest. The record identifies the recipient, exact check scope, amount, and ending condition.

The penalty does not reduce Physical, Prowess, movement, or every attack unless the actual agreement says so. Steady Hands can still qualify for rope work; its +2 and the consequence's −2 cancel numerically on a check to which both apply. That arithmetic does not mean the injury has ended. Keep the consequence until its recovery condition is met.

If the DM wants an injury that prevents the activity entirely, use an explicit restriction instead. A numerical penalty and a prohibition are different rules. Do not discover the prohibition only after adding the dice.

### Record a restriction

**Example: keep the song going.** A maintained passage remains open while Ilex sings a continuous phrase and can hear its echo. They cannot speak another sentence without breaking that requirement. The table agrees the continuation point and the consequence of stopping: the passage closes safely at the next stated instant, leaving occupants on their current side.

This is a specific authored example, not a rule that every sung working has the same closure timing. Losing the echo can disrupt it because the requirement says so. Taking a wound is not universally enough. If Ilex is incapacitated and cannot sing, they cannot fulfill the task.

Write what others can observe. Companions can notice the song faltering and plan around the closing passage. The requirement becomes a playable part of the scene rather than a private surprise.

### Record lost access

**Example: exhausted current.** Mira accepts that her Water reshaping access will be unavailable after an unusually demanding diversion until she spends quiet time at a natural moving stream, as agreed before the attempt. The consequence identifies which permission is blocked and what restores it.

Her acquired primitive remains in her ledger. She does not lose its ownership or receive automatic BU back. Other acquisitions and ordinary actions remain available within their own scope. A short or long rest clears the restriction only if the agreed ending actually includes that rest.

This type of consequence can be exciting when the player accepts it knowingly and still has useful choices. It can be frustrating when broad access loss is imposed without discussion. Ask what duration and recovery make the cost intelligible before accepting it.

### Record an environmental change

**Example: the diverted storehouse.** A successful flood diversion protects the workers but sends water through a storage building. The consequence is damage to the building and supplies, plus the people affected by that damage. It is not necessarily a penalty on Mira's check bonus.

Healing her Vitality does not dry the storehouse. A rest does not erase responsibility. The party can help repair it, make restitution, dispute who authorized the diversion, or deal with the lost supplies. Those are future choices created by the result.

An environmental consequence should be visible enough to understand. Before commitment, the DM points out the available outlet and what lies beyond it. A player can choose another route or accept the cost. Hidden facts may still exist, but do not use them to conceal every meaningful stake.

### Record a narrative obligation

**Example: a borrowed key.** The lock keeper loans a restricted key in exchange for returning it before the evening inspection and helping them explain the unauthorized opening. The obligation has people, timing, and possible responses. It grants no automatic negative number on the sheet.

If the party keeps the key, the situation changes: trust is damaged, the keeper needs an explanation, and access may close. The DM portrays those results from the actual motives and facts. A consequence can be serious without requiring an imported condition called “wanted.”

Such obligations are not automatic mirrored drawbacks. Mirror credit concerns eligible reversed acquisitions with accepted scope. An agreement made in a scene is not a universal source of free character budget.

### Several consequences can coexist

Mira may maintain a channel, carry a Water restriction that begins after releasing it, and owe repairs for a flooded storehouse. Track each cause and ending separately. Ending the channel releases its active-maintenance requirement; it does not silently end the later restriction or the repair obligation.

For several maintained capabilities, requirements accumulate. One capability containing several effects has one upkeep track. Do not count every nested effect as a separate ongoing bill unless the actual construction makes them independent capabilities or explicitly establishes another requirement.

At Council, ask which agreed requirements recur now. In exploration, use their actual intervals or events. Upkeep may be material, restrictive, numerical, or narrative. It need not be damage, and it need not be identical every time if its agreed terms depend on the situation.

### Use your remaining recovery

Maximum Vitality 13 gives an ordinary short-rest allowance of ceil(13/2) = 7. At 10/13, restore only the missing 3, leaving 4. Later, at 9/13, the remaining 4 can bring you to full. A third short rest cannot restore more from this exhausted pool unless an agreed rule or adjustment provides it. Being able to pause does not refill the allowance.

An agreed completed long rest normally heals to maximum and refreshes the pool to 7. Its healing is not charged against the short-rest allowance. Overnight recovery can be partial by agreement, but time alone does not apply it. Keep the actual restoration and the refresh decision explicit.

Short-rest healing is not permission to revive someone still actively dying without rescue. Stabilize through the chosen procedure first. Stabilization stops the countdown without granting consciousness or Vitality. Healing above zero normally wakes them unless another consequence prevents it.

### Ask to revise an unclear record

If a consequence has no ending, ask what was intended. If its scope is too broad to apply consistently, propose readable terms. The purpose is to make the continuing situation playable, not to win an argument by exploiting a missing word.

Revisit an agreement between scenes when possible. A record can change with the table's consent. Do not silently erase a drawback because the session ended, and do not expand a narrow cost after someone already accepted it.

## Keep your character growing

Growth is both new mechanics and a changing person. BU rewards are agreed by the table; there is no compulsory per-session schedule. A friendship, discovery, responsibility, or reputation can change a character even before a ledger changes.

### Spend a reward with a purpose

Ask which new ability supports the experiences you want next. A courier who keeps finding hidden routes might want a sense, different reach, or improved training. A gatekeeper who becomes a mediator might develop Mental approaches. A records keeper might build a mobile workshop rather than a stronger attack.

Read the proposed ingredient, its cost, conditions, and dependencies. Check whether it duplicates a purchase you already own, grants a new behavior, or simply reorganizes existing acquisitions. Record the actual new acquisition once.

Do not erase advancement by spending its purchase budget. Progression totals and the unspent ledger answer different questions. When starting above level 1 or crossing a threshold, use the literal cumulative table and current formulas rather than reconstructing it from memory.

### Rework a character transparently

You can discover that a construction is difficult to use or no longer fits the character. Discuss changing it with the DM. SwordWeave does not impose a universal retraining interval, fee, or free replacement rule. Your table can agree how to preserve the story and accounting.

Make a copy of the old record, identify replaced acquisitions, and resolve the resulting sheet. If the character uses a shared or published version, keep the version change intelligible. A private edit does not rewrite every other person's adopted character.

Current Vitality, consequences, and ongoing workings are gameplay state. A build change is not automatic healing or cancellation. Agree any legitimate state adjustment when the new maximum or access changes.

### Use mirrors as real drawbacks

A mirrored acquisition must be eligible, understandable, and actually applied. The debt ceiling limits how much accepted drawback credit can be used; it does not create that credit automatically. Negative prose must have a real subject and ending or scope.

If you reverse a personal flight grant into lost flight, granting an ally flight elsewhere does not remove your own drawback. If a rule is conditional, the reversed rule follows its actual eligible scope. Set To does not have an automatic opposite. Your table can reject a reversal that is not coherent even when the UI can display a negative number.

Ask what decisions the drawback will create. A clearly bounded drawback can enrich the character. A loophole designed never to apply can leave the ledger misleading. Record positive spent, available credit, actual debt used, and ordinary unspent BU separately.

### Arrive at the next session ready

Review current and maximum Vitality, remaining short-rest recovery, consequences and their endings, ongoing capabilities and continuation points, gear, and any purchases made since the last session. Keep the last meaningful position or goal in your notes.

Ask what the party wants to pursue. A short recap of unresolved choices often helps more than a complete transcript. If you cannot attend, agree what happens to your character and who, if anyone, may operate their sheet. Viewing a shared digital record does not itself give editing permission.

## Questions players often ask

### Do I need a class or a complete heritage?

No. Start with a concept and actual acquired permissions. Lineage, Upbringing, and Manifest organize content. A descriptive identity is valid without a compulsory mechanical package. The foundation still needs its required owned positive verb and domain access; read those actual purchases.

### Can I invent a working during play?

Yes, when your owned ingredients and the ordinary circumstances support it. Explain the result, scope, targeting, timing, and consequences. A new arrangement is not automatically a new purchase. A genuinely missing behavior or ingredient needs acquisition, another source, or an explicitly agreed exception.

### Must I roll for using a capability?

No. Roll for meaningful uncertainty using the chosen procedure. A safe, secure small task may resolve directly. An attack expression may use an attack; a resisted expression may use a save. They are not both mandatory merely because the record is called a capability.

### Is every consequence damage?

No. Damage, restrictions, resources, environmental changes, obligations, and loss of access can all matter. Write the actual change and ending. Strain grades guide appraisal; they are not a universal Vitality invoice.

### Does more BU mean more actions?

No. Budget does not grant extra Main Intents or reactions. Additional economy must be explicitly authored and agreed. “Boss,” “master,” “legendary,” or “advanced” is a descriptive title until its actual rule supplies an opportunity.

### Can I affect someone I cannot see?

Locate a specific target through an applicable sense, which can be something other than ordinary sight. Otherwise, propose a known area or spot and accept uncertainty about who is there. Authored alternative methods can establish exceptions. Reach alone does not provide a targeting lock.

### Does a long rest solve every problem?

No. The table agrees whether it earns its recovery effects. Vitality recovery and the short-rest pool follow their agreed procedure. Other consequences use their own ending conditions. Sleep does not automatically repair a map, satisfy a debt, or restore access withheld until a specific task.

### Can we change a default?

Yes, by clear table agreement. Label the variation and explain its scope. Keep shared expectations consistent. Do not surprise a player with a new rule after they committed under the old one. When publishing a compatible variant, identify the changes rather than presenting them as unchanged core rules.
