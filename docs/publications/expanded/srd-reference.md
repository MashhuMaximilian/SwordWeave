## Reference conventions and rule boundaries

This extended reference supplies readable record formats, explicit calculations and procedures for compatible SwordWeave creation. It supplements the principal primitive families and play rules in this SRD. It does not require a platform account, a particular database, a fixed class list or a separate monster book. Keep the actual definitions needed for play with the record; a name, image or unavailable link cannot substitute for an ingredient's rule.

**Core rule.** The table establishes an intended result, checks ordinary circumstances and extraordinary access, agrees the actual scope and stakes, chooses the appropriate resolution, then carries the result forward. Permission to propose an outcome and success at accomplishing it are different facts. Ordinary actions remain possible without purchasing a primitive for every task.

**Default** means the supplied procedure unless the table agrees a variation. **Authored rule** means a particular component's recorded permission, operation, condition, restriction or exception. **Optional guidance** means a tool the group may choose. **Example** means the stated construction or situation, including its example prices. **App note** describes digital organization or calculation; it does not add a tabletop prerequisite.

### Three records to keep distinct

| Record | Question answered | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Definition | What is the rule or construction? | A bound Water permission; a capability's ingredients; an item's equipped requirement |
| Acquisition | Which occurrence does this actor own, and how was it counted? | One Water I acquisition for 4 BU; a separately purchased permitted occurrence |
| State in play | What is true of this actor or scene now? | Current Vitality; movement already used; a maintained channel; an injury |

A new capability can organize existing acquisitions without changing the acquisition ledger. Using that capability can change state without editing its definition. Editing a definition can change the resolved sheet without automatically healing the actor or clearing a consequence. Track the relationship explicitly when several people use the same template.

**Example.** The definition of Guided Water references Verb II, Water I and Near. Mira owns one acquisition of each. She presently maintains one channel beside a stair. Adding another capability that references the same three acquisitions neither buys them again nor creates another channel already in the world. A second actual channel requires a new attempted use and appraisal of its scope and requirements.

### Complete does not mean automated

A complete record lets people decide what qualifies, what changes and what ends. Descriptive rules can be complete without becoming a static sheet number. A narrative obligation may identify a person, deadline and consequence rather than an arithmetic modifier. A sensory permission may describe information and barriers rather than supply an attack bonus.

When a rule is ambiguous, clarify its intended meaning with the author and table. Do not replace missing terms with a familiar package from another game. A condition named stunned, blessed or invisible grants only its stated consequences. A creature labeled boss has only its actual economy. A heritage title grants only its actual content.

### What compatibility requires

A compatible creation should identify which SwordWeave defaults it uses and which rules it changes. It should retain intelligible acquisition accounting, actual permissions and scopes, the distinction between character and item budgets, and the agreed resolution procedure. A campaign may choose different budgets, consequences or optional tools without concealing those changes as unchanged defaults.

A construction's BU figure does not establish approval in every campaign. Its author proposes a price; the table reviews the actual content and can accept or revise it. Suggested catalogue anchors are reference points, not universal tariffs guaranteeing equivalence between every similarly priced rule.

## Readable construction record formats

These formats specify the information people need to use a construction. They are not API payloads or mandatory software field names. Combine fields into readable paragraphs where convenient. Omit genuinely irrelevant details; explicitly settle details that affect the intended use. A paper label such as Water acquisition A can identify a purchase just as well as a digital identifier.

### Primitive definition format

| Detail | What to record |
| --- | --- |
| Name and readable rule | A concise label followed by the actual permission or change |
| Family and bound subject | The relevant construction family and the actual subject, rather than all examples in that family |
| Recipient | Who receives the grant or numerical change: self, equipped bearer, an eligible target or a defined group |
| Operation | Grant, revoke or a stated mathematical operation, where applicable |
| Value or permission | The amount, granted behavior, training, die type or other actual content |
| Conditions | What must be true for the rule to apply |
| Scope | Relevant check, attribute, save, source/domain, working, distance or other boundary |
| Stacking and uniqueness | Whether distinct qualifying acquisitions may combine; any exclusion, cap or unique identity |
| Duration and ending | When a temporary change begins and ends; what restores a restricted permission |
| Acquisition price | The proposed BU price and any actual reviewed mirror-credit terms |
| Provenance and edition | Which definition was adopted, its author/source and any relevant changes |

**Example primitive.** Steady Hands: add +2 to the owner's Finesse checks to thread or tie a rope while both hands are free. It is a numerical practice-check contribution, not a Physical increase. Its recipient is its owner, not everyone using their rope. The example proposes 4 BU. Its authored rule must settle any deliberate additional occurrence before that occurrence is used; two references to the first acquisition are not two qualifying purchases.

### Effect definition format

| Detail | What to record |
| --- | --- |
| Intended outcome | The related result or arrangement the effect organizes |
| Actual ingredients | The definitions and acquisitions supporting that result |
| Contribution of each ingredient | Which permission or change each ingredient supplies |
| Recipient and placement | Who or what is affected and where the arrangement exists |
| Conditions and limits | What qualifies, which behaviors remain unavailable and what bounds the result |
| Interaction with use | Which details are fixed and which are appraised for a particular attempt |

An effect need not introduce a separate execution or upkeep requirement. If it is used inside a maintained capability, the capability's actual continuation governs that use. If the author intends an independently maintained working, identify it as such rather than allowing the nesting diagram to decide.

**Example effect.** Guided Water organizes Verb II, grounded Water I and Near. It supports guiding or reshaping existing located water within 30 ft. The attempted volume, route, opposition and continuation are appraised in use. It does not create water, grant Life access or automatically move every creature standing in the water.

### Capability definition format

| Detail | What to record |
| --- | --- |
| Intent or talent | The usable working, response or continuing ability |
| Ingredients | Actual primitives and any useful effects, with their contributions |
| Normal expression | A readable reference use, if the author supplies one |
| Delivery and targeting | Actual path, target location, selectivity and recipient rules |
| Resolution | A stated procedure, or the question to appraise at the table |
| Timing | Complexity, dependencies, trigger and any explicit economy change |
| Output | Damage, healing, change or information actually established |
| Persistence | Instantaneous, fixed duration, event-ended or actively maintained |
| Continuation | Requirements, due point and consequences of ending when applicable |
| Limits | Exclusions, conditions, stacking and ending terms |

A capability may contain primitives directly. It does not require a separately named effect or one ingredient from every family. A reference expression helps people understand an ordinary use; it should say whether changed scope is appraised or prohibited. Do not present a reference expression as permission for unrelated behaviors.

### Heritage definition format

| Detail | What to record |
| --- | --- |
| Kind and concept | Lineage, Upbringing or Manifest, with its narrative meaning |
| Actual content | Acquisitions and constructions organized by the heritage |
| Acquisition relationship | Which purchases are new, already owned or deliberately additional |
| Rule contributions | Actual grants, modifiers, restrictions or capabilities |
| Drawbacks | Their real scope and any eligible accepted credit |
| Development | Any authored limits or changes; no assumed universal class ladder |

Lineage can organize inherited or bodily features. Upbringing can organize learned practices, customs and work. Manifest can organize a current role, awakened gift or chosen expression. These meanings are useful organization, not compulsory one-of-each packages. A heritage can be descriptive without purchased mechanical benefits.

### Item definition format

| Detail | What to record |
| --- | --- |
| Ordinary identity | Material, size, function and relevant physical facts |
| Actual extraordinary content | Any primitives, effects, capabilities or modifications |
| Activation condition | Whether a rule applies while carried, equipped, braced, used or otherwise qualified |
| Item BU | Equipment-mechanics accounting, separate from character or creature BU |
| Load | Carried contribution, including quantity and any explicit authored change |
| Final equipped slots | One normally, two for two-handed gear, or a higher stated requirement |
| Quantity | How many actual items are carried and equipped |
| Setting value | Ordinary availability or purchase value, if relevant; independent of BU |
| State | Damage, charges or other actual restrictions only where authored |

An ordinary rope or hook can have Item BU zero and still be useful. Zero Item BU does not mean zero Load, automatic availability or indestructibility. A rule attached to a hook does not apply to every check merely because the actor possesses the hook.

### Actor and encounter record formats

An actor record combines a foundation, acquisitions, resolved values and current state. An encounter record organizes several such actors and a situation. Neither record grants new permissions merely by naming them.

| Actor detail | Character treatment | Creature treatment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Foundation | Agreed level and progression budget | Chosen positive base creature budget |
| Attributes | Default base total 10, each −1 to +5 | Nonnegative allocation of the creature foundation's points |
| Training | Chosen proficient attribute and actual grants | Chosen proficient attribute and actual grants |
| Vitality baseline | Level/PB formula | Budget-derived creature baseline or explicit agreed override |
| Acquisitions | Positive purchases, eligible drawbacks and reuse | Creature purchases, eligible credit and reuse |
| Equipment | Separate Item BU, Load and slots | Separate Item BU, Load and slots |
| Current state | Actual Vitality, position, reactions, consequences and continuation | The same kinds of state, independently for each creature |

For an encounter, record name, situation/objective, notes, Party BU, Party Item BU, any known party size, selected creature definitions or versions and quantities. During a run add independent actor state, round/phase, intent and resolution markers, objective changes and meaningful events. Visibility settings and digital ownership are platform properties, not encounter difficulty or character permissions.

## Acquisitions, occurrences and dependency accounting

### Count ownership before counting appearances

An acquisition is one owned occurrence counted in the appropriate ledger. A reference says that a construction uses an acquisition or definition. A nested appearance says where that reference can be found. Those are different relationships. The same acquisition can support several compositions without being charged again or supplying several copies of its numerical contribution to one check.

Identify shared ingredients when reading a bundled heritage or capability. If two capabilities both reference Water acquisition A, their container totals can each display Water's cost as composition information. Adding those displayed totals is not automatically the character's acquisition total. Resolve the actor's ledger against actual owned occurrences.

Deliberately purchasing additional occurrences is a different choice. Record quantity, cost and the actual stacking or uniqueness rules. A second purchase is not automatically useful: a unique training state or nonstacking defense may be unchanged. Owning several rules also does not automatically grant several Main Intents.

### A complete reusable ledger

**Example.** An actor owns these five acquisitions. The bound access definitions below reproduce everything needed for the example; they are not a fixed class package.

| Acquisition label | Actual rule | BU |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| V-A | Verb II: change eligible existing states or properties by reshaping, redirecting, combining, separating, transferring or stabilizing | 8 |
| W-A | Water I: grounded existing water is an eligible subject; no grant of all grounded domains | 4 |
| R-A | Near: eligible reach up to 30 ft with actual targeting and delivery requirements | 4 |
| D-A | d8 output: this die type is available for an appropriate damage/healing construction; no automatic behavior or dice count | 4 |
| K-A | Knowledge proficiency: supply PB training where not already supplied | 4 |
| Total | Five actual acquisitions | 24 |

Guided Water references V-A, W-A and R-A. River Hand uses Guided Water. Canal Impact references those same three acquisitions plus D-A. River Caravan Upbringing organizes V-A, W-A and K-A. All four constructions can be recorded without a second charge for their overlapping ingredients. The actor's positive ledger stays 24 BU.

If the actor uses River Hand to divert water and Canal Impact later to strike, both uses rely on the same ownership. Each actual attempt still has its own timing, scope, resolution and consequences. The names do not create simultaneous free outputs. If a rule instead explicitly consumes a resource on each use, track that authored resource separately from the acquisition.

### Acquisition and active contribution are separate

A condition can make a purchased rule inactive without refunding its price. An unequipped item can remain owned and carried without supplying its equipped-only bonus. An accepted drawback can remain in the ledger while its condition is presently absent; its real eligible scope still matters when reviewing credit.

Likewise, suppressing a rule through a consequence changes availability, not ownership. If Water reshaping is unavailable until a stated task, the character does not silently receive its BU back. A build edit that removes a purchase needs table agreement about replacement, accounting and existing state.

### Ingredient availability and adopted versions

A readable offline record should reproduce the relevant ingredient definitions or include an accessible reference copy. Record the adopted edition when changing versions could change mechanics. A newer published definition does not automatically alter everyone's current character, template or run.

**App note.** Digital compositions can reference pinned definitions and resolve their dependencies through the platform. A visible container does not grant access to another person's private ingredients. Availability and ownership are checked through existing permissions. If a preparation contains an inaccessible reference, treat its unresolved information as unknown rather than displaying private details or pretending its budget is zero.

For paper play, the same principle is practical rather than technical: make the actual approved rules available to the group. A missing page or inaccessible link prevents reproducible appraisal until the definition is supplied or replaced. This is not a requirement to obtain a digital account.

### Quantity belongs to a real occurrence

State what quantity counts. Three creatures are three actors. Three hooks are three physical items. Two permitted numerical acquisitions can supply two contributions if their actual rules allow it. Three references to one hook are not three hooks; three references to one Steady Hands acquisition are not three bonuses.

Multiplying quantity does not automatically multiply every other property. Two creatures have independent Vitality and reactions, not one creature with double its maximum. Carrying two hooks multiplies Load, but only actually equipped items use slots. Repeating a training grant establishes the same trained state unless a separately authored rule specifies a different contribution.

## Numerical operations, grants and scope

### Operation meanings

Choose the quantity before choosing the operation. An operation on an attribute, a practice bonus, a selected die, a final result or maximum Vitality can produce very different outcomes. Read the rule's actual stage and scope.

| Operation | Readable meaning | Scope example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Add | Increase the identified quantity by the stated amount | Add +2 to qualifying Finesse checks |
| Subtract | Decrease it by the stated amount | Subtract 2 from a specified attack bonus |
| Multiply | Multiply it by the stated factor | Multiply covered damage by 2 for vulnerability |
| Divide | Divide it by a valid stated divisor | Halve covered damage, rounded up, for resistance |
| Minimum | Establish a floor for that identified quantity or result | A stated check result cannot be below 10 |
| Maximum | Establish a ceiling for that identified quantity or result | A stated result cannot exceed 18 |
| Set To | Replace the identified quantity with the stated value | An explicitly authored speed replacement |
| Grant | Establish the specified permission, state or training | Grant Knowledge proficiency |
| Revoke | Remove or prohibit the specified permission within scope | Revoke a stated movement mode while qualified |

Ordinary rounding is upward where a calculation needs an integer. A formula that explicitly says floor retains that operation. A rule may establish its own particular exception. Division by zero is not a meaningful numerical rule; choose valid parameters and state their use.

Some operations interact. Adding 2 then multiplying by 2 differs from multiplying by 2 then adding 2. The framework does not turn unspecified order in an ambiguous creation into a new universal tabletop hierarchy. Define the intended quantity and interaction, show a before/after calculation and settle conflicts before relying on them. Preserve explicit special procedures, such as damage-defense cancellation and training upgrades, rather than replacing them with raw arithmetic.

**App note.** A digital resolver uses its supported operation order and metric scope. Authoring a modifier that produces a number in the app does not resolve unclear tabletop prose. Keep the human-readable rule and intended example consistent with its digital representation. If they differ, review the construction rather than treating a storage detail as a new core rule.

### Grants are not always additions

Permission, training, state and bias grants require their stated interpretation. Granting Water access does not add Water's tier number to a roll. Granting flight establishes its actual movement permission; its speed and conditions still need definition. Granting proficiency upgrades applicable training rather than adding PB every time the same word appears.

For a practice, the current full parent attribute is the baseline contribution. Applicable proficiency supplies one PB. Eligible expertise brings training to twice PB. An expertise record should establish or identify the required proficiency; a bare ambiguous label is not a way to purchase repeated PB. Separate explicit numerical bonuses follow their own scope and stacking rules.

**Example.** At PB 2 and Mental 3, untrained Knowledge is +3. A valid proficiency grant makes it +5. A second proficiency grant leaves the training component at +2, so the total remains +5. Eligible expertise gives a +4 training component and a +7 total. An independently qualifying explicit +1 Knowledge bonus brings that check to +8; it does not become another proficiency state.

Saving-throw training is separate from practice training. The chosen proficient attribute supplies PB to its own practices and saving throw. Knowledge proficiency does not by itself train the Mental save. A Defensive Save Upgrade can grant training to its stated saving-throw scope without increasing Reason or Influence.

### Recipient and metric matrix

| Rule | What can change | What remains unchanged unless separately affected |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Add +1 current Physical | Physical-based practices/saves and formulas actually using Physical | Ownership, output permissions and independent action economy |
| Add +2 to one qualifying Finesse check | That check's applicable bonus | Physical, Prowess, capacity and unrelated attacks |
| Grant a sense with stated limits | Information and target location within that sense's actual scope | Damage output, unrelated knowledge and blocked sensory channels |
| Add maximum Vitality | Resolved maximum within its rule | Spent current Vitality is not automatically restored |
| Grant extra equipment slots | Available equipped capacity | Carried capacity, Load and Item BU |
| Grant advantage on a specified save | Bias dice for that save when conditions qualify | Static save bonus, all attacks and other saves |

The bearer, user and recipient may be different. An item can be carried by one actor, actively used by another and target a third. Its rule must say whose metric changes and when. A projected support field can supply a stated ally bonus without transferring permanent ownership of its ingredients to those allies.

### Provenance of a contribution

For a changed value, a useful breakdown identifies baseline, actual active source, recipient, condition, operation and result. Provenance explains why the value applies; it does not create an additional benefit.

**Example.** Torren's qualifying rope Finesse begins with Physical 5 and trained PB 2: +7. His owned Steady Hands supplies +2 while both hands are free. An agreed trembling-hands consequence subtracts 2 from delicate hand work until care and an agreed short rest. If both qualify, +7 +2 −2 = +7. The numerical cancellation does not end the consequence. If one hand is occupied, Steady Hands does not qualify, so the same injured check is +5.

Record the injury separately from the acquisition. Referencing Steady Hands through an upbringing and a capability is two provenance paths to one acquired rule, not an excuse to add its +2 twice.

## Conditions, stacking and rule interactions

### Define conditions that can be answered

A condition states when a rule applies. It can concern actor, target, scene, equipment, timing or a prior event. Identify the subject and enough detail for the table to answer. Avoid an undefined adjective doing the work of several different facts.

| Unclear phrase | Example of a settled authored condition |
| --- | --- |
| Near water | Within 10 ft of an open body of water large enough to immerse a hand |
| While prepared | After securing both anchors, until either anchor is moved |
| Against darkness | Against a target located through this stated sense in an unlit area |
| While using the hook | While equipped, firmly anchored and applying force through the hook |
| Until healed | Until the stated care and recovery condition, rather than any Vitality restoration |

These replacements are examples, not mandatory definitions of those words. If the author intends a broader or narrower condition, write that condition. A restrictive condition can affect the proposed price, but no fixed percentage discount is implied.

### Conditions do not import recipients or triggers

“While an ally falls” can qualify an actor's rule without establishing who can perceive the fall, whether they have reach or how they respond. “While singing” can restrict maintenance without granting a free action. “While equipped” does not make the item affect all allies.

For a trigger, identify the event, eligible observer, opportunity, response and action economy. If a response spends the independent Reaction Slot, it needs an available slot and the ordinary immediate scope unless an explicit exception changes them. If it is an automatic authored change, say what actually changes and when; do not conceal an extra action inside a trigger label.

**Example authored response.** When a conscious ally within ordinary reach begins to fall and the actor can perceive it, the actor may spend an available independent Reaction Slot to attempt a catch with a free hand. Resolve actual weight or interference through the agreed procedure. This example grants no remote rescue, additional slot or guaranteed success.

### Resolve applicability before stacking

1. Identify the actual acquisitions or sources, avoiding duplicated references.
2. Confirm each source's recipient, metric, condition and scope.
3. Apply stated uniqueness, caps, exclusions or nonstacking rules.
4. Combine the remaining contributions using their actual operation or special procedure.
5. Record continuing consequences independently of the resulting number.

Applicable contributions stack by default unless their authored rules say otherwise. This does not erase training's upgrade semantics, make repeated resistance quarter damage, or turn a reference into a purchase. Those are different kinds of contribution with explicit procedures.

When two rules contain incompatible instructions, identify the conflict and agree their interaction. Do not invent a global priority such as latest, highest BU or strongest title unless the campaign has deliberately adopted it. A future use can be revised before commitment; a surprise reinterpretation after a roll is not a reliable adjudication procedure.

### Probability bias calculation

Let A be qualifying advantage stacks and D qualifying disadvantage stacks after actual exclusions. Net bias is A − D. If positive, roll net bias +1 resolution dice and keep the highest. If negative, roll its absolute value +1 and keep the lowest. If zero, roll one. Then add the applicable static bonus once. Damage dice are not automatically resolution dice.

| Advantage | Disadvantage | Remaining bias | Resolution dice |
| ---: | ---: | --- | --- |
| 0 | 0 | None | 1d20 |
| 1 | 0 | 1 advantage | 2d20, highest |
| 2 | 1 | 1 advantage | 2d20, highest |
| 3 | 1 | 2 advantage | 3d20, highest |
| 1 | 3 | 2 disadvantage | 3d20, lowest |
| 2 | 2 | None | 1d20 |

**Checked example.** Three advantage sources and one disadvantage qualify for a check with +6. Roll 3d20: 4, 11 and 17. Keep 17 and add +6 once for 23. If the first advantage's condition fails, the same remaining sources leave one advantage: roll two dice instead. A static −2 penalty affects the +6 separately; it does not cancel an advantage stack unless its rule actually changes bias.

### Floors, ceilings and fixed results

Identify whether a rule constrains the raw die, the combined check result, a bonus or an underlying stat. “Minimum final Knowledge result 10” and “minimum d20 result 10” are mechanically different. Neither changes Mental by itself.

**Example authored floor.** A trained Knowledge check has combined bonus +5 and a rule saying its final result is at least 10. A die of 2 gives 7, then the stated final-result floor gives 10. If the rule instead says the selected d20 cannot be below 10, the same roll would become die 10 plus bonus 5, totaling 15. Record which was intended before use.

A fixed result likewise needs a stated stage and scope. Explain its relationship to bias and other modifiers. If a ceiling and floor conflict, revise or agree the interaction; this reference does not prescribe a secret winning operation. Natural 20 and natural 1 have no universal automatic success/failure or damage multiplier in the default framework.

## Character construction in full

### Foundation procedure for a new character

1. Agree the campaign, expectations, starting level or custom BU budget and separate equipment terms.
2. Write a concept, purpose and connection to the party. These descriptions do not themselves grant unlisted mechanics.
3. Allocate base Physical, Mental and Magical. The default total is 10; each base value is −1 through +5. Keep later contributions separate from that allocation.
4. Choose the proficient attribute under the agreed foundation and record additional actual training grants. Use the ten current practices, not an older eighteen-skill or sliced-bonus system.
5. Acquire positive owned verb and domain access in the character build. Identify each permission's bound scope. Items and mirrored access alone do not satisfy that foundation requirement.
6. Add other useful acquisitions, keeping actual ownership, permitted additional occurrences and credit readable. Missing range/output categories use Touch and 1d4 baselines.
7. Organize effects, capabilities and any useful heritages. Reusing an acquisition does not charge again.
8. Record equipment separately, including Item BU, Load, final slots and activation conditions.
9. Resolve current attributes, PB, practices, saves, selected DC, maximum Vitality, movement and capacities through their actual active rules.
10. Establish current state, including current Vitality, remaining short-rest allowance, consequences and any ongoing work.
11. Test normal, stretched and outside-scope uses. Review unclear rules, prices and limits before the first tense scene.

The foundation permits useful ordinary competence without purchasing a catalogue action for every task. A positive Water permission is not required to lift a bucket by hand. Conversely, ordinary competence does not provide distant extraordinary water control. Use the actual intended result to identify which access matters.

### Derived character calculations

| Quantity | Default calculation or interpretation |
| --- | --- |
| Baseline PB | 2 + floor((level −1)/4), then active PB rules |
| Practice bonus | Current full parent attribute + applicable training + actual qualifying modifiers |
| Proficiency training | One current PB where supplied |
| Eligible expertise training | Twice current PB where granted and eligible |
| Saving-throw bonus | Current relevant attribute + actual saving-throw training + qualifying save modifiers |
| Selected-attribute DC | 5 + current PB + selected eligible current attribute + direct DC contributions |
| Maximum Vitality | (10 + current PB) × level, then active maximum-Vitality rules |
| Short-rest allowance | ceil(maximum Vitality/2) between completed long rests; consume actual healing |
| Carrying capacity | Size base +5 × current Physical + applicable capacity contributions |
| Equipped slots | Six plus actual slot contributions |

Use the cumulative BU table for progression rather than substituting a remembered flat growth rate. At level 5, the supplied threshold is 69, baseline PB 3 and maximum Vitality 65 before modifiers. At level 21, retain the explicit 286-BU threshold. Spending a purchase does not reduce the character's established progression level.

**App note.** A custom starting budget and explicit DM bonus can affect the platform's progression pool. Current pool calculation takes the greater of declared starting BU and the cumulative level budget, then adds the explicit bonus. Item BU is excluded. This is a way to represent an agreed foundation, not permission to silently choose whichever budget is most favorable during play.

### Validation example for a complete foundation

**Example.** Nera is a lock-maintenance apprentice. Her table accepts level 1 with 25 character BU and ordinary equipment. Physical 4, Mental 4 and Magical 2 total 10; each is within the default base limits. Physical is proficient, so current PB 2 supplies training to Physical practices and the Physical save.

She purchases Verb II 8, grounded Metal I 4, Near 4, Knowledge proficiency 4 and d6 output 2: 22 BU. Metal I identifies grounded metal only. Verb II permits eligible changes to existing states; Near reaches up to 30 ft with actual targeting/path requirements. d6 establishes an output die type for suitable behavior, not healing or automatic attacks. Three ordinary BU remain.

Her Physical practices are +6. Awareness, Reason and Influence are +4; trained Knowledge is +6. Magical practices are +2. Saves are Physical +6, Mental +4 and Magical +2. Physical-selected DC is 5 +2 +4 =11. Maximum/current starting Vitality are 12/12 and the unused ordinary short-rest allowance is 6. Medium movement is 30 ft walking and 15 ft swim/climb; carrying capacity is 40 +5 ×4 =60 Load.

**Align the Latch** references Verb II, Metal I and Near: reshape a located existing metal latch within 30 ft. Its attempted precision, resistance, delivery arrangement and time are appraised. It does not identify a lock through an unseen wall or create a missing key. A descriptive Workshop Upbringing organizes Knowledge and those access purchases without changing the 22-BU acquisition ledger.

She carries one Medium two-handed hook: Item BU 0, Load 2, two equipped slots. She also carries one Medium rope bundle: Item BU 0, Load 2 and one slot if equipped. With both equipped she uses three of six slots and four of sixty Load. This entire record is reproducible without a catalogue lookup. Her unspent three BU supplies no unlisted mechanic.

### Changing a character without erasing state

A new acquisition changes the ledger. An additional reference changes composition. A reorganization into another heritage changes presentation unless actual rules change. A replacement definition can change permissions and derived values. Identify which change is occurring and agree any price, replacement or retraining terms; no universal retraining fee or free replacement interval is prescribed.

Preserve current Vitality when a maximum rises. If a maximum falls below current Vitality, the current value cannot exceed the new maximum, but losing maximum is not a new damage execution with a new lethal overrun. Consequences and ongoing work follow their actual conditions rather than disappearing merely because the sheet was edited.

**App note.** Draft edits, revision checks, backups and synchronization help preserve state. A read permission does not grant gameplay editing permission. The GM preference exposes interface controls without granting access to another person's private character. Sharing and authorized editing remain separate decisions.

## Creature construction and allocation reference

### Budget-derived foundation table

Choose positive base budget B. Compute unrounded rank r = sqrt(B/25). Base attribute points are ceil(3r). Baseline PB is 1 +ceil(r). Baseline maximum Vitality is max(1, ceil(B/2)). Apply rounding at the specified stage, not by first rounding rank. Rank is a creature foundation measure, not a player character level.

| Base creature BU | Rank | Base attribute points | Baseline PB | Baseline Vitality |
| ---: | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 1 | 0.2 | 1 | 2 | 1 |
| 25 | 1 | 3 | 2 | 13 |
| 50 | sqrt(2), about 1.414 | 5 | 3 | 25 |
| 75 | sqrt(3), about 1.732 | 6 | 3 | 38 |
| 100 | 2 | 6 | 3 | 50 |
| 150 | sqrt(6), about 2.449 | 8 | 4 | 75 |
| 225 | 3 | 9 | 4 | 113 |
| 250 | sqrt(10), about 3.162 | 10 | 5 | 125 |
| 1,000 | sqrt(40), about 6.325 | 19 | 8 | 500 |

Allocate exactly the available nonnegative base attribute points among Physical, Mental and Magical. Choose the proficient attribute and add actual granted modifications separately. Unlike the player foundation, this budget-derived allocation is not limited to a total of 10 or a maximum base attribute of +5.

**App note.** Creature definitions also retain practice-allocation slices whose sums match each base attribute. These satisfy the editor's allocation ledger. The current check procedure still uses the full relevant current attribute, not each slice as a reduced bonus. Do not teach a player to divide their Physical contribution among three checks because an older storage field remains present.

### Complete creature construction procedure

1. State purpose, motives, encounter use and observable behavior.
2. Choose positive base BU and compute the foundation.
3. Allocate points, select proficiency, size and the actual sensory/mobility facts.
4. Select readable acquisitions supporting the intended abilities. Inspect actual dependencies and reuse.
5. Define capabilities, their normal expressions, source/domain, targeting, path and resolution.
6. Define actual defenses, triggers and economy exceptions; neither size nor role title supplies them.
7. Record separate equipment mechanics, Load, slots and activation conditions.
8. Resolve current attributes, training, DC, maximum Vitality and actual conditional contributions.
9. Show base budget, positive acquired BU, eligible mirror credit, used budget/debt and remaining build allowance separately.
10. Test the creature in a favorable environment, against a counter-approach and after a relevant dependency changes.

Use an explicit agreed Vitality baseline override only when actually chosen; record it distinctly from the normal budget baseline and active modifications. A portrait of thick armor does not establish resistance. An environment tag organizes discovery; it does not grant a swimming speed. A support role suggests tactics; it does not grant an extra reaction.

### Reproducible example: the Gate Brace

**Authored example.** A small constructed assistant holds a dangerous gate steady while workers pass. Its chosen base budget is 50. Rank sqrt(2) gives five base points, PB 3 and baseline Vitality 25. Allocate Physical 3, Mental 1 and Magical 1; Physical is proficient.

| Acquisition | Bound rule | Creature BU |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| Verb II | Reshape, redirect, combine, separate, transfer or stabilize eligible existing states | 8 |
| Metal I | Grounded existing metal is the eligible subject, not all matter or social obligations | 4 |
| Near | Reach up to 30 ft with actual targeting/delivery requirements | 4 |
| d6 | This output die type for suitable damage/healing behavior; no automatic behavior | 2 |
| Braced Frame | While both feet are planted and both hands hold a fixed support, halve matching Physical damage rounded up | 8 |
| Total | No mirror credit | 26 |
| Remaining | 50 −26 | 24 |

Braced Frame's conditional defense and 8-BU price are this authored example, requiring table review. The rule covers Physical damage, not Magical or Psychic damage, every condition or an ally behind the creature. Let players observe the brace. Releasing the support removes the condition; taking a hit does not universally end every maintained working.

The creature's Physical practices are +6, Mental practices +1 and Magical practices +1. Saves are Physical +6, Mental +1 and Magical +1. Physical-selected DC is 5 +3 +3 =11. Small size gives walking 25 ft and ordinary swim/climb 13 ft. Ordinary sight and hearing supply no sense through walls. Capacity is 20 +5 ×3 =35 Load, with six equipped slots. Maximum/current starting Vitality are 25/25; its unused ordinary short-rest allowance is 13 when agreed recovery applies.

**Brace the Gate** references Verb II, Metal I and Near. It redirects or stabilizes a located existing metal mechanism within 30 ft. Appraise actual force, route, opposition and continuation. In its normal gate scene, the creature keeps both hands on a fixed brace; that maintained task prevents using those hands elsewhere. This is restrictive upkeep, not an automatic Vitality invoice.

**Metal Tap** uses the same access and d6 to strike one located target through an actual projected path up to 30 ft. This example uses Physical-trained attack +6 against DC, with 1d6 Physical Metal damage on a hit. It grants no disarm, armor, additional intent or healing. A changed expression requires appraisal and actual access. While both hands are bracing, the recorded circumstances may prevent performing this particular attack without releasing the brace.

The creature has one ordinary Medium two-handed support hook: Item BU 0, Load 2 and two final equipped slots. Its carried Load is two of thirty-five. The hook's ordinary function supplies no additional numerical modifier. The creature's remaining 24 BU does not become an unlisted ability or a runtime resource.

### Template cost and encounter cost

For building the Gate Brace, 26 spent and 24 remaining describe acquisitions. For encounter appraisal, one Gate Brace contributes its chosen base 50 creature BU plus resolved Item BU zero. Two contribute 100 creature BU, not 52 because they spend only 26 each.

Accepted mirrored drawbacks can increase permitted build headroom within their actual rules; that credit is not added again to chosen creature BU for appraisal. An equipment modifier belongs to the Item BU contribution, not both creature and item totals. Resolve shared ingredients against actual acquisitions and actual equipment occurrences before multiplying creature quantity.

### Independence when creatures share a definition

Gate Brace 1 and Gate Brace 2 can adopt the same pinned definition but occupy different places, have different current Vitality and make different choices. Each has its own ordinary Main Intent and Reaction Slot. Damage to one does not damage the other. Changing one play copy's state does not rewrite the template.

If the campaign authors a swarm or collective actor with shared state, state that exception and construct its actual rules. A quantity field alone does not create shared damage, shared reaction availability or simultaneous automatic actions.

## Item accounting, Load and equipped use

### Four independent measures

| Measure | Meaning | Not a substitute for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Item BU | Acquisition accounting for equipment mechanics | Money, carrying burden or extra actions |
| Ordinary value | Setting price, scarcity or exchange value | Mechanical permission or BU |
| Load | Carried burden, including equipped gear | Equipped-slot availability |
| Equipped slots | Available use capacity | Carrying capacity or Item BU |

A valuable relic can be mechanically ordinary. A simple-looking tool can carry an extraordinary rule. State the real properties rather than infer them from cost, material name or art. Containers organize contents; capacity changes require an actual rule.

### Quantity calculations for ordinary gear

For ordinary non-Tiny gear, multiply each item's stated Load by its actual carried quantity and sum the contributions. Size defaults are Small 1, Medium 2, Large 4, Huge 8 and Gargantuan 16 Load per item. Apply explicit authored Load changes at their scope. Equipped gear still contributes carried Load.

Tiny objects use the pouch treatment: one pouch holds up to 1,000 tiny objects and contributes one Load. Record actual packaging and any agreed special treatment. Do not invert the non-Tiny table into pieces per Load: a Large item's default is four Load for one item, not four Large items per Load.

For equipped gear, apply the final requirement to actual equipped quantity. Normally one slot; two-handed normally two; a higher authored requirement can represent an unusual item. Size alone does not multiply that requirement. Available slots begin at six plus actual slot grants.

### Checked mixed equipment ledger

**Example.** A Small actor has current Physical 3, so capacity is 20 +15 =35 Load. They possess the following actual items. There is no capacity or extra-slot grant.

| Item record | Carried | Equipped | Load each | Final slots each | Item BU each |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Medium two-handed hook | 2 | 1 | 2 | 2 | 0 |
| Small working tool | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 0 |
| Large two-handed blade, authored unusual fit | 1 | 1 | 4 | 3 | 0 |
| Pouch of 600 ordinary tiny tokens | 1 pouch | 0 | 1 per pouch | Not equipped | 0 |

Carried Load is 2 ×2 +1 +4 +1 =10, below capacity 35. Equipped slots are 2 +1 +3 =6. The spare hook uses carried Load but no equipped slots. This table's unusual blade explicitly requires three slots; neither its Large size nor its zero Item BU quietly changes that requirement. Another unusual item could be agreed differently.

Unequipping the blade frees three slots but leaves Load at ten while it remains carried. Leaving it behind reduces Load by four. Enlarging slot capacity does not change thirty-five Load capacity. A Load contribution above capacity is encumbered; the table agrees actual penalties or complications rather than importing a hidden universal package.

### Equipment modifier example

**Authored item example.** An anchored hook gives its equipped bearer +2 to Prowess checks to hold a gate while the hook is firmly fixed. Proposed Item BU 4, Medium Load 2, two-handed final requirement two slots. The actual condition must qualify; simply carrying it grants no bonus.

For an actor with current Physical 3 and applicable PB 3, ordinary trained Prowess is +6; the qualified hook makes this check +8. It does not increase Physical to 5, increase capacity by ten or improve every attack. If two capabilities reference the same equipped hook, that is still one item's contribution. If two actual augmented hooks are used, review feasibility and authored stacking before assuming both qualify.

Extraordinary equipment is not a substitute for the character's owned positive verb/domain foundation. It can supply its own usable behavior within the item rule. Record whether a capability depends on gear so losing or unequipping it has an intelligible effect.

## Execution envelopes and contextual appraisal

### Record the actual attempted expression

An acquired definition can support several attempts. The attempted expression identifies what happens now. Its scope may be fixed by the construction or negotiated within the access. Record enough detail to avoid turning every ambitious attempt into a vague request for more power.

| Expression detail | Question |
| --- | --- |
| Actor and intent | Who acts, and what result do they want? |
| Subject and change | Which bound domain and behavior support it? |
| Target location | How is this particular recipient located? |
| Range | Is the actual distance within the permission or agreed exception? |
| Delivery | Does output travel through a path or manifest directly through supported behavior? |
| Arrangement | Which recipients, area, shape or selectivity are intended? |
| Output | What damage, healing, information or change actually occurs? |
| Timing | What depends on another action, and when does it resolve? |
| Persistence | What lasts, and what supports it? |
| Stakes | What can success, failure or interruption mean? |
| Cost | Which agreed consequences apply, and at what point? |

This is a clarification tool, not eleven mandatory rolls or eleven new purchases. A small ordinary action can need only a sentence. An unusual multi-target maintained working needs more explanation because more facts can change its result.

### Access before scale

Verb concerns change; domain concerns subject; structure concerns arrangement; range concerns reach; output concerns eligible intensity or dice. They are separate permissions. Domain Water I does not automatically grant every Tier I subject. Verb II does not become Domain II. d8 is not a healing behavior or a ranged path.

Read the actual tier definition and bound primitive. Broader permitted scope can support an eligible narrower use, but a tier number alone does not make every lower-labeled catalogue entry an additional acquired occurrence. A new behavior needs its actual permission or an explicit table exception; a higher Cost cannot purchase it invisibly during resolution.

**Example.** Redirecting a located stream within 30 ft can fit Verb II, Water I and Near. Redirecting a street flood adds scale and pressure within that subject. Restoring a drowned person's life introduces a different behavior and subject relationship; it is not simply a greater number of water dice. A known empty trough supplies no existing water to reshape.

### Location, reach and path separately

For a specific target, establish location through a relevant sense. Vision, granted blindsight, tremorsense or another actual channel can qualify. A sense's limits matter: hearing an approximate sound does not universally pinpoint its maker. Authored alternative targeting, such as a defined scrying working or any qualifying target within range, must actually establish that method.

Then establish reach. Near permits up to 30 ft, Far 60, Very Far 120 and Extreme continuous reach up to approximately three miles, including the transition beyond 120. The DM can agree an extension. No extra distance reveals a target or negates the chosen sense's barriers.

Then establish delivery. A projected vector travels through intervening space; minor obstruction imposes −2 accuracy, half cover −4 and total cover blocks its trajectory. Judge the actual path. A supported direct manifestation avoids those intervening projected-cover penalties, but still needs legitimate target location and actual manifestation behavior. It is not granted solely by owning Far.

An area aimed at a known spot can be proposed without pinpointing every hoped-for creature. Resolve uncertainty and actual inclusion. The chosen area does not guarantee the desired target is inside or automatically exclude allies.

### Complexity, Strain and output separately

Complexity concerns dependencies and timing: Fast 0–1, Measured 2–3, Heavy 4+. Output size does not automatically determine track. Strain concerns the pressure of the whole attempt in context. The 0–6 guide runs from effortless through light effort, noticeable strain, heavy burden, dangerous instability, critical risk and catastrophic consequence.

A grade is not a universal Vitality invoice, acquisition surcharge or probability of success. State the actual consequence. Time, tools, assistance, environment, opposition, recipients and urgency can change it. Cost may be negligible, numerical, material, restrictive, environmental or narrative; combinations are possible.

**Optional evaluation.** If a table compares minimum evaluated composition B and selected expression A, ΔCV =A −B and total CV =B +ΔCV =A. For B 12 and A 30, the increase is 18 and the total is 30. These are evaluation figures, not BU repurchased on execution and not numbers added to a d20. Do not impose a universal percentage conversion from CV to Vitality.

## Resolution procedure and numerical edge cases

### Select the actual uncertainty

| Question | Suitable default procedure |
| --- | --- |
| Can an actor accomplish an uncertain task? | Check against agreed DC |
| Which opposing intent arrives first when timing matters? | Reaction Clash |
| Which directly opposing effort wins? | Active Contest |
| Does an attack hit a defended target? | Attack against target DC |
| Does a recipient resist a save-based working? | Relevant save against user's DC |
| Is the result secure under established circumstances? | Resolve directly without a compulsory die |

The same scene can contain several different questions. Do not automatically demand every procedure for one attempt. A capability does not require both attack and save by default. A clash deciding timing does not universally delete the losing intent. When the situation changes, revise the remaining approach from the actual new facts.

### Calculation sequence for a check

1. Establish the intended result, qualifying approach, DC and stakes before commitment.
2. Identify current attribute, applicable training and actual qualifying static contributions.
3. Resolve applicable bias after conditions and exclusions.
4. Roll/select the resolution die according to net bias.
5. Add the combined static bonus once and apply any authored result rule at its stated stage.
6. Compare to DC: meeting it succeeds under the agreed terms.
7. Apply the outcome and agreed consequences, including those that can apply independently of success.

**Checked example.** Physical 4, trained PB 3 and qualifying +2 produce combined bonus +9. One advantage and one disadvantage cancel. Roll one d20 showing 6: total 15, meeting DC 15. Do not add Physical and PB again to make 22. A separate final-result floor or unusual natural-result rule would apply only if actually authored and qualified.

### Attack and save outcomes

An attack's relevant current attribute, eligible training and attack modifiers form its combined bonus. Meeting the recipient's DC hits. Then resolve the stated damage or effect. A miss does not deal hit damage unless the actual rule says so. Source/domain defenses are applied to the resolved output, not subtracted from every attack roll as an unstated armor package.

A save uses the recipient's relevant current attribute, actual saving-throw training and save modifiers against the user's DC. Use the actual working's success/failure outcomes. There is no universal half-damage-on-success clause attached to every save. A practice proficiency grant does not automatically add PB to that attribute's save.

**Example.** A working states that a successful Mental save prevents its temporary perception change. A recipient with Mental 3 and no Mental-save training rolls 9 for 12 against DC 12 and resists that change. Knowledge proficiency does not raise this save. If a real Mental-save training grant supplies PB 2, the combined bonus is +5 instead of +3. The procedure does not also require the user to hit an attack first unless this particular construction establishes it.

### Damage instances and rounding

Identify execution source, domain and actual output portions before defenses. Resistance halves matching damage, rounding upward. Vulnerability doubles matching damage. Immunity negates covered damage. Matching resistance and vulnerability on the same instance cancel. Repeated resistance does not quarter damage by default. Damage immunity alone does not negate a separate condition.

| Final raw instance | Applicable defense | Vitality loss |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| 9 Magical Fire | Matching resistance | ceil(9/2) =5 |
| 9 Magical Fire | Matching vulnerability | 18 |
| 9 Magical Fire | Matching resistance and vulnerability | 9 |
| 9 Magical Fire | Matching damage immunity | 0 |
| 6 Physical Fire plus 8 Magical Fire | Magical-only resistance | 6 +4 =10 |

State whether mixed output splits one expression or creates separate damage instances. Dividing nine damage into two separately defended instances can change rounding; do not invent that split after seeing the result. Covered and uncovered portions follow their actual scope rather than treating a mixed-domain label as blanket immunity.

Apply final post-defense damage to current Vitality. Compare lethal overrun against the pre-hit current value and actual maximum before a stored sheet clamps current to zero. One execution dealing at least twice maximum or taking current to negative maximum can cause immediate death under the massive-damage rule; lethal stakes should be established.

**Checked boundary.** Maximum 13, current 4: final damage 16 reaches −12, below zero but above the negative-maximum boundary; handle collapse and rescue unless another actual rule changes it. Final damage 17 reaches −13. Final damage 26 independently meets twice maximum. Resistance is applied first: a covered raw 17 becomes 9, leaving 4 −9 =−5 rather than −13.

### Round movement arithmetic

Track one ordinary round allowance. For Speed 30, moving 10 in Fast leaves 20. A qualifying reaction using 5 leaves 15. Moving that 15 in Heavy exhausts the allowance. A later track or spent Reaction Slot does not reset it. Additional travel needs actual extra speed access or an agreed consequence/exception.

Actual routes and interference matter. A 30-ft allowance does not permit crossing 30 ft of sealed wall. Ordinary swim/climb defaults use half base speed rounded up; authored movement changes apply as written. When combining movement modes or special timing, state the intended interaction rather than inferring extra independent allowances from several displayed speed lines.

## Persistent workings and consequence state

### Duration and upkeep record

| Detail | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Start | When the result becomes active |
| Duration | Fixed interval, event ending or maintained continuation |
| Requirement | What the actor must do, pay, refrain from or preserve |
| Due point | Council or another explicitly agreed time/event |
| Failure to continue | What changes and at what moment |
| Lingering consequence | Any separately stated effect surviving the working |

A maintained capability has one upkeep track even when it contains multiple effects. Several maintained capabilities can coexist; their agreed requirements accumulate. Their records can impose practical conflicts, such as both needing both hands. There is no default single-concentration limit and no universal hit-triggered interruption.

**Example timeline.** A channel remains directed while the actor sings and hears its echo. Council is the agreed continuation point. A separate lantern working consumes a stated material each Council. At the next Council the actor can still sing but has no lantern material: the channel may continue, while the lantern ends according to its terms. Losing one requirement does not automatically end every maintained capability.

If the channel's difficult use also imposed loss of Water reshaping after it ends until a stated task, stopping the song ends the channel but begins or preserves that separate consequence as agreed. Healing current Vitality does not perform the recovery task. Ending an encounter does not silently remove either record.

### Interference and payment

State Cost and when it applies before commitment. An attempt may require payment at commitment, at resolution, at a continuation point or through another actual condition. Interruption has no universal refund; decide from the agreed terms and what actually happened. An instantaneous resolved execution cannot be interrupted retroactively, though its resulting persistent effect may be disrupted through suitable access.

An actor unable to perform a required task cannot continue it. Silence can matter to singing; loss of an anchor can matter to an anchored field; unconsciousness can prevent active direction. A wound matters if it changes actual ability or a relevant authored requirement, not because every hit imposes an imported concentration save.

**Alpha rule pending.** Additional Vitality repayment triggered by incoming damage, including its threshold, counter window, reset and excess handling, remains undecided. Do not automate or publish the historical damage-threshold proposal as settled. Ordinary agreed continuation, including recurring Vitality payments, remains available. This pending question supplies no damage counter for restrictive, material or narrative upkeep.

### Consequence definition and state format

Record cause, subject, actual change, timing/trigger and ending/recovery. If a consequence has several effects, make each legible. A numerical cancellation does not end its state; an agreed ending does. A new round, scene, rest or session clears it only when its terms say so.

| Example consequence | Actual continuing terms |
| --- | --- |
| Trembling hands | −2 to delicate Finesse hand work until stated care and an agreed short rest |
| Lost Water access | The specified permission is unavailable until quiet time at a natural moving stream, under the agreed terms |
| Borrowed key | Return it before inspection and assist its lender with the explanation |
| Damaged outlet | Water damages the stated building/supplies; repair or restitution remains a fictional problem |

These examples have no universal price or compulsory Strain grade. Named consequences should create understandable choices. If a record has unclear scope or no intelligible ending, clarify it with the table before silently applying a broader penalty.

## Recovery accounting and rescue transitions

### Rescue states

| State | Default treatment |
| --- | --- |
| Above zero Vitality | Actor can act subject to actual conditions/consequences |
| Collapsed and actively dying | Unconscious/incapacitated; contextual rescue countdown needs intervention |
| Stabilized at zero | Countdown halted; no automatic healing or consciousness |
| Healed above zero | Dying ends and consciousness returns unless another consequence prevents it |
| Immediate lethal result | Distinct from a rescue clock; do not infer rescue from a zero-clamped display |

An ally can dedicate an action track to Fieldcraft stabilization or suitable owned primitives. Establish access, time, resolution and Cost. There is no universal stabilization DC or mandatory training gate for an ordinary attempt. Roughly a minute is rescue guidance, not ten guaranteed safe rounds in every environment. A stabilized actor may recover through assisted rest; active dying needs rescue first.

### Finite short-rest formula

Let M be the applicable maximum Vitality, C current Vitality, R remaining agreed short-rest recovery allowance and H the accepted requested restoration. Actual healing cannot exceed missing Vitality, remaining allowance or the agreed restoration. For ordinary nonnegative values, actual restored points are the least of H, M −C and R. New current is C plus actual healing; remaining allowance is R minus actual healing.

The normal pool at a completed long rest is ceil(M/2). Unused points carry across short rests. A pause does not replenish the pool. A table adjustment may change the available allowance or accepted healing, but it must be stated rather than inferred from time passing.

**Checked ledger.** Maximum 13 begins with R 7. At C 10, accepting up to 7 restores only 3, reaching 13 and leaving R 4. After later damage to C 9, another agreed rest restores 4, reaching 13 and leaving R 0. After damage to C 8, another ordinary short rest restores 0 from that exhausted pool. A completed long rest normally restores to 13 and refreshes R to 7 independently of those earlier uses.

### Partial long-rest treatment

A long rest is agreed overnight sleep or equivalent recovery, not a mandatory eight-hour timer. The table can agree partial recovery under poor circumstances. State what a percentage applies to: an amount restored, a target current value or another explicit treatment. “70% recovery” without a denominator does not supply reproducible arithmetic.

**Example agreed interpretation.** At maximum 13 and current 2, the table permits restoration of 70% of maximum: ceil(0.7 ×13) =10 points. Current becomes 12, capped at 13 if the accepted amount would exceed it. This is one explicitly chosen interpretation, not a mandatory partial-rest formula. The completed long rest refreshes the ordinary short-rest pool to 7; long-rest healing is not charged against it.

Maximum changes and special recovery rules must have intelligible terms. Increasing maximum alone does not grant current healing. If a maximum changes during a rest cycle and an authored rule affects the allowance, record that rule or agree its treatment rather than silently refilling spent recovery. Lingering injuries, obligations and lost access retain their own endings.

## Encounter preparation, appraisal and independent runs

### Preparation procedure

1. Describe the actual situation, objective, visible pressures and possible alternatives.
2. Enter Party BU and separate Party Item BU manually, or choose to calculate from accessible characters.
3. Record party size if known; it is optional, and unknown size does not prevent preparation.
4. Select creature definitions, adopted versions and quantities. Review actual dependency availability.
5. Resolve each creature's chosen base BU and separate Item BU; keep build spent/remaining information distinct.
6. Calculate quantity totals and available comparisons without inventing missing values.
7. Review counts, budget concentration, tactics, terrain, information, objectives and alternatives.
8. Preserve the approved preparation separately from the state of each actual run.

Manual totals are the primary sufficient workflow. Linked characters provide optional budget references and quick access to their records. Linking does not grant the GM permission to change a character's gameplay state. If calculated totals are used, show the result and intentionally replace or override manual values; a later character change should not silently rewrite a saved preparation.

### Appraisal equations

For each creature entry i, let qᵢ be actual quantity, Bᵢ its chosen base creature budget and Iᵢ its resolved Item BU. Enemy creature BU is the sum of qᵢ ×Bᵢ. Enemy Item BU is the sum of qᵢ ×Iᵢ. Combined enemy total is their sum. Party combined total is Party BU plus Party Item BU where both are known.

Difference is combined enemy minus combined party. Ratio is combined enemy divided by a known positive combined party total. Do not calculate a ratio with a zero or unknown denominator. Enemy count is the sum of actual quantities. Largest-creature share is the largest individual chosen base creature budget divided by total enemy creature BU where that total is positive.

Largest-creature share concerns one creature, not an entire repeated entry. Five identical 25-BU creatures contain 125 enemy creature BU; each is 20% of that total, not 100% because the roster has one row. An entry can separately show its group contribution.

### Checked mixed-roster appraisal

**Example.** A prepared encounter uses two Canal Watchers and one Gate Brace. The Canal Watcher is the complete 25-BU creature in Worked records: no items, baseline Vitality 13 and its stated Water capabilities. The Gate Brace above is base 50, Item BU zero and baseline Vitality 25. No mirror credit changes appraisal.

| Entry | Quantity | Base BU each | Item BU each | Creature subtotal | Item subtotal |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Canal Watcher | 2 | 25 | 0 | 50 | 0 |
| Gate Brace | 1 | 50 | 0 | 50 | 0 |
| Enemy totals | 3 actors | — | — | 100 | 0 |

Three default level 1 characters contribute 75 Party BU even if each has spent only 24. If their actual equipment is 8 Party Item BU, combined party is 83. Combined enemy is 100; difference +17; ratio 100/83, approximately 1.205. Enemy count equals known party size three. The largest individual creature is 50/100 =50% of enemy creature BU.

If the Gate Brace instead adopts the 4-Item-BU augmented hook above, enemy Item BU becomes 4 and combined enemy 104. Difference becomes +21 and ratio 104/83, approximately 1.253. Its chosen creature BU stays 50; do not count the same hook as four additional creature BU as well. The example equipment changes its actual qualifying Prowess contribution and item record, so review that definition rather than merely changing the displayed total.

The ratios are arithmetic summaries, not supplied difficulty grades. Budget concentration, control, area output, support, burst, environment, sensory barriers, objectives and table rulings can change the encounter substantially. Spending every remaining creature BU is not a requirement for usefulness; unspent build points do not grant hidden actions.

### Missing information and incomplete comparisons

Unknown is different from zero. Party Item BU zero means known ordinary or zero-cost mechanics; unknown Party Item BU means combined comparison cannot yet be completed. An inaccessible template is not a free creature. Preserve the preparation's reference and identify the unresolved information without revealing private content.

An empty opposition can be a valid situation to prepare; it has no enemy creature budget concentration. Party size absent means omit count comparison. Combined party zero means omit a ratio even if totals and difference are known. The group can save notes and decide the roster later; complete numerical comparison is not a prerequisite to describing an encounter.

### Starting and replaying

Create independent state for each actual creature from the adopted definition. Name repeated creatures clearly. Start with the actual maximum/current Vitality, position, consequences, available reactions and ongoing work. A future template edit does not retroactively rewrite an existing actor's pinned content or wounds.

**App note.** Starting a platform run creates independent private monster play copies from prepared pins. Previewing or adding an entry does not create a play copy. Repeated retry-safe starting requests do not create duplicate creatures. Resume opens an existing run; Start new run creates fresh copies and preserves previous runs. Starting needs connectivity; an authorized cached run can support existing offline gameplay and queued state changes through its established synchronization.

These platform protections are implementation behavior, not extra tabletop rules. Paper play uses separate state lines or sheets. If the party returns to the same injured guardian, continue its actual state rather than accidentally substituting a fresh copy. If the group deliberately replays the situation, identify that new run.

| Record | What changes during play |
| --- | --- |
| Shared round and phase | Council, Fast, Measured, Heavy and the current round count |
| Actor intent | Coherent Main Intent, track, dependencies and resolved status |
| Position and movement | Actual location/route and ordinary movement already used |

At Council, coordinate the changed scene, address due continuation and refresh ordinary independent reactions. Starting the next round clears prior round intent/resolved markers, not injuries, current Vitality or every ongoing effect. Resolve tracks from actual dependencies. Advancing a phase is organizational; it does not automatically apply damage, expire effects or alter a linked character.

Ending a run records completion and aftermath without deleting its creatures or erasing consequences. Deleting preparation is not permission to erase independently playable copies. Archive relevant history and keep actual authorized state controls distinct from a viewer's budget references.

## Mirror accounting and creation boundaries

### Eligible reversal

Supported opposites include Add/Subtract, Multiply/Divide, Minimum/Maximum and Grant/Revoke. Reverse the actual eligible rule within its scope and access, respecting authored non-mirrorable limits. Set To has no automatic opposite. A descriptive reversal requires a coherent agreed meaning; a negative name alone establishes neither drawback nor credit.

The accepted personal drawback remains applied to its recipient. Separately granting the opposite to an ally does not remove it or repay the ledger. A drawback's condition and duration must still represent its real reviewed scope. Review claimed credit against the actual acquired definition rather than treating every inconvenience in play as a purchase refund.

### Distinct budget figures

| Figure | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Ordinary budget | Agreed positive character allowance |
| Positive spent | Actual positive acquisitions, counted once |
| Mirror credit | Accepted eligible drawback credit |
| Base used | Positive acquisitions allocated to ordinary budget first |
| Used debt | Positive spending beyond ordinary budget covered by actual eligible credit |
| Unused credit | Accepted credit not currently needed for those purchases |
| Uncovered overflow | Spending beyond ordinary budget and actual eligible credit |
| Debt ceiling | Maximum permitted drawback credit under the level rule, not automatic credit |

The default ceiling is 4 ×ceil(level/4): four at levels 1–4, eight at 5–8, twelve at 9–12, continuing. Creation accounting applies ordinary budget, then eligible credit, then any uncovered overflow. DM agreement is required for exceeding the ordinary allowance; limits on permitted credit and the next-level creation ceiling still apply. Do not treat a later soft sheet warning as permission to ignore the original agreement.

### Three checked credit cases

**Example A.** Level 1, ordinary budget 25, positive spent 23, accepted credit zero. Base used 23, ordinary unspent 2, used debt zero. The four-point debt ceiling is only a limit; it does not give four extra BU.

**Example B.** Level 1, ordinary budget 25, positive spent 27, accepted eligible credit 2. Base used 25; used debt 2; unused credit zero; uncovered overflow zero; net spent 25. The two-point drawback remains actual. The accepted credit is below the four-point ceiling and net spent is below the next-level creation reference of 35.

**Example C.** Level 1, ordinary budget 25, positive spent 31, accepted credit 2. Base used 25; covered used debt 2; uncovered overflow 4; net spent 29. The ceiling does not invent another two or four credit. This proposal requires explicit DM approval for its uncovered excess and remains subject to the current creation limits. It is not a legal claim that every under-next-level proposal must be approved.

Item BU remains outside these character acquisition figures. Mirror credit is not another creature budget in encounter appraisal. CV and runtime consequences do not silently reduce positive acquisition spending.

## Reference validation before publication or play

### Definition checklist

- Can a reader identify actual recipients, behavior, conditions and ending terms?
- Does each numerical operation name its metric and intended stage?
- Are training upgrades, static bonuses and bias distinguished?
- Does targeting specify location and path rather than relying on range alone?
- Are duration and continuation separate from track timing?
- Do unusual triggers state their action economy instead of hiding extra actions?
- Are suggested prices and custom example prices labeled as reviewed proposals?
- Can the required definitions be read without an inaccessible catalogue link?

### Ledger and state checklist

- Count actual acquisitions once, resolving shared references and deliberate quantities.
- Keep positive character/creature BU, eligible credit and separate Item BU legible.
- Verify foundation formulas before active contributions and current state.
- Check item quantity, carried Load, equipped quantity and final requirements independently.

- For repeated creatures, keep independent state and actual adopted definitions.

### Reproducibility checklist

Test a normal use, an ambitious eligible use and an outside-scope request. Show at least one numerical breakdown where the construction affects a number. For contextual consequences, show what changes and how the record ends. For encounters, show quantity multiplication and which comparisons are unavailable rather than substituting zeros.

Retain the one pending alpha rule in its actual section: damage-triggered additional Vitality upkeep and counter handling. A temporary campaign treatment can be written and used by agreement, but must be labeled as that treatment. It must not appear elsewhere as a quietly settled core formula.

### Digital and tabletop limits

The platform can support creation, validation, versioned references, permissions and synchronization. A software limit on record length or creature count is not a new law of the fictional world. A GM interface preference is not an account role granting private access. A preview is not a write action. A template is not the same thing as a creature's present state.

For compatible offline creation, preserve the readable definitions, acquisition ledger, calculation procedure and actual state. For digital use, preserve existing authorized controls and access checks. Both approaches serve the same play framework: make intentions intelligible, establish what supports them, agree stakes, resolve the relevant uncertainty and carry the consequences forward.
