## Watch the decisions inside a whole encounter

This chapter slows one encounter down so you can see the decisions behind the spoken description. It uses the published workshop records, including their limits. Read the conversation, then study the changing record. The point is to make the shared rhythm usable when several people act together, not to establish a compulsory script for a canal adventure.

**Original annotated example.** A retractable footbridge has stuck above a narrow spill channel. A fallen timber blocks the arm that would lower it. Workers want a crossing for their carts. Two Canal Watchers fear that a sudden release will wash timber into a spawning bed. The party wants a safe crossing. Nobody needs the guardians dead. This is a separate teaching scene, not an additional required sequence in The gate that will not open.

### Carry only the records this scene will use

Mira, Torren and Ilex are the three starting characters in the player workshops. Each has a progression budget of 25 BU and 24 positive BU acquired, with no mirror credit. Party BU is therefore 75, not 72. For this example, their ordinary equipment has no extraordinary acquisition costs: Party Item BU is 0. An item with an added rule would change that column, even if nobody used it during the encounter.

The Watchers each use a chosen base budget of 25, with 20 acquired and 5 remaining. Their ingredients are Verb II, Water I, Near and d8. Water Impact is a single-target projected attack +3 against DC, dealing 1d8 Magical Water on a hit under the example's normal expression. Turn the Current redirects existing located water within 30 ft. Their unused budget adds no invisible sense, new action, healing or water creation.

The enemy appraisal is 50 creature BU plus 0 Item BU. Enemy minus party is −25 and the ratio is 50/75, approximately 0.67. There are two enemies and three party members. Each enemy holds half the enemy creature budget. These are facts about the roster. They do not say the crossing is safe, that the Watchers must lose, or that a flooded route cannot cause a severe consequence.

### Establish space before introducing timing

Use these stated distances, or draw them on a small sketch. The party begins on a broad western landing. The timber and lowering arm are 20 ft along a walkway. The walkway has a solid post at its start and a smaller brace beside the arm. Both Watchers stand in shallow water beside the walkway, each within 30 ft of the timber and the central portion of the channel. The spawning bed is farther downstream, outside the immediate work area.

A person at the timber can see both Watchers. Mira can locate the narrow channel she wants to redirect from the landing. Ilex can speak to the guardians without shouting over an impossible distance. The walkway offers no cover against the first projected attack. If someone later moves behind the solid landing wall, that path changes. Do not announce a permanent battlefield-wide accuracy penalty just because water is present.

Describe two warnings. Loose boards knock against the bridge arm. A Watcher repeatedly points downstream, then at the timber. These are useful observations, not hidden prizes for a successful check. The workers tell the party that lowering the bridge with the timber still in place could break the arm. Their account is ordinary information from people who know the machinery.

> **Before Council**
>
> DM: “You can reach the timber along the walkway. Both guardians can see that route. One is gathering water beside its hands. The other points from the timber toward the reeds downstream.”
>
> Ilex: “I want to understand what they are warning us about.”
>
> DM: “You can plainly see the direction. Ressa says that the reeds hold the spawning bed. You do not need a roll to learn what she knows.”
>
> Torren: “Then I want a line on the timber before anyone moves it.”

This exchange gives the group enough information to coordinate. It has not given them a free successful repair, and it has not forced them into combat. The threats and resources now have recognizable relationships.

### First Council: say what depends on what

Mira intends to redirect a narrow portion of the existing channel so the timber can be worked without washing loose downstream. This is supported by Water I, Verb II and Near. She does not claim control of every stream or permanent protection for all living things. The table agrees that directing this particular lane requires her to keep attending to it; while she does so, this working cannot be redirected to a separate water task. That is a scene-specific restrictive upkeep agreement.

Torren intends to secure the timber with rope. He will move 10 ft early, then the remaining 10 ft as the channel settles. He has 30 ft for the whole round. Both hands will be free for threading and tying after he sets his ordinary hook aside as part of the declared task. The table accepts that mundane handling within this intent. It does not establish a universal rule that every equipment change is an unlimited free action.

Ilex intends to explain the safe plan to the Watchers. They name the spawning bed and offer to keep the timber tied until it reaches the landing. This addresses the actual objection. Ilex is not rewriting the guardians' agency. The DM decides Watcher B is willing to consider a demonstrated plan; Watcher A remains distrustful and intends to strike Torren before the timber moves.

Watcher B intends to keep the current away from the bed, opposing Mira's channel because it mistakes the proposed flow for a release. Its immediate control and Mira's immediate control meet in Fast. The question is whose opposed effort shapes that water, so the table selects a contest. It does not add a separate setup DC check to resolve the same opposed question a second time.

| --- | --- | --- |
| Mira directs the narrow lane | Fast, Complexity 1 | Located existing water; Watcher B opposes the same flow |
| Watcher B turns that flow back | Fast, Complexity 1 | Same water and actual control contest |
| Watcher A strikes Torren | Fast, Complexity 1 | Visible target within 30 ft and clear projected path |
| Torren secures the timber | Measured, Complexity 2 | Reach the arm; channel settles; anchor and tie the line |
| Ilex explains the plan and points out the tether | Measured, Complexity 2 | Visible demonstration supports the explanation |

These complexity choices belong to the described dependencies. A different approach can change them. They are not prices attached to water magic, persuasion or rope. No participant receives another Main Intent because they are in a later track.

### First Fast: resolve one question at a time

Mira rolls 13 and adds her combined Magical bonus +6 for 19. Watcher B rolls 11 and adds its combined Magical bonus +3 for 14. Mira wins the actual control contest. Describe a narrow lane turning toward the safe bank while the guardian's contrary flow curls around it. Her success establishes that lane under the agreed maintenance restriction. It does not damage the guardian or acquire another Water primitive.

Watcher A attacks Torren through the clear path. Its d20 result is 14; 14 + 3 = 17 against Torren's DC 12. The attack hits. Its d8 result is 5. Torren has no applicable Water resistance in the example record, so he loses 5 Vitality, going from 12 to 7. His ordinary coat does not secretly supply armor. He has not also failed a saving throw: this expression uses an attack, not both procedures by default.

Torren could propose a valid response if his actual position and permissions support one. Spending a slot alone would not make the attack miss, halve its damage or provide a distant counterattack. His player chooses to continue the repair and preserve the slot. A reaction is an opportunity for an eligible action, not a mandatory defense calculation on every hit.

The hit does not automatically end Mira's lane. It struck Torren, but even a hit on Mira would require the actual interference rule or a relevant consequence. The alpha has no settled additional damage-triggered Vitality-upkeep repayment scheme. Do not introduce one here because the scene contains injury.

### First Measured: the setup makes another choice secure

Torren has moved 10 ft in Fast. He moves another 10 in Measured, reaches the timber and has 10 ft of the round's walking allowance unused. He secures the rope with both hands. Steady Hands qualifies for this precise threading and tying work, so his Finesse is +9. It is not added to every Physical check or to his DC.

The table had agreed DC 14 for making the tether secure before the timber shifts. Torren rolls 9: 9 + 9 = 18. The tether holds. On a failure, the agreed outcome was an insecure line and a changed opportunity, not an unexplained disappearance of all rope. On this success, the workers can see the timber is secured. It has not yet been pulled out.

Ilex explains the tether and points to Mira's safe lane. Watcher B can observe the proof that answers its concern. The DM does not require Influence merely to insist that every social action has a die. Watcher B agrees to inspect the downstream route next round. Watcher A sees the same facts but has a different judgment: it will not consent until the timber is moving away from the bed.

If Watcher B were frightened of a hidden obligation or distrusted Ilex specifically, an Influence check might resolve that uncertainty. The check would need a stated question and outcome. The record's +7 does not automatically overwrite every motive. Here the proposed arrangement is credible enough for B to accept without that uncertainty.

### Read the first-round state aloud

No Heavy action remains, so the group reviews the actual changes before the next Council. A digital phase control can organize that review; it does not apply it for you.

| Mira | 12/12; safe landing; narrow lane active; directing requirement continues |

This summary takes less time than retelling the conversation. It makes the next decision possible. A single “party winning” marker would hide the injury, the differing guardians and the unfinished objective.

### Second Council: change intentions when the world changes

The new Council refreshes the ordinary independent Reaction Slot for each participant. It does not refresh current Vitality, short-rest allowance or the whole list of consequences. The group replaces the previous round's intent markers with these new declarations.

Mira accepts the continuation requirement and intends to keep the lane directed. Nothing has changed its scale or opposition enough to demand another identical roll. The table can simply continue the established working while its terms hold. This is her meaningful contribution this round; she does not also announce another complete water attack without reviewing compatibility and a coherent intent. Her choice here does not establish that every upkeep requirement consumes a Main Intent; other actual requirements can permit other compatible work.

Torren intends to pull the timber toward the landing using the secured rope. The table agrees a Prowess check, DC 14, because the timber is caught under uneven pressure. His +7 applies. Steady Hands does not apply: pulling a load is not threading or tying a rope. The announced failure is that the timber stays caught and loosens the small brace, creating an immediate risk of the loose rope end dropping into the channel.

Ilex intends to carry and guide that end beside Torren. They move 20 ft along the walkway early in the round. Their help makes the chosen approach physically possible and puts them near the loose end. The table has not granted a numerical help bonus, an extra roll or a success entitlement merely because they said “I help.” If a bonus were proposed, its actual rule or agreed exception would need to be stated.

Watcher A's intent is to guide stray splinters away from the bed. That works on a different small portion of existing water from Mira's lane. Watcher B inspects the downstream release. They have each changed their coherent Main Intent after seeing the tether; they are not secretly attacking as well because their roster role says enemy.

### Second Measured: a failure creates a response

Torren rolls 5: 5 + 7 = 12 against DC 14. The pull does not free the timber. The small brace shifts as announced, and the loose end of the line begins dropping. The scene changes at the point of failure. Nobody needs to retroactively reveal a second hidden penalty.

Ilex is beside the line, conscious, able to perceive it and has a free hand. They propose catching the falling end. The table agrees that this is an immediate Complexity 1 response affecting one reachable object. It uses their available independent Reaction Slot. They have the opportunity; the wet rope still makes success uncertain.

The table selects Fieldcraft, DC 12. Ilex's combined bonus is +4. They roll 8, totaling 12, and catch it. The reaction is now spent for this round. It pauses the triggering fall, resolves the catch, and continues the scene from the new fact. The timber has not been removed. Catching this line does not catch every loose object elsewhere on the bridge.

Torren does not get another full pull immediately because the reaction succeeded. His attempted intent has resolved. The party can prepare another approach at the next Council, or pivot within any actual unresolved part of the scene as agreed; this particular failed pull is not silently rerolled until it works.

### Second-round review: keep a consequence separate from failure

Torren remains at 7/12. The failed pull did not inflict damage because the pre-agreed outcome was environmental. Ilex's reaction is spent. Mira's lane continues with the same restriction. The damaged brace changes how the next pull can be made. Watcher A has redirected stray splinters; Watcher B confirms a safe downstream route.

The DM says, “The tether is still sound. The smaller brace is loose. A worker can place a board under the rope, but you need to draw the timber along the landing side rather than straight upward.” This is new actionable information produced by the failure. It does not establish that a failed roll always awards a clue. It follows from this mechanical problem becoming visible.

Ask whether the group still wants the same objective. They can stop and wait for a repair crew, secure the bridge in its raised position, or make the supported pull. Continuing is a choice. The shared rhythm should not make a player feel that the only permitted next decision is another attack.

### Third Council and resolution: the objective decides the ending

At the third Council, Ilex's ordinary reaction is available again because a new round has begun, not because the catch was erased. The secured line remains a changed fact. The workers place the board while everyone can see the route. Mira continues directing the lane. Ilex guides the tether from the landing side. Torren proposes the revised pull. Watcher A moves a small flow away from the board; Watcher B signals when the downstream path is clear. Ordinary preparation and their actual Water access support those acts. Neither guardian gains Life control or a free healing effect because it has become cooperative.

The table agrees that removing the caught timber remains uncertain but the announced failure is now simply that it stays lodged and the group must use a slower repair. Torren rolls 8 and adds +7 for 15 against DC 14. The timber comes free. It is pulled toward the landing rather than released downstream. The lowering arm can move.

Do not demand a new check for every secure subsequent step. Workers familiar with the mechanism lower the bridge carefully once the obstruction is gone and the line is safe. If a separate unresolved problem existed, it would need attention; the DM has not prepared one merely to lengthen the encounter. Mira releases her lane once it is unnecessary, ending its directing restriction. The Watchers leave the route open.

End when the crossing is practically safe, not when the two healthy guardians lose all Vitality. A three-round scene has involved injury, opposition, setup, reaction, negotiation, a failed action and a changed plan. It used no initiative roll, extra boss action, compulsory damage tariff or secret concentration rule.

### After the bridge: a recovery ledger that tells the truth

Torren is at 7/12, with all 6 of his short-rest allowance unused. The group takes a protected pause and agrees that it qualifies as a short rest. He restores 5, reaches 12/12 and retains 1 allowance. He does not lose the sixth point merely because this pause ended, and he does not refresh to 6 by taking another short pause immediately.

Later, a separate event deals 3 actual damage, leaving Torren at 9/12. At a second agreed short rest he can use the remaining 1, reaching 10/12. The other 2 missing Vitality is not paid by an empty allowance. An agreed long rest can restore him to 12 and refresh the allowance to 6; its healing does not spend that pool. These are ordinary examples without a DM-adjusted recovery amount.

The bridge's loose brace still needs replacement. Ressa's promised introduction still matters. Neither is healed by Torren's recovery. The final record distinguishes personal state, environmental repairs and relationships. That distinction gives the next session something understandable to begin with.

### What this encounter would reveal in a review

Ask whether the players could name the objective and the guardians' concern before commitment. Ask whether Mira's maintenance restriction was clear enough to guide her second-round choice. Check that Torren's +9 appeared only during qualifying rope work. Check that Ilex's response consumed the available slot and that it did not replay Torren's failed pull.

If the scene felt slow, remove redundant questions before changing the rules. You can shorten the record review or give obvious facts earlier. If it felt arbitrary, look for unannounced failure stakes, unknown distances or a changed definition. These are repairable preparation issues. Raising or lowering enemy BU would not automatically correct them.

Try one variation as a thought exercise. Put Watcher A behind the landing wall; its projected path is blocked until it changes position. Empty the channel; neither Water capability creates a new supply. Place the timber 50 ft from Mira; her Near permission no longer reaches it from the landing. Give Ilex no free hand; catching the line needs a changed setup. Each alteration changes a specific part of the plan, not the entire construction language.

## Design pressure that offers useful choices

Encounter design begins with what the participants can decide. A difficult number can make a roll uncertain, but it cannot supply a meaningful reason to roll. Terrain, competing aims, information and actual creature permissions produce the decisions that make a scene memorable.

**Optional design method.** Write a sentence naming the decision before choosing a roster: “Do we hold the crossing while the last cart passes, or abandon the cart to protect the spawning bed?” Then list the facts that make both answers plausible. A good dilemma permits ingenuity to change its terms. It is not a rule that a player must always sacrifice one objective.

:::art watcher

### Sketch a field of opportunities rather than a decorative arena

Draw the routes people can use and the things that interrupt them. Put ordinary resources on the sketch: a cart that can block a path, a wall that offers actual cover, a stair, a rope anchor, a water supply, a bell that can be heard from another location. Include locations where someone can withdraw or change the angle. A map becomes useful when its marks answer a practical question.

For a compact outdoor scene, three connected places may be enough: an exposed crossing, a protected bank and an elevated observation point. Give their distances. Explain whether the elevation changes sight, cover or ordinary climbing. Do not label every raised platform as a numerical advantage unless the actual circumstance or an explicit rule supplies it.

Consider senses separately from reach. A creature with a long-range working may not locate targets behind reeds. An observer on a tower may locate them but still lack the required reach or path. A tremorsense grant may help locate someone through one surface but say nothing about a flying target. Let the actual definition create useful distinctions rather than treating every sense as a universal radar.

Mark which barriers block projected delivery and which merely obstruct it. The published projected-path defaults are −2 for minor obstruction, −4 for standard half cover and a blocked trajectory for total cover. Direct manifestation requires its actual support and targeting even when those intervening projected penalties do not apply. A wall is not rendered irrelevant by a broad ability name.

### Put the controller's limitation where people can see it

The Sluice Warden is a useful control example. Its Guiding Wall organizes a located band of existing water within 30 ft through Verb II, Water I, Near and Structure II. The temporary obstruction has a stated shape, crossing question and continuation requirement. It does not become an unconditional immobilization label on everyone in the area.

Give the Warden a visible brace. Its Braced Resistance halves covered Physical damage, rounded up, while both feet and one hand brace the fixed sluice. A character who wants to move it away, loosen the hook or negotiate a different flow has a recognizable counter-approach. Present the brace before the party spends several attempts against a defense it could not know existed.

The fixed-hook brace also gives +2 to Prowess checks to hold the gate when anchored. Warden Prowess is normally +4, becoming +6 for that condition. It does not become Physical 6, raise every Physical practice or add to a water attack. The equipment contributes 4 Item BU, separate from the 100 chosen creature budget and 52 acquired creature BU.

**Example design test.** A rope pulls the Warden away from the fixed sluice. Review the actual opposed effort and anchoring, rather than declaring that “controller” means immovable. If the brace condition ends, the resistance no longer qualifies. Its acquired Physical increment remains part of current Physical; losing an equipment condition does not undo an unrelated purchase.

### Make support change an understandable relationship

A support role needs a recipient, an actual benefit and a dependency. “The bell keeper makes allies stronger” is not a playable record. It could mean training, a static bonus, extra output, healing, information or an additional reaction. Choose what the support actually does before deciding whether the group contains too much of it.

Ordinary support can be concrete without numerical automation. A creature can warn an ally that a route is closing, hold a rope, move a shielded cart or negotiate for a pause. That activity occupies its actual intent and opportunity. It does not secretly grant +2 to every friendly roll, and a whispered warning cannot locate something the supporter itself cannot perceive.

**Authored review example.** An author proposes a primitive: “While you audibly count the gate's turning rhythm and an ally can hear you, add +2 to that ally's Prowess check to steady this gate.” The table must decide recipients, distance of ordinary hearing, whether multiple qualifying sources stack and its proposed acquisition price. This is not a new free primitive issued by the book. Once accepted, its condition gives both teams meaningful options: break hearing, change the gate or make the steadying task unnecessary.

If two support creatures maintain two separately accepted capabilities, their requirements accumulate on each actual maintainer. If several effects are contained in one maintained capability, that capability has one upkeep track, with the full scope still appraised. Nesting ten effects is not a technique for concealing the demand they create.

### Review burst before the first damaging expression

Burst matters when an actor concentrates an unusually strong expression in one moment. Identify who can be affected, actual dice type and count, defense scope, timing and Cost. A d10 output permission does not automatically supply ten d10 or a second Main Intent. A high chosen creature budget does not decide how many dice the normal expression contains.

The Bellhouse Custodian's normal Hammer Sweep is one target, attack +7, 1d10 Magical Metal on a hit. Structure II can support a reviewed multiple-recipient arrangement, but that is a different declared expression. Locate the recipients, describe the line and path, review the output and consequences, and choose the actual resolution before committing. Do not announce the normal single-target line, then apply it to everyone after a favorable roll.

Warn the party through observable preparation: hammer arms rising together, marked metal turning toward a route, the Custodian leaving its lift task. These tells do not necessarily reveal an exact damage number. They reveal that a choice is arriving. Players can take cover, move, interfere, secure the objective or accept the danger for a reason.

Avoid adding burst merely to compensate for a solo being outnumbered. A powerful opening that removes a new player before they understand the scene can reduce decisions sharply. Review with the actual party record. An objective threat, an explicit extra response or a position that can change may produce more useful play than an unexplained damage increase.

### Keep area effects connected to target information

An area is not automatic knowledge of its contents. A character who cannot locate a hidden target can propose affecting a known spot with actual area support. Establish the spot, dimensions, delivery and who might be there. Friendly participants and bystanders matter if the definition affects them. “Enemies only” is a recipient filter that needs its own understandable basis, not an assumption on every hostile working.

If a wall hides the room, a projected path into it may still be blocked. If a supported direct arrangement can affect a known area, the uncertainty about contents remains. A granted method such as a specifically authored scrying or “any qualifying recipient within range” can change targeting. Read the grant rather than making every high tier equivalent to omniscience.

**Example.** A party hears footsteps on the far side of a wooden screen. They can put a marked cone of a reviewed working across the doorway if they know its position and have the appropriate access. They cannot assume that the footsteps belong to all of the desired targets, or that a friend is absent. Give the players the information their senses support and let them choose whether to act on that uncertainty.

### Design an ambush without assigning a universal surprise package

Prepare what the ambushers know, what hides them and what signs remain. Broken reeds, a quiet path, an oddly placed light or a watch animal's behavior can support discovery. Do not decide that noticing any one sign prevents every danger. Nor should a failed awareness check force every character into a fixed imported condition called surprised.

There is no universal surprise mechanic in this edition. A table may choose the suggested treatment in which prepared enemies complete a round before surprised participants respond; circumstances or an agreed d20 check can change it. If you use that optional treatment, explain what made preparation meaningful and who was actually caught unaware. It is not a permanent entitlement for the side that says “we ambush.”

Consider whether the first event can be an environmental change instead of a lethal attack. A dropped barrier, a stolen key or a severed towrope can create an urgent encounter with choices. Use real permissions and opportunities. A creature cannot teleport into a concealed position merely because an ambush would be dramatic.

### Give a solo more than one practical question

The Custodian has 225 chosen BU, 56 acquired and 169 remaining. Its foundation provides maximum Vitality 113, PB 4, Physical 4, Mental 2 and Magical 3, with Magical proficient. It has two independent Reaction Slots because Second Response explicitly grants the second. It still has one coherent Main Intent by default. Neither the budget nor the word solo supplies an invisible exception.

Its Plated Resistance covers Physical Metal only. An Iron Knock from Torren deals that source/domain and is halved, rounded up; a normal roll of 5 would become 3. Mira's Canal Impact is Magical Water and is not covered by that resistance. Other declared defenses can apply only if actually present. Explain visible deflections so the party can reason about the distinction.

Design competing jobs around the creature's purpose. One actor can protect passengers, another restore a bell sequence, another talk to the guardian. Hold the Counterweight binds one arm to a continuing task. Disrupting that task can threaten passengers; making it unnecessary can free the arm without turning a rescue into a punishment. These relationships let players change what the solo needs to do.

Test the scene when the party wins an important contest early. If the guardian's task becomes unnecessary, can the encounter conclude? If you would introduce immunity to preserve three more attack rounds, revise the preparation instead. The purpose of an asymmetric creature is to create a situation, not guarantee a fixed fight duration.

### Build groups whose members have different reasons to act

Two Watchers can share mechanics without sharing judgment. One distrusts machinery; the other wants the river and settlement to cooperate. A Warden protects the gate because that is its installed purpose. Those motives can divide a group naturally. You do not need a private mind-control effect to let one enemy refuse another's plan.

With larger groups, give repetitions names or positions and choose a few readable intent patterns. Three creatures may hold a route while two move toward a cart. Each remains an independent creature. Apply its own damage, position, reactions and conditions. Do not merge Vitality into a group pool unless you deliberately author and review that exception.

Enemy count is useful because independent intents and responses add work. It does not mean the GM must play a dozen nearly identical animations. Use concise declarations and shared definitions, then resolve the actual intersections. If the record overwhelms you, reduce the number of independent participants or split the scene into connected pressures rather than hiding state in a single anonymous bar.

### Rehearse a scene by changing one important fact

Before play, test a favorable case, a counter-approach and an unexpected choice. Move the opponent away from its useful domain supply. Have the party negotiate immediately. Remove the most obvious route. Put an untrained but determined character in the key position. Can you still explain the question and the actual records?

Check whether each essential clue has another sensible route and whether a failed attempt changes the next choice. Count the distinct goals, not merely the creatures. A scene with one narrow exit and only unavoidable damage may need another opportunity. A scene with no pressure may be a useful conversation rather than a combat encounter; do not force it into rounds to justify the roster.

Write down the one flaw this rehearsal finds. “The guardian's weakness is invisible” suggests adding a tell. “The lift has no safe outcome once held” suggests a support or release route. “Every failed search stops the adventure” suggests multiple information sources. This kind of revision makes the preparation more usable without pretending a numerical simulation can predict table enjoyment.

## From an idea to an adventure someone else can run

Writing an adventure for another GM asks more of your notes than preparing for yourself. You know which facts you intended to improvise. A reader does not. Give them enough cause, location, motivation and consequence to respond to a plan you never imagined. You can remain concise without hiding the answers they need.

**Original design workshop.** This chapter develops The Lanterns Beneath the Road as an adventure outline. It is a worked writing method, not a third fully statted compulsory adventure. Use the two complete adventures elsewhere in the guide for ready-to-run examples. Any new supernatural mechanics in a finished version would need their own definitions and audit.

### Find a premise with an immediate human use

Begin with a practical disruption. “A road settlement's night lanterns have begun illuminating an old path beneath the paving” gives the group a place, a visible change and a question. Add a reason to care: a missing courier followed the light, and the regular supply wagon cannot travel safely. You do not need to decide the entire buried civilization first.

Write the premise in a sentence the players can understand without lore. Then write the cause in a different sentence for the GM: a recent repair exposed an older signaling route; its keeper is trying to summon people to a neglected inspection point. The players' first description need not reveal the cause. Your preparation should know it, so the clues do not contradict each other when discovered in a different order.

Choose what remains genuinely open. Is the keeper trustworthy? Who will maintain the older route? What should happen to the regular road? These questions can be answered through play. The cause of the lights need not change whenever a player makes a convincing guess. Responsive storytelling uses their choices without making evidence meaningless.

### Turn the premise into people and pressures

Give each important participant a desire, information, resources and a limit. The wagon owner wants a safe route and knows where the lights begin, but cannot excavate alone. The road mason wants the repair accepted and remembers which stone shifted, but fears liability. The old keeper wants the inspection restored and can guide people through the route, but does not know how the settlement has changed.

Those facts produce negotiations. The owner can lend a wagon. The mason can identify a hatch. The keeper can describe the actual mechanism. No one needs to carry the complete explanation in one monologue. Nor should everyone lie until a player rolls sufficiently high. A person with partial knowledge can speak truthfully and still leave a useful question.

Pressure comes from what happens if nobody acts: another wagon reaches the unsafe paving, the courier remains unaccounted for, and a damaged lantern housing loses its signal. Describe a warning before each irreversible change when circumstances allow. These are authored scene developments, not compulsory countdown stages or a universal cost for waiting.

### Write conclusions the players need, then their evidence

Decide which conclusions are necessary for the immediate problem. The lights follow a route; the route begins at a repair hatch; the courier reached an inspection chamber; closing the hatch without checking the mechanism could trap them. Each conclusion needs concrete evidence. “There is a mystery here” is not enough to make the next decision.

| Necessary conclusion | Evidence through observation | Evidence through people or records |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The light follows a path | Marks appear in an ordered line under loose paving | An old route sketch uses matching marks |
| The new repair exposed its start | Fresh mortar stops beside a shifted stone | The mason remembers lifting that stone |
| The courier followed it | A broken lantern handle and identifiable footprints enter the hatch | A witness saw the courier crouch there |
| The inspection chamber has a second access | Air and distant sound reach a side opening | The keeper's maintenance record shows a service route |

The alternative sources need not provide identical detail. Footprints suggest a route; a witness gives a time; a sketch identifies a second access. A player can combine them. If one source is destroyed, the situation remains understandable through another sensible approach. Optional secrets can still require effort and may remain undiscovered.

Checks can address risk, incomplete material or an uncertain interpretation. They should not suppress facts someone can plainly observe. A soaked diagram can be preserved through Ilex's actual Paper working at Touch, but that working does not restore nonexistent words or verify the keeper's honesty. Distinguish the physical clue from the conclusion drawn from it.

### Connect places through routes the players can choose

The outline needs only four working locations: the road's lantern line, the repair hatch, the inspection chamber and the side service opening. Connect them through known or discoverable routes. The hatch is direct but narrow. The side opening is longer but offers a route for bulky equipment. A surface detour reaches the settlement without using either underground path.

Give useful distances and barriers. Is the chamber 30 ft below the hatch or 300? Can a person at the top locate someone below by hearing? Does a bend block a projected path? Can a rope reach? Which sections are stable enough for a cart? You need these answers before a mobility or sensing primitive becomes relevant.

A map should be legible to a person who has not read every paragraph. Label scale, access, level changes, barriers, objective locations and important resources. Put the encounter's main question beside the map rather than relying on a decorative compass rose. If the map omits a passage mentioned in the prose, the omission is a usability fault, not a mysterious feature.

### Make the first description actionable in several directions

Write an opening that shows the situation and invites a decision. “Three lanterns shine through cracks in the road. A wagon has stopped beside fresh mortar. The mason holds a broken brass cover, and someone calls that a courier is missing.” Readers can paraphrase this without performing a page of narration.

Immediate options are to inspect, ask, protect traffic, follow the route or search for the courier. Do not put the adventure's only starting clue in a successful private check. Give the GM what each obvious approach can reveal. They can then respond confidently when a player asks an unexpected ordinary question about the mortar, lantern or wagon.

Avoid using an unavoidable lethal hazard to force participation. A visible road problem can be compelling without killing a named ally if the group hesitates. If the tone includes harsh time pressure, agree that tone and describe the actual signs. The players should know enough to choose intervention, delegation or withdrawal.

### Prepare outcomes as changes rather than ending labels

Write what a few broad results would change. Restoring the inspection route makes traffic safer but creates a maintenance obligation. Closing it after rescuing the courier leaves the old keeper without a route. Moving regular traffic creates a dispute about land and trade. Leaving changes who responds and what they can accomplish. These are consequences of choices, not four mandatory endings selected from a menu.

Include the cost of a seemingly successful plan when it is predictable. Excavation can damage the surface road; a temporary closure can delay supplies; a public accusation can affect the mason's standing. Establish those stakes before a player commits when their character could understand them. Do not reward ingenuity with an unrelated concealed punishment.

If a player finds a credible way to preserve several aims, let the situation support it. The workshop is not built around a required moral sacrifice. A good dilemma invites thought; it does not need to protect your favorite tragic ending from a better plan.

### Write encounters as reusable situation records

Each location can have a short encounter record: visible situation, pressure, goals, people, routes, facts, ordinary resources and any actual creature definitions. If combat is plausible, give the opponents' motives and conditions for stopping. If no creature is needed, do not insert one simply to use a budget table.

For a mechanical guardian, you may adapt a published complete record only where its mechanics make sense. Reusing a Canal Watcher in a dry lantern chamber would require a useful supply of existing water or different reviewed definitions. A story reskin does not convert Water I into Light I. If you create a new mechanic, reproduce it offline with actual pins and rights for digital use.

Party budgets can be reference values while writing. Label them as editable examples, never balanced guarantees. Identify what makes the scene demanding: a narrow approach, limited information, incompatible objectives or concentrated output. A reader can adjust those facts along with the roster, rather than assuming that changing a 100 to a 75 corrects every problem.

### Prepare a handout that a player can actually use

A handout should answer a question or support a choice. A rough route sketch can show a service opening and missing section. A maintenance receipt can identify a replaced plate. A witness note can name the time the courier entered. Include enough plain text for accessibility and for a GM who cannot print color artwork.

Distinguish what is known from interpretation. A stamped date is evidence of that stamp, not proof nobody altered the object. If the document is intentionally misleading, explain its cause and the discoverable mismatch in the GM notes. Do not make every written clue unreliable; players need a reason to investigate rather than abandon all inference.

Keep the handout physically usable. Short readable lines, sufficient contrast, labels and blank margins are often more helpful than simulated staining across every word. An atmospheric fragment can accompany a clean transcription. The art should increase comprehension, not make a simple route impossible to read at the table.

### Review the adventure in the order a reader might use it

Read the opening without looking ahead. Can you explain what the characters perceive? Pick the least expected obvious option and follow its information. Check that the reader can find the relevant person, location or creature without a long search. Then imagine the group learning the cause early. Does the situation still contain choices?

Check an unsuccessful approach. If they fail to interpret the sketch, can they ask the mason, inspect the hatch or find another route? Check departure. Does the world have an understandable response that does not punish the group for failing to follow your plot? Check a direct extraordinary approach against actual permission and targeting.

This is an editorial rehearsal, not a human playtest. You still need people to run the adventure to discover unclear instructions, pacing and unexpected assumptions. Record what the rehearsal covered and what it did not. A polished layout or a valid budget does not prove that the scene teaches itself to a new table.

### Package the work so its mechanics remain open and usable

Put the adventure's required rules and definitions where a reader can use them. Link a platform collection as a convenience, but include the offline record needed for play. Identify which version belongs to the adventure. A later public template change should not silently alter the edition in someone's printed book.

Separate compatible mechanical material from any protected setting text, map, artwork or plot under the publication's explicit rights notices. SwordWeave's open mechanical framework can support someone else's commercial work with attribution under the applicable license. Access to an official image or a purchase of a PDF does not by itself define every reuse right. A clear notice helps both the creator and the reader.

Keep download and print preparation distinct. An attractive digital edition is not automatically ready for every printer. A physical product requires the chosen trim, bleed, binding gutter, image resolution, color handling and a reviewed manufactured proof. Do not write press-ready on a file solely because it exported without an error.

## Price an idea by reading what it changes

The Library's reference prices provide a common conversation, not a compulsory tariff for every authored rule. A new primitive needs a readable behavior before it can have a useful price. A very high number cannot make an ambiguous permission fair, and a low number is not automatically wrong for a narrow rule that contributes little to the campaign.

This workshop examines proposals at the level where they affect play. The prices below are **authoring examples for table review**, not newly issued canonical entries. When a comparison uses a published reference anchor, it says so. Acquisition, equipment and execution remain separate throughout.

:::figure composition

### Begin a review with a testable sentence

Ask the author to describe a normal use and a limit. “I want to recognize damaged structures” could support a practice bonus, a sense, expertise or an information-producing working. Those proposals have different effects. Start with the experience they want rather than guessing a large package from the title.

Use a plain first sentence: “Add +2 to Reason checks to identify the cause of visible damage to a manufactured structure after inspecting it at ordinary reach.” This gives the operation, recipient, check, purpose and condition. It does not identify damage through walls, locate hidden traps automatically or improve attacks against the structure. The author can decide whether those limits match the concept.

Now test the sentence. Does it help with an old crack under clear inspection? Yes, if a check is actually needed. Does it reconstruct a missing blueprint? No. Does inspecting one wall qualify every structure in the city? No. Would an ordinary inspection already make the answer certain? Possibly; then the bonus does not create a reason to roll. These tests reveal practical scope before price.

### Compare a check modifier with a changed attribute

Ilex has Mental 5 and PB 2 from Mental proficiency. Their Knowledge, Reason, Awareness and Influence checks are each +7. Their selected Mental DC is 12. A proposed +1 only to Knowledge makes that check +8 while the others and DC remain unchanged. A proposed +1 Mental at an accepted attribute scope makes Mental 6 and its trained practices +8, and selected DC 13 before other modifiers.

These two proposals are not interchangeable. The attribute also contributes to formulas and saves that actually use its current value. A practice-specific bonus does not. Compare their breadth and stacking before accepting equal prices simply because both say +1. Respect actual caps and permitted operations in the construction; this example does not waive those requirements.

**Example review response.** “Your 4-BU proposal affects one kind of Knowledge task. That seems understandable here. If you want all Mental uses to increase, write an attribute rule instead and review that wider effect.” The response identifies a choice. It does not claim that 4 is the universal correct price for all knowledge or that the table must accept a proposed attribute increase.

### Distinguish training from repeated numerical benefit

A proficiency grant establishes the trained state at its stated scope. The chosen proficient attribute already trains its practices and save. Granting the same proficiency again does not repeatedly add PB. An explicit expertise grant, a direct modifier and advantage are different contributions with their own rules.

Torren has Physical-trained Finesse +7. Steady Hands adds +2 during its qualifying rope task, making +9. A second capability referencing that very same acquired occurrence of Steady Hands does not add +2 again. It is another route to an existing acquisition, not another purchased occurrence. A genuinely separate acquired contribution must qualify and obey its authored stacking or uniqueness rules.

Suppose two sources grant advantage and one grants disadvantage on that +9 rope check. Cancel opposing stacks, leaving one advantage. Roll two d20, keep the higher and add +9 once. With three advantages and one disadvantage, two remain: roll three d20, keep the highest and add +9 once. Bias changes the resolution dice selected; it does not turn +9 into +18 or supply training where none exists.

### Review an apparently harmless permanence clause

“The bridge stays repaired” contains more than a duration. It may imply new material, structural strength, indefinite resistance to weather or an information claim that the work is safe. Ask what was actually changed. Ordinary repair can produce a lasting ordinary bridge. A supernatural result can persist if its definition supports that outcome; permanent does not mean indestructible.

A proposed water support is different. Mira reshapes existing water, and this example's support needs continued direction. Renaming it a permanent support does not create stone or make the canal freeze forever. If the author wants a new structural material, review the required behavior and subject. If they want an ordinary repaired support using tools, those ordinary acts remain available without buying every workshop step.

Test interference and ending. Can the bridge be struck? Does removing its anchor matter? Does releasing a maintained support remove the support immediately or according to a stated ending? A clear lasting result can be easier to manage than an undefined permanent enchantment with every possible immunity implied.

### Review the phrase any within range

An authored targeting method can change the usual specific-target requirement, but it needs a practical meaning. “Any fish within range” asks how qualifying recipients are identified, what happens to hidden or unknown fish, and which behavior applies. Range alone does not answer those questions. “Any” is not a free omniscience clause attached to every high-tier working.

Perhaps the proposal means a visible shoal the actor can locate. Perhaps it means a specialized influence on every eligible fish within a defined water volume, using an expressly granted method. Those versions differ in knowledge and discrimination. Write whether the actor learns locations or merely changes the eligible area. An effect that influences a region need not automatically report every recipient by name.

**Example limit.** A reviewed weather working affects an agreed valley without personally locating every raindrop. It still needs the actual domain, behavior, scope, recipients and consequences. It does not make every individual-target attack legal through a mountain. Scope-specific exceptions should be described at that scope.

### Ask what a restriction costs the author in practice

A condition that says “only at night” matters differently in a campaign about midnight journeys from one set mostly in daylight. A bonus “only while wearing the coat I always wear” may provide little practical restriction. A lost ability “until the broken bell is repaired” can be severe when the bell is inaccessible. Review the fiction and expected use with the player.

There is no compulsory percentage discount for a condition. Conditions are part of the authored proposal. You may accept the price, ask for a different price or change the scope. Avoid treating the player as an adversary for wanting a concept that is convenient. Your shared goal is a readable rule that supports the agreed campaign.

Test whether the condition can be observed and voluntarily managed. “While calm” may require a subjective argument every round. “After a protected pause to prepare the instrument, until its string breaks or it is put away” gives actual events. That can be a useful revision even if the acquisition price remains the same.

### Review a mirror without rewarding a fictional inconvenience twice

An eligible mirrored drawback is an actual acquired rule with its scope, conditions and restrictions. A character's Water working requiring existing water is already limited by its definition. That limitation does not automatically become additional mirror credit. If a player wants a separate accepted drawback, write what it changes beyond the underlying permission.

At level 1, suppose positive acquisitions cost 27 and an accepted eligible personal drawback gives 2 credit. Net spent is 25. Actual used debt is 2, within the level 1 ceiling 4, and positive acquisitions remain below the next-level creation ceiling 35. The debt ceiling is a limit, not an automatic gift of 4. If the drawback only provides 2, it cannot fund 31 positive BU as if all 4 appeared by default.

Ask what the drawback means when it is inconvenient. If a rule revokes flight from its bearer until a stated task is completed, an ally gaining flight does not suppress that loss. If two records refer to the same drawback, their references do not double its credit. If a chosen operation has no automatic opposite, do not invent one from the title. Set To has no automatic mirrored inverse.

### Keep an item proposal in its actual ledger

A fixed-hook brace can have Item BU 4 for its conditional +2 Prowess contribution. A creature carrying it still uses its chosen base creature budget for encounter appraisal. Equipping it makes its qualified contribution available and consumes its stated slots. Carrying it while unequipped still contributes its Load but not the equipped conditional bonus.

For two Sluice Wardens, the chosen creature total is 2 × 100 = 200. The two braces add 2 × 4 = 8 Item BU. Combined enemy is 208. Their acquired creature total is 2 × 52 = 104, which describes their builds rather than replacing the appraisal's chosen 200. Do not put the equipment into 208 creature BU and then add it again for a fabricated 216 total.

An ordinary item can have Item BU 0 and meaningful setting value. A rare brass key might be priceless to the characters but have no extraordinary acquired rule. The availability of a device, its money price, its burden, its final equipped slots and its Item BU answer different questions. A single large number cannot communicate all of them.

### Reuse a component without multiplying ownership

Mira's River Hand references Guided Water, an effect containing Verb II, Water I and Near. Canal Impact reuses those acquisitions and also references d8. The four distinct acquisitions total 20. Adding her 4-BU Knowledge proficiency gives 24 spent. Arranging them in two capabilities does not cost 40 before Knowledge. The ledger counts actual occurrences, not every route by which the interface reaches one occurrence.

Now imagine an author deliberately buys a second permitted occurrence of a static modifier. That is a different ownership question. Verify its identity, stacking, uniqueness and actual accounting. A second card with the same acquisition identifier is not proof of a second purchase. Conversely, do not automatically erase a genuinely separate acquisition merely because its rule has similar text.

In a paper record, give acquired occurrences short identifiers and let compositions reference them. “W1: Water I, 4 BU” can appear under both capabilities. This simple practice makes ownership visible without requiring an account. It also exposes accidental duplicate charges before someone changes the character to compensate for them.

### Test the ordinary, stretched and impossible expressions

For each accepted creation, write three uses. One should be a normal expression, one should stretch its scale or circumstances while retaining its actual behavior, and one should be outside its access. The author and DM can explain the boundary together. This is a review tool, not a requirement to annotate every action during play.

For Water I and Verb II, redirecting a cup is ordinary. Redirecting a surging channel before a rescue is a demanding but related expression. Creating a new living body from water is outside the stated grounded water reshaping permission. A large execution Cost can appraise the channel; it does not buy Life or a new verb on the fly without an actual agreed construction or bounded exception.

For Torren's Iron Knock, striking a located target through existing metal at Touch is normal. Using it while hanging one-handed over a drop may be demanding or incompatible with its needed opportunity. Reshaping a lock into a key is outside its Verb I expression. A title that suggests strength does not convert a direct force action into every alteration of metal.

### Finish a review with a usable decision

Accept, revise or postpone the actual proposal. Write one sentence explaining the reason and the agreed version. “Accepted at 4 BU for checks identifying visible structural damage after close inspection; no remote detection” tells the player what they own. “Needs another review” without a question tells them very little.

When you postpone, offer a playable narrower version if possible. The player may begin with a specific sense or check benefit and develop the wider concept later. Do not promise the future version automatically, but do not leave them unable to play their character while you discuss every theoretical edge case.

Record exceptional rulings separately from permanent acquisitions. A desperate one-time rescue can be allowed with agreed consequences without becoming a new always-available capability. A permanent accepted mechanic belongs in the authored record and ledger. That difference prevents both accidental free upgrades and surprise revocation of something the player thought they had purchased.

## Give consequences a shape people can live with

A consequence is useful when the player understands its actual change and can decide what to do next. It can be numerical, restrictive, environmental or narrative. It can be severe without dealing damage, and it can be gentle without being meaningless. The cause, timing and ending make it playable.

The cases in this chapter are **optional contextual agreements**. They illustrate how to write and administer consequences. They do not create a compulsory injury list, resource pool, exhaustion ladder or fixed price for improvisation.

:::figure consequence

### Separate the attempted price from the uncertain result

Before commitment, explain whether Cost applies when the attempt begins, when it succeeds, when it continues or after a stated event. Explain what failure means as a separate clause. “You accept aching hands to hold the rope; the check determines whether the worker reaches the landing” differs from “on a failed check, the rope burns your hands and the worker remains exposed.”

Neither arrangement is universally required. The scene and proposal decide. What matters is that the player can understand the trade-off before choosing. A failed roll does not automatically refund a paid Cost, and it does not automatically demand all possible Costs. There is no universal interruption refund.

**Example conversation.** A character wants to brace a heavy beam immediately. The table offers a stated shoulder restriction that applies if they make the sudden lift, whether the following secure-support check succeeds or not. The player proposes a lever instead. That changes the approach and may change the agreed restriction or uncertainty. The negotiation is part of play, not an attempt to evade an undisclosed tariff.

### Write an injury without importing a condition package

“Strained shoulder” can be purely descriptive or have an actual rule. Decide which. A clear restrictive version is: “Until treated and an agreed rest has occurred, you cannot carry another person without reviewing further effort and consequence.” A numerical version might be a stated penalty to a bounded class of checks. These have different effects; do not switch between them whenever one becomes inconvenient.

Record whether an extraordinary permission remains available. An injured shoulder might affect lifting but not every Magical check or all walking. If it does affect a capability's required motion, say so. Names like blinded, stunned or poisoned do not import another game's complete mechanics. Write the actual lost sense, action restriction, numerical change and ending that this consequence uses.

Healing current Vitality does not automatically clear the shoulder restriction. If the agreed remedy is treatment and rest, follow that remedy. If a healing working explicitly treats the injury within its access, resolve that actual behavior. A die permission by itself is not a healing construction, and recovering points is not automatically repairing every narrative harm.

### Make loss of access bounded and visible

Temporary loss of access can be a meaningful consequence when it has a cause and return condition. A character overextends a working and agrees that the affected capability cannot be used until they repair its focus. Write what is affected: the one capability, its particular delivery method or an actual domain permission. These are different restrictions.

Do not broaden the restriction to every related primitive without agreement. If a focus supports a single named capability, losing it does not automatically delete every acquisition beneath the character's heritage. Conversely, if the focus is the actual source of a broad granted permission, losing that source can matter at its stated scope. The record should show the relationship.

Give a reachable recovery route where the fiction supports it. Replacing a string, finding a witness or making a protected preparation can create useful play. An indefinite “until the DM says so” restriction makes planning difficult. The creator's direction allows contextual judgment; it does not require obscuring every ending condition.

### Let a narrative cost change a relationship

A character may accept public exposure, an obligation or damage to a place. Write who learns what and what the recipient can reasonably do about it. “The lock keeper saw your forbidden working and expects an explanation before lending tools again” is a usable relationship. “Everyone hates you now” is an undefined global change unless the scene actually supports it and the table agreed that scope.

A promise needs terms. Name the promised task, the beneficiary, the expected time or circumstance and what fulfillment looks like. An obligation to repair a damaged landing is not an automatic prohibition on every other adventure. The group can negotiate a deadline, delegate with consent or face an understandable consequence of failing to keep the promise.

Separate moral judgment from bookkeeping. A public accusation can be justified and still change a relationship. A successful rescue can create gratitude without erasing the cost accepted to perform it. The world can have complicated responses without the GM using them to punish a player for solving a problem creatively.

### Administer upkeep at the agreed maintenance point

Upkeep is an ongoing requirement of a maintained capability. Council is a useful agreed continuation point within the shared rhythm. Record the requirement, the capability, the next point and the ending terms. Do not silently charge a standing requirement several times because its capability contains several effects.

Mira's directed lane can require continued directing. The Custodian's Hold the Counterweight binds one arm. Another reviewed capability might require an ongoing audible chant, a resource contribution or a stated numerical penalty. None of those automatically become Vitality damage. Several maintained capabilities can coexist when their actual requirements permit it; the requirements accumulate rather than imposing an imported universal concentration slot.

When the player cannot meet a requirement, explain the timing of the resulting change. If a chant must remain audible and the actor becomes unable to vocalize, continuation is threatened by a real circumstance. If a restriction ends when the repair is complete, completing the repair matters. A new round alone does not erase a consequence whose stated condition persists.

### Compare continuation with an increased instant expression

A character can ask to hold a modest effect over time instead of forcing a much greater result immediately. That often distributes effort or makes the initial demand smaller. There is no mandatory discount percentage. Compare the actual work: an ongoing narrow diversion versus moving the entire surge at once, or a steadily supported lift versus a sudden violent displacement.

Name what makes the continuing option manageable and what it commits. A narrow diversion may preserve hands or access for other ordinary tasks if the agreement permits, or require continuous attention that prevents a separate use of that same working. An instant increase may incur a severe contextual consequence but leave no maintained task afterward. The player can compare real choices.

Do not let nesting hide the scale. One capability with a water lane, a lighting effect and an unrelated defensive grant still has the full combined demand to appraise. One upkeep track means one organized maintenance record, not one tiny price no matter what is packed inside it.

### Preserve the one expressly pending upkeep question

**Alpha rule pending.** Additional Vitality upkeep repayment triggered by incoming damage, including threshold, counter reset and treatment of excess damage, remains undecided. This book does not resolve or impose that numerical scheme. Do not derive it from these examples or automate it because an older guide described an additional payment.

If a table needs a temporary treatment, agree it explicitly and label it as their choice. Ordinary continuation upkeep, restrictive requirements, narrative consequences and contextual interference remain usable. A hit is not a universal cancellation rule. The unresolved numerical detail does not make every sustained capability unusable.

Keep the pending decision in the rules register when revising a creation or writing an adventure. Do not conceal it by replacing all upkeep with damage, or by asserting that damage never matters. Damage can matter through the actual injury or interference in the scene. The specific additional-payment formula is what remains open.

### Offer a recovery scene rather than an automatic reset

A short rest is a short pause, perhaps 15 or 30 minutes. A long rest is overnight sleep or equivalent rest, not necessarily eight hours. The group agrees whether the actual circumstances earn either effect. Safe shelter, interrupted sleep, ongoing work and immediate danger can matter. Elapsed time alone does not apply recovery.

Before the pause, explain any proposed adjustment. “The shelter is noisy and you must take turns watching; we can agree a partial overnight recovery” lets the players choose their arrangements. A later surprise reduction after they already relied on full recovery can break trust. The creator's example permits a 70% recovery after a poor night when agreed; it is an illustration, not a compulsory five-hour formula.

At maximum Vitality 13, ordinary short-rest allowance is ceil(13/2), which is 7. At 10/13, using 3 reaches full and leaves 4. If later damage leaves 8/13, the remaining 4 can restore to 12, not 13. A completed agreed long rest refreshes the allowance; long-rest healing is not charged against it. Ordinary rounding is upward, but you do not add fractional points to a healing ceiling the record cannot hold.

### Keep rescue urgency separate from a timer puzzle

At zero Vitality a character collapses. Explain the immediate danger and the contextual rescue countdown; roughly a minute is guidance, not ten guaranteed safe rounds. A drifting body, inaccessible platform or dangerous current can make location and assistance important. Do not keep a player uncertain whether their character is dying while everyone argues over unrelated bookkeeping.

Stabilization needs a dedicated action track, suitable Fieldcraft or actual primitives, and agreed access, resolution and Cost. There is no universal stabilization DC in the shared rules. Success stops the rescue countdown without restoring Vitality or consciousness. Healing above zero normally ends dying and wakes the character unless another actual consequence prevents it.

An active dying character needs rescue before assisted rest. A stabilized character can recover through an agreed assisted rest. Distinguish those states visibly. Massive damage can be outright lethal under its actual rule; use final post-defense damage and current Vitality before clamping a display at zero. Do not turn a zero-clamped number into evidence that every extreme blow caused the same outcome.

### Revise a consequence when its meaning proves unclear

If a record says “exhausted” and people disagree about its effect, pause to clarify the original intent. Agree a specific rule and ending. Do not silently choose the broadest possible restriction in the middle of a costly action. If the ambiguity has already affected choices, acknowledge that and make the transition understandable.

Keep a short revision note: previous wording, agreed new wording, who is affected and when it applies. A lighter clarification does not necessarily refund a past Cost; a harsher rule should not be applied retroactively without discussion. The aim is a usable shared record, not a legal argument about who first chose an imprecise word.

At the end of a scene, ask what remains. Current Vitality, used short-rest allowance, obligations, lost access and environmental damage have different endings. A concise review helps players feel the continuity of their decisions. It also prevents a later session from inheriting either an accidental clean slate or a list of penalties nobody remembers agreeing to.

## Run places, conversations and journeys with confidence

Not every useful scene needs an enemy roster. Exploration can ask where to go, what to learn and what to risk. Conversation can ask whose cooperation is possible and what changes their judgment. A journey can give the group decisions about routes, relationships and resources without becoming a mandatory ration simulation.

The methods here are **optional GM practice**. Use as much structure as your table needs. Do not turn a preparation aid into a new core subsystem unless the group deliberately wants that procedure and understands its terms.

:::art camp

### Describe a place in the order people can act

Start with orientation: entrances, visible people, obvious danger and useful features. Then offer distinguishing details the characters would notice. In an archive, the nearest desk, the dripping roof and a keeper protecting a box are more immediately useful than a paragraph about the stone's age. You can add atmosphere while the players choose where to look.

Answer ordinary questions directly when the facts are evident. A character can tell whether a door is open, a rope is within reach or a worker can hear a loud voice. An uncertain structural weakness, hidden compartment or disputed interpretation can require an approach and perhaps a roll. Avoid making the group buy information about every object through repeated awareness checks.

When a player asks for a detail you did not prepare, choose a plausible answer and preserve it. A corridor either has a window or does not. Once a plan relies on that window, changing it to protect an intended challenge would invalidate the conversation. You can discover complications through play without rewriting established facts whenever an idea works well.

### Make incomplete information useful instead of useless

Give an actionable fragment. A witness knows where the courier went, but not why. An old map shows the route, but not its present condition. A granted sense locates vibrations through a surface but does not name their source. Each clue supports a next choice while leaving uncertainty.

State what is observed and what is inferred. “You hear two distinct footfalls behind the screen” differs from “there are exactly two guards and no one else.” Players can choose to inspect, speak, retreat or use a supported area expression. They do not need certainty to make a plan, but they need an honest account of what their characters know.

If information is essential for progress, prepare more than one route to it. If it is an optional advantage, it may reasonably remain hidden. This distinction lets failure matter without making the entire adventure dependent on one die. A failed interpretation can change time, expose a mistaken assumption or encourage another source, following the agreed stakes.

### Treat a negotiation as changing proposals

Write the listener's aim, concern and available authority. A keeper can lend a key but cannot promise that an entire council will approve. A worker can describe a fault but may not be able to command a crew. A guardian may accept a safe diversion but reject an unsupported assurance. These limits make the conversation concrete.

Let players make an actual offer. Evidence, a witness, a practical compromise or a shared risk can change the answer. Select Influence when uncertainty about that approach matters. Do not require a sequence of rolls merely to continue speaking, and do not make a high number force every requested concession regardless of motive or authority.

**Example exchange.** The party asks the keeper to release a lift before the records are repaired. She fears people will blame her if it fails. They propose a supervised release with a signed repair obligation and a worker on the lower landing. That addresses her actual concern. The DM can accept the secure arrangement or resolve remaining uncertainty. An unrelated threat would create a different conversation and consequence.

### Keep a lie distinct from missing knowledge

If an NPC lies, know why and what evidence can expose it. If they are mistaken, know what they believe. If they simply lack information, let them say so. Treating every uncertain answer as deceit makes ordinary inquiry feel pointless and gives the GM too much freedom to revise truth afterward.

A check can address reading demeanor or comparing claims within the actual approach. It does not automatically reveal every concealed thought. A capability that reads memory or detects a specifically defined falsehood needs its actual subject, behavior, targets, resolution and limits. Its title cannot grant universal truth detection.

Use contradictions that matter. A ledger date differs from a witness's recollection; a claimed repair uses material not present at the site. Players can investigate the cause. A tiny irrelevant inconsistency may provide atmosphere, but it should not always turn into an ambush designed to punish reasonable suspicion.

### Share spotlight without rationing every sentence

Ask quiet participants a concrete question about the current situation: what does their character inspect, whom do they trust, or which approach do they support? Give them time to answer. A confident speaker can help explain a plan without deciding every other character's intent. During Council, hear each coherent intent before resolving intersections.

Use the party's varied ordinary and acquired skills. Ilex can compare records, Torren can judge a repair route and Mira can assess available water. Do not insist that each person uses their most expensive capability in every scene. A player's clever ordinary observation can be valuable even when their character has no purchased mechanic for that exact topic.

When people split up, cut between meaningful decisions rather than every footstep. Keep the others' next choices visible. Agree how much real time a solo conversation should occupy. These are table-management choices, not a mechanical action tax on exploration or a reason to deny a character an ordinary private conversation.

### Give journeys decisions at a useful scale

Choose what a route decision covers: a morning, a dangerous crossing or a whole ordinary stretch. Describe what distinguishes alternatives. A ridge route has visibility but exposure; a river route has water and trade but damaged locks; a sheltered road is slower but permits rest. Those differences should matter through actual events, not secretly predetermined failure on every unchosen route.

Track only resources the group has agreed are relevant. If ordinary supplies matter, state what is consumed, who has them and how replenishment works. If the campaign abstracts routine provisioning, do not suddenly demand a detailed ration count after several sessions. Neither approach is universal SwordWeave law.

A new movement mode can change routes within its actual grant. Flight might cross a gap but not carry a wagon or navigate unknown weather without support. Burrowing might pass through eligible material but not reveal every underground destination. Greater range can affect a remote known subject but does not supply sight. Review the actual travel problem, not just the most impressive word on the sheet.

### Make weather a circumstance people can plan around

Rain can obscure a route, swell a ford or make a shelter uncomfortable. Describe those facts and warning signs. Do not attach an all-purpose disadvantage stack to everyone merely because the scene says storm. A particular slippery grip or uncertain sight can affect a particular approach through the agreed circumstances.

A character can propose ordinary precautions: cover documents, anchor rope, wait for a gust or choose a sheltered path. Let those acts change the question. A weather-working needs its actual access and regional scope; influencing a storm does not automatically clear every personal consequence or repair a damaged road.

If weather pressures a rest, explain that before the group settles. They can find better shelter, assign ordinary watch duties or accept an agreed partial recovery. The presence of rain is not an automatic denial of all rest, and a convenient shelter is not secretly unsafe solely because you prepared an interruption encounter.

### Let a campaign remember people rather than only milestones

Keep a small relationship record. For each recurring person, note what happened, what they believe about it and what they want now. Ressa saw a careful repair; the keeper received a promise; one Watcher saw violence while another saw a rescue. Their different knowledge can produce different future responses without a compulsory favor score.

Record what changed in places too. A bridge is usable but still needs a brace. A service door is open. A bell sequence is restored while its charter remains disputed. Returning to a place can reveal those consequences. Do not restore every scene to its original state just so another party can face the same planned obstacle.

Choose a few active developments between sessions. A crew may gather materials, a witness may travel or a guardian may relocate a bed. Give the group signs of changes relevant to their next choices. You do not need to simulate every faction daily or make all unattended problems immediately become disasters.

### Negotiate growth before it changes the next scene

Explain the campaign's advancement agreement and review proposed acquisitions before someone relies on them. A new ingredient can be invented or selected with table approval. Spending acquisitions does not reduce progression. Equipment rewards need their own Item BU and ordinary record. Money, social standing and progression can all matter without being the same resource.

If a player wants to reorganize a heritage, preserve actual acquired occurrences and their references. Moving an ingredient from Upbringing to Manifest does not automatically charge again, remove it or increase its strength. If the new definition changes mechanics, review the change, version and accounting explicitly.

At high budgets, keep the same clarity. Bigger scenes may require more recipient, domain and dependency decisions, but a high level is not permission to skip access. Explain cumulative advancement thresholds accurately rather than inventing a simple replacement from memory. Invite the player to describe what the new purchase lets them do that they could not do before.

### Adjust tone when the group's interests change

A campaign may begin with practical rescue and grow into political intrigue or strange exploration. Discuss that shift. Existing characters can remain useful through ordinary competence, changed relationships and reviewed acquisitions. Do not force a mandatory rebuild because you have become interested in a different genre.

Revisit boundaries as well as mechanics. A plot about debts and public promises can become uncomfortable if it unexpectedly turns into coercion or humiliation. Give the group a way to pause, redirect or remove material without demanding personal explanations. A safety discussion is not a character weakness and never earns or costs BU.

Ask what players want more and less of. Their answers can guide the next situation without granting them automatic success or giving away every secret. Your enjoyment matters too. A sustainable campaign gives everyone useful choices and a reasonable preparation burden.

## Keep a small, reliable record at the table

You do not need to write a transcript to preserve an encounter. You need the few facts that affect the next decision. Separate a stable definition from changing state, and separate a player-facing warning from information the characters do not yet have. Paper cards, a notebook or the platform can all support that work.

The tools below are **optional recording formats**. Complete the fields that serve your group. None creates an extra action economy, fixed damage timer, compulsory condition package or hidden difficulty calculation.

:::art tools

### A stance can summarize behavior without granting a bonus

When many participants act, a short behavioral phrase can help: guarding the bed, withdrawing toward cover, bracing the gate, bargaining for a witness. Call it a stance in your notes if useful. It summarizes an intention and visible posture. It is not a universal mechanic with an automatic modifier, defense or action.

Distinguish that summary from an actual authored conditional rule. The Warden's bracing is relevant because Braced Resistance has a precise qualification and because its brace item supports a bounded check. A different creature saying “defensive stance” receives neither benefit by default. A player can understand the posture and propose an approach without needing to memorize invented stance packages.

When the stance changes, explain the observation. The guardian leaves the gate; the keeper lowers a weapon; the ambusher backs toward the side door. That can change sight, paths, conditions and intent. Apply the actual facts, not an assumed all-purpose advantage from a posture label.

### Keep a declaration line for each active participant

| Record field | A usable entry |
| --- | --- |
| Desired result | Draw the timber to the landing without releasing it downstream |
| Approach | Secure tether, guide the lane, then pull along the board |
| Opportunity and access | At the timber; ordinary rope; Mira's located Water working within 30 ft |
| Placement | Measured because the pull depends on setup and route |
| Chosen uncertainty | Prowess DC 14 for the actual caught load |
| Stated failure | Timber remains caught; revised route needed |
| Cost and timing | Only the separately agreed requirements; no hidden damage bill |
| Completion | Mark resolved when the declared pull has been answered |

You can speak the line instead of writing every field. Use more detail where ambiguity or stakes justify it. A secure ordinary door opening may need only a description. A desperate rescue over a closing gap benefits from clearer opportunity, timing and consequence.

### Record objective advancement as changed facts

“Timber tethered,” “safe downstream route verified,” and “bridge arm freed” are useful changes. They show what later tasks can use. A progress counter can be an optional summary if the table wants one, but it should correspond to actual established facts. Do not require filling three boxes if a coherent plan has already solved the situation.

Distinguish progress from an irreversible outcome. A tether enables a pull; it does not guarantee the bridge will lower. Negotiating with one guardian changes that guardian's behavior; it does not mind-control the other. A repaired mechanism can resolve the practical problem while leaving a political disagreement. Keeping those facts separate makes outcomes richer without requiring more rolls.

If progress stalls, identify the actual uncertainty or missing ingredient. The group may need a tool, information, support or a different approach. If there is no meaningful uncertainty left, resolve the secure work. Do not keep a scene open merely because everyone has not used their preferred attack.

### Use a current-state line that fits on one card

For a repeated creature, write name, pinned definition, current/maximum Vitality, location, movement used, reaction slots remaining, current intent and any ongoing requirement. Add a consequence with its ending only when it exists. The Watchers share a definition but keep separate lines. Watcher A at 13/13 guarding the bed is not interchangeable with Watcher B at 7/13 negotiating on the bank.

When the record changes, say why. Damage from a resolved hit changes current Vitality. A moved creature changes location and used movement. A spent response changes availability. Advancing a phase merely changes the organizing marker. It does not heal, damage, end a consequence or apply another person's sheet edit.

Keep maximum and current values visibly separate. A new maximum from a reviewed build change is not automatic healing. A carried item being unequipped frees its slots but not its Load. These distinctions prevent a compact record from becoming inaccurate simply because only one number is shown.

### Prepare a pause summary for interrupted sessions

End mid-scene with a concise snapshot: round and phase, unresolved intents, locations, movement used, reaction availability, current Vitality, consequences, maintained requirements and immediate pressure. State whether a triggering event is paused for a response. That lets the next session continue without replaying a completed attack or granting another reaction because real-world time passed.

Tell the players what their characters can still perceive. Keep private preparation private, but do not hide the state they already knew. Ask each participant to confirm their own changing record. A character linked to an encounter is a budget reference and quick link, not automatic authorization for the GM to edit it.

The platform's synchronization and independent play copies can preserve this state across devices. Use its actual permissions and conflict handling. Offline cached gameplay does not make a newly started run possible without connectivity. Paper play can use the same snapshot without the service; the procedure remains understandable without an account.

### Close with a review that produces the next preparation

Ask four concrete questions: what changed, what remains, which decision mattered and what the group wants to pursue next. Record the answers alongside actual recovery and acquisitions. A short discussion can reveal that the most memorable moment was a negotiated repair, not the largest damage roll.

If a rule was hard to use, preserve the exact question. “We did not know whether the wall blocked this delivery” is useful for a definition review. “The encounter was bad” is an experience worth hearing but needs a follow-up about the decision, information or pace that caused it. Do not assume every complaint can be corrected by changing BU.

For the next session, prepare a changed opening and a few unresolved pressures. Bring forward obligations, injuries and relationships through their actual ending terms. Leave completed difficulties completed. SwordWeave's construction tools support a world that responds to the table; a reliable record makes that responsiveness possible.
