## Follow a whole encounter

A short example can make a die roll clear without showing how the rest of the table works. This chapter follows a longer encounter. You will see plans survive a bad result, movement split across tracks, a reaction change a situation, and a victory that means more than defeating an opponent. The numbers belong to these example records. The DCs, timing judgments, and consequences belong to this particular table.

The players use Torren, Mira, and Ilex from Three people, three starting builds. All are Medium, level 1, and begin at 12 Vitality. Torren has Physical-trained practices +7 and DC 12. Mira has Magical-trained practices +6 and DC 11. Ilex has Mental-trained practices +7 and DC 12. Each has 30 ft of walking movement across a round and one independent Reaction Slot. Nobody has acquired extra action economy.

:::art watcher

### Put the problem where everyone can understand it

The party reaches a sluice beside a narrow dry bank. A damaged barge is drifting toward the gate. Opening the sluice carelessly will protect the barge but wash out a spawning bed. Keeping it closed will crush the barge against the gate. A Canal Watcher, the creature described in Worked records, protects the bed and warns people away from the wheel. A worker is trying to keep a rope attached to an anchor post.

For this example, the useful positions lie along the same bank. The party starts at the 0-ft mark. The wheel is at 30 ft, the anchor at 35 ft, and the Watcher initially at 40 ft. Ilex can see the worker's old charter; Mira can see water beside the wheel. Beyond the anchor, a secure landing at 50 ft can receive people from the barge. These positions are a simple teaching sketch. The table agrees actual routes rather than treating every distance as an unobstructed straight line.

The DM explains the visible pressure: the barge is getting closer, the wheel can be turned, and the Watcher points toward eggs in the shallow water. The players do not need to roll to receive the premise. They ask whether the wheel's ordinary latch works, whether the worker can hear, and whether water exists on the safe side of the bed. The latch works but cannot hold against a full surge; the worker can hear; a smaller side channel can take some water.

There are several possible goals. The party could drive the Watcher away, preserve the spawning bed, rescue the crew without opening the gate, or negotiate a controlled diversion. They choose to open a safe channel, get the barge through, and avoid harming the bed. That goal helps them judge their actions. Reducing an enemy's Vitality is not a required measure of success.

### First Council: connect the intentions

Torren wants to reach the wheel and control its opening. Mira wants to redirect existing water through the side channel so Torren is not fighting the full pressure. Ilex wants to get close enough to read the charter and show the Watcher that the workers are using a permitted route. Their intentions support one another but do not become one character's action.

Mira's Water I, Verb II, and Near Range support reshaping located existing water within 30 ft. The table describes a narrow diversion, not control of the whole river. It must be established before Torren opens the wheel fully. The agreed ongoing requirement is that Mira keep one hand directed toward this channel and refrain from a second Water working until she releases it. This particular limit expresses this attempt's concentration and support. It is not a universal SwordWeave concentration rule.

Torren can walk to the wheel and turn a mechanism using ordinary tools and Prowess. He does not need a special “sluice turning” purchase. Ilex can read and speak through ordinary access. Paper I does not automatically tell Ilex whether the charter's claim is legally valid; their evidence and reasoning must establish that separately.

The table puts the Watcher's straightforward Water Impact in Fast. Mira's setup and Torren's dependent opening resolve in Measured, in that order. Ilex's read-and-explain intent resolves in Heavy after the outcome of the opening is evident. This placement is an example appraisal of dependencies, not a permanent rule that reading always takes Heavy or that water is always Measured.

> **Listen to the plan**
>
> Torren: “I move to the wheel and open it only after Mira has the channel ready.”
>
> Mira: “I move far enough to see the side channel properly, then shape that smaller flow. I accept the hand requirement.”
>
> Ilex: “I approach the worker without crossing the wheel. I want to find the clause about the spawning bed and explain what we are trying to preserve.”
>
> DM: “The Watcher sees Torren approaching the wheel. It gathers water to strike him. You can still revise your plan before we begin.”

The opposition's visible intent is useful information. It does not reveal every possible hidden fact or promise that the Watcher cannot change its response later. Torren accepts the risk. His player knows his current and maximum Vitality before committing.

### First Fast: danger arrives before the setup

Torren moves 10 ft along the dry bank. Mira moves 10 ft to a better viewing point. Ilex moves 10 ft toward the worker. Each has used only part of their own allowance. They have not received an extra Main Intent for making that movement.

The Watcher at 40 ft now has Torren, at 10 ft, exactly 30 ft away. Its Near reach is sufficient. It can locate him and has an unobstructed projected path. Its Water Impact uses attack +3 against Torren's DC 12. The DM rolls 10, for 13: a hit. The example 1d8 output rolls 5. Torren has no matching acquired resistance, so he loses 5 Vitality, falling from 12 to 7.

A hit does not automatically consume Torren's reaction, cancel his intent, or move him. This Water Impact did not include a knockback rule. His player records 7/12 and continues. He could propose a valid response if a relevant opportunity existed, but he does not spend the slot simply to reroll a hit he dislikes.

Mira's water channel has not resolved yet. The party cannot retrospectively claim that it intercepted the attack. Nor does Torren's injury automatically stop Mira's uncompleted work. Timing asks what exists now, who is actually affected, and what requirements are threatened.

### First Measured: resolve the dependencies in order

Mira shapes the smaller side channel. They agreed a Magical check against DC 12, with failure meaning she cannot establish a safe diversion in time. Her combined bonus is +6. She rolls 8, for 14. The water takes the smaller route, and the maintenance requirement begins. No damage die is needed for this result.

Torren spends his remaining 20 ft to reach the wheel at 30 ft. His total movement for the round is now 10 + 20 = 30 ft. He has none remaining unless another real permission or agreed consequence provides it. Arriving at Measured does not refresh his Speed.

He opens the latch and controls the wheel against the reduced pressure. Prowess is +7. Against the agreed DC 13, a roll of 6 gives 13, meeting the target. The gate begins to open. The table applies the success they agreed: enough controlled flow for the barge's next approach, with Torren holding the wheel until it can be braced.

His success does not mean the spawning bed is invulnerable or the whole encounter is finished. The worker's rope remains strained. The barge must still be guided. Mira remains committed to her channel. All those facts can matter in the next round.

### First Heavy: evidence can change an opponent

Ilex moves another 10 ft, reaching 20 ft and using 20 total. They are close enough to hear and read the worker's held document. The document visibly includes a spawning-bed clause and a sketch of the side channel. The DM does not call for a check merely to read legible words under these circumstances.

Ilex explains that the party is preserving the shallow flow rather than draining it. The Watcher hears them but sees Torren holding the gate open. It demands proof that the full surge will not follow. The conversation does not force immediate agreement. The evidence gives the party a meaningful question for the next Council: can they secure the wheel and show the safe route?

At round end, the players make a short record. A long transcript would slow the scene. A few relevant entries prevent forgotten resources and imaginary movement.

| Actor | Position along bank | Movement spent this round | Current Vitality | Continuing state |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Torren | 30 ft, wheel | 30 ft | 7/12 | Holding wheel; reaction unused |
| Mira | 10 ft, viewing point | 10 ft | 12/12 | Directing one channel; reaction unused |
| Ilex | 20 ft, charter | 20 ft | 12/12 | Has identified the relevant clause; reaction unused |
| Watcher | 40 ft | No movement in this example | 13/13 | Warns against an uncontrolled surge |

The Watcher's Vitality has not changed because nobody attacked it. Its attitude may change through the party's evidence and actions. A creature's social response is not automatically locked to whether it has lost a particular percentage of health.

### Second Council: make a plan from the changed scene

Movement and the independent Reaction Slot refresh for a new round. Current Vitality does not. Torren is still at 7/12. Mira's continuing requirement is reviewed at the agreed Council point. She can fulfill it now; this example has no additional Vitality payment. The pending damage-triggered upkeep scheme is not used.

Torren proposes fitting a brace between the wheel and anchor. Mira will release the original diversion only after the wheel is secured, then redirect a dangerous local surge away from the rope. Ilex will demonstrate the charter's flow plan. The worker is at the anchor, five feet beyond Torren.

The Watcher still fears a full release. It proposes a quick flow against the anchor to test whether the rope and wheel are secure. Torren wants to get his brace in place first. Their opposing efforts meet in Fast, so the table uses a Reaction Clash to settle which relevant event arrives first. That clash is a timing procedure. It does not spend Torren's independent Reaction Slot merely because “reaction” appears in its name.

Torren's whole intent is securing the wheel; moving and placing the brace are parts of it. Mira's new diversion depends on that brace and therefore follows in Measured. Ilex's explanation follows the demonstration in Heavy. The table checks that each dependency has an actual source and is not just a way to claim several unrelated intents.

### Second Fast: a lost clash leaves choices

Torren moves 5 ft to the anchor at 35 ft. The worker is immediately beside him. Torren's timing roll is d20 +7; a 7 gives 14. The Watcher's Magical-trained roll is +3; a 13 gives 16. The flow reaches the anchor before the brace is fully seated.

Acting first, the Watcher redirects a small unopposed flow against the unsecured anchor line. The table judges that narrow flow secure under its actual access; the clash did not automatically turn an attack into a hit. The rope pulls the worker off balance. This is the previously stated risk of this construction and position, not an automatic effect of every Water attack or irresistible control of a person. The table has already established the worker's position, the rope, and Torren's free hand.

Torren spends his available independent Reaction Slot to catch the worker. He can perceive the fall, reach them, and perform an immediate one-person catch. Under these circumstances the DM treats the catch as secure and resolves it without another roll. If the worker were too heavy, out of reach, or falling through a hazard, another procedure or consequence could be needed.

Torren catches the worker but cannot seat the brace in that same instant while carrying their weight. He places them on the dry bank, then continues his original wheel-securing intent in the later order the table agrees. Losing the clash changed timing and introduced a rescue; it did not automatically delete every possibility of completing his plan.

### Second Measured: pivot without pretending nothing happened

Torren moves 5 ft back to the wheel, for 10 ft used this round. He seats the brace while Mira's original channel still reduces the pressure. The worker has released the rope and is safe beside the anchor. With the altered load and intact brace, the table judges the securing task reliable. They do not require another roll solely because a clash already occurred.

Mira can now release her first working. That ends its hand-and-single-working requirement. She redirects the newly surging water through the safe outlet, using the same owned Water, Verb II, and Near ingredients. The relevant water at 35 ft is 25 ft from her position at 10 ft. She can locate it. The table reviews this new expression and agrees its scope before resolving.

The example new working settles once the gate and terrain establish a stable path. It does not need continuing direction after completion. That is a specific result supported and accepted at this table, not a rule that every water channel is self-sustaining. A different current could require ongoing maintenance or make the route impossible.

The wheel is braced; the bed retains its protective flow; the rope is no longer dragging the worker. The situation has changed enough that Ilex's planned proof now exists. Had Torren failed to secure the wheel, Ilex would have needed another offer, not an explanation describing a brace that was absent.

### Second Heavy: resolve the offer that actually exists

Ilex moves 10 ft from the 20-ft point to the wheel at 30 ft, using 10 of their refreshed allowance. They read the sketch and relate it to the visible channel. The remaining uncertainty is whether the frightened Watcher accepts the demonstration before striking again. They agree an Influence check against DC 13 for a bounded result: the Watcher permits this crossing while the bed's flow remains safe. It will not become a servant, reveal all river secrets, or abandon its purpose.

Ilex rolls 7 and adds their +7 combined Influence bonus, for 14. The Watcher accepts the crossing and lowers its gathered water. A successful check can establish a limited agreement. It does not override the other participant's agency beyond the terms of an actual agency-changing rule.

The party records that condition alongside the secured wheel. If the bed later dries, the Watcher can respond to that new fact. This is not a hidden punishment for making a good roll; it is part of the expressly agreed permission to cross.

### Third Council: recognize that the problem has changed

The Watcher is no longer attacking, but the drifting barge still needs a safe landing. The table continues the shared rhythm for the urgent rescue. They could also leave combat structure once the scene no longer benefits from it. Council and tracks are tools for coordinating pressure, not a demand to keep rolling through a peaceful conversation.

Torren wants to take the freed rope to the landing at 50 ft. He is at 30 ft, so the route uses 20 ft. Ilex plans to reach 45 ft and guide the crew toward the rope. Mira moves to 30 ft so water beside the barge, presently 25 ft farther along the route, remains within her Near reach. These are individual coherent intents, with movement accounted for across the round.

Torren's reaction is available again this round. Mira and Ilex also have theirs. Resetting those slots does not heal Torren, restore a short-rest allowance, or clear an injury. Each resource has its own procedure.

### Third Fast and Measured: the right person can respond

Ilex walks 15 ft from the wheel at 30 ft to the 45-ft point. Torren walks 20 ft to the landing in Measured. Mira walks 20 ft from 10 to 30 ft, using 20 of her round allowance, then guides water so the crew can approach the rope safely.

A crew member slips beside the barge at the 55-ft point. Ilex is ten feet away. Spending a Reaction Slot alone cannot extend their ordinary hand reach across that gap. They can call a warning, but a physical catch needs another actual opportunity.

Mira sees the crew member and the water around them. From 30 ft, the relevant point is 25 ft away. Her owned access can support a narrow immediate water response aimed at one located person. The table agrees the proposed catch is within immediate Complexity 0–1 and her actual access, then resolves it through her available reaction. She is not maintaining the old single-working-restricted channel anymore; its release matters.

They agree a consequence before commitment: the water keeps the crew member at the rope long enough to grab it, but washes a packet of ordinary supplies overboard. This is an example environmental cost, not a compulsory tariff for every watery rescue. Mira accepts. The person reaches the rope; the packet is lost; her Reaction Slot is used.

### End with the actual victory

By Heavy, the crew can follow the secured line to the landing. The Watcher maintains the safe current as part of its agreed cooperation, not because Ilex purchased control of it. The barge passes the gate without destroying the spawning bed. Nobody needs to reduce the Watcher to zero to complete the encounter.

Torren remains at 7/12. No healing occurred during the rescue. When the group later earns an agreed short rest, his ordinary allowance is 6, assuming it has not previously been spent. He needs 5 to reach 12/12, so actual healing consumes 5 and leaves 1. The lost supplies remain lost after that rest. The damaged barge still needs repair. The agreement with the Watcher can matter when the party returns.

This was one possible encounter. A failed Influence result could have left the Watcher demanding a different proof. An unusable brace could have pushed the party toward evacuation. A party without Water access could have used barriers, tow lines, local help, or a different offer. The chapter demonstrates readable decisions, not a required solution sequence.

### Take useful lessons from the transcript

Notice what did not have to happen. Every character did not roll every track. Every action did not deal damage. The party did not acquire a new primitive whenever a description changed. The DM did not turn every check failure into the same consequence. A clash, a reaction, an ordinary action, and a negotiation answered different questions.

When your own scene feels confusing, ask which question is unresolved. Do you need to establish access, timing, opposition, a path, a consequence, or the updated position? Name that question first. Choosing a familiar die procedure before knowing its purpose can create more confusion than it resolves.

## Translate a fantasy into purchases

A concept is wider than its first character record. You can express an armored duelist, a weather singer, or a tireless messenger without buying every association their titles suggest. These workshops adapt the earlier examples into three different level 2 characters. Each uses the 35-BU cumulative foundation, PB 2, baseline maximum Vitality 24, and ordinary short-rest allowance 12. They are examples to review, not prescribed careers.

The prices explicitly identified as proposed belong to these authored records. Canonical reference anchors remain useful, but they do not create a universal price for every sense, restriction, numerical bonus, or expertise grant. The purpose of each ledger is to show what was actually acquired.

### Sella: protect a route rather than buy a class

Sella escorts workers across broken quarry paths. The player first describes “a stone duelist who guards people.” They could write a long package containing armor, extra attacks, stone creation, ranged restraint, and healing. Instead, they ask what the next adventure actually needs: a reliable physical approach, a grounded subject, some reach, and training that helps notice danger.

The foundation is Physical 5, Mental 3, Magical 2, totaling 10, with Physical proficient. This is not Torren with a new portrait. Sella deliberately acquires more ordinary training and a smaller output die rather than Torren's rope bonus. Their ordinary weapon, clothes, and rope are recorded separately and grant no unstated defenses.

In an earlier draft, Sella's player had chosen Fieldcraft proficiency instead of the Influence entry shown above. Fieldcraft is already trained by Physical proficiency, so another proficiency grant would not add a second PB. The player replaces that redundant 4-BU choice, with the table's approval, with **Influence proficiency**, a different named practice at the same 4-BU reference anchor. The total stays 34, while the trained checks change. The table above is the corrected record.

That correction is important: a neat ledger can still contain an unhelpful choice. Review mechanical contributions before treating a saved draft as finished. A duplicated training grant may support a container's independent reuse, but on this character it does not grant another training bonus. The player chooses a useful different purchase rather than declaring a refund mid-encounter.

Sella's Physical practices and save are +7. Awareness, Knowledge, and Influence are +5. Reason is +3. Magical practices and save are +2. The trained Mental save is +5. Their Physical-selected DC is 12. Qualifying Quarry Footing changes an ordinary +7 Finesse check to +9. Their Medium capacity is 40 + 5 × 5 = 65 Load; equipped slots remain six.

**Stone Tap, an authored expression.** Apply a direct impulse to existing located stone within 30 ft. A normal harmful use can be one projected chip or moving stone edge: attack using Physical training, +7, against the target's DC, then 1d6 Physical Stone damage on a hit. The actual path matters. This definition does not grant reshaping a wall, turning flesh to stone, or striking a creature whose location is unknown.

**Route Guard, a descriptive practice.** Sella tests a path, marks a safe foothold, watches a loose slab, and offers a hand. This is an ordinary approach, not a free automatic shield aura. Their Finesse, Awareness, and Influence training support different uncertain parts. If they want a rule that automatically protects everyone within a radius, they must author its recipients, behavior, timing, and limits rather than rely on “guard” in their title.

Sella's player now has three useful tests: dislodge a visible loose stone, guide a frightened worker across a ledge, and try to stop a roof-wide collapse. The first uses their narrow access; the second uses ordinary cooperation and trained approaches; the third exposes missing scope and perhaps different verb permission. Larger d6 quantities alone cannot supply a structural repair rule.

### Nami: make supernatural versatility readable

Nami plays a reed instrument that seems to answer the wind. The player wants a person who can divert smoke, lift curtains, carry sounds, and protect a crowded room. Those outcomes contain several distinct subjects and arrangements. The first purchase set concentrates on grounded existing air and a small-group structure, leaving supernatural communication and extraordinary protection for later review.

The attributes are Physical 2, Mental 3, Magical 5, totaling 10. Magical is proficient. Nami has baseline DC 12, Magical practices and save +7, Physical practices and save +2, and Mental practices +3 except acquired Awareness +5. Maximum Vitality is 24 and short-rest allowance 12. No special instrument resource pool exists unless the table later authors one.

**Clear a Window, authored capability.** Redirect smoke-bearing existing air away from a located opening within 30 ft. Define the opening, outlet, and available air. A normal small use may be secure; an occupied burning building creates different stakes. Moving smoke does not automatically remove its heat, toxic contents from every room, or danger to people beyond the outlet.

**Divide the Draft, authored capability.** Use a basic line or field arrangement to direct existing air around several visible workstations in one small area. Structure II supplies an arrangement permission. It does not supply automatic enemy-only selection, a large hall regardless of its shape, silence, or immunity to flying debris. The player and DM discuss the actual recipients and flow before use.

Nami's instrument can help express the concept without secretly increasing its power. If an authored working requires continuous playing, identify whether that is a use condition or ongoing upkeep. If a working can also be performed by whistling, record that alternative. A picturesque instrument is not automatically an essential component; an essential component is a real restriction when agreed.

The player tests two arrangements side by side. Blowing dust off an unoccupied shelf uses a simple narrow flow. Clearing a corridor around six frightened people requires careful scope and consequences; the outlet may affect another room. They can reduce the ambition by clearing a single exit first. They need not add a compulsory dice roll to the first use merely to make it feel magical.

Nami cannot automatically heal through a breath because d8 is in the ledger. Nor can they selectively deafen enemies because they bought Air and Structure II. Those fantasies can inspire later creations, but their actual behavior and information/agency implications must be authored. The 3 unspent BU are a choice, not a defect to fill with an unclear benefit.

### Eren: build a specialist who matters without blasting

Eren carries messages across disputed borders and repairs the cloth seals used to authenticate a dispatch. The player wants someone useful when the party needs passage, clues, and safe transport. They choose ordinary expertise and grounded material work rather than importing a magical truth detector. The difference creates interesting scenes: Eren can examine a seal expertly while still having to decide whether its sender is trustworthy.

The foundation is Physical 3, Mental 5, Magical 2, totaling 10, with Mental proficient. Eren has ordinary Mental checks +7, Mental save +7, Physical save +3, Magical save +2, and Mental-selected DC 12. Their purchased Fieldcraft and Communion each add applicable PB to a practice outside the proficient attribute. No additional saving throw is trained by those two practice purchases.

The 6-BU expertise price is this workshop's proposal. It is not claimed as a universal catalogue price. If a repair risks altering evidence, discuss that risk before using it.

**One service, several questions.** Eren can notice a cut through Awareness, identify a common seal knot through Knowledge, infer a sequence from the damage through Reason, and ask a clerk for an explanation through Influence. The player does not roll all four in a compulsory sequence. They ask what remains uncertain and select a relevant approach. If the evidence is sufficient, a conclusion may need no roll.

Eren's equipment might include a courier case with a reviewed augmentation: “While the case is closed, its supported compartments hold these named fragile dispatches in place during ordinary walking.” Its ordinary protection can come from the item's construction, while an extraordinary preservation rule needs its own authored definition and proposed Item BU. Try Eren in a scene without a damage roll: a frightened clerk denies passage because two seals conflict. Eren can inspect the records, preserve evidence, offer a verification route, and negotiate limited access. Their contribution matters because it changes the group's options. The party does not need a universal healer or attacker slot for Eren to deserve a place.

### Carry only the promises you can explain

The three workshops begin at the same level and budget while supporting different choices. Sella spends 34, Nami 32, and Eren 34. All still have 35 progression BU for an encounter comparison, not their smaller spent totals. Their combined Party BU is 105 before any deliberate variation; their actual Item BU stays separate. No mirror credit has been used.

The point is not to search for the mathematically perfect mixture. It is to keep the first record readable, useful in likely scenes, and faithful to what the player wants. A concept can hold future ambitions without claiming their rules now. Keep a short “later” list beside the actual purchases. When advancement arrives, that list helps turn story into a deliberate next acquisition.

## Learn the families by making decisions

A reference tells you what a family means. A lesson asks you to choose between two constructions that sound similar but behave differently. Use these comparisons while reading a creation or writing one. The examples do not create a compulsory recipe with one purchase from every family. They show where a missing distinction can change play.

### Lesson: choose the kind of change before its scale

A character wants to open an iron-bound door. One approach is an ordinary key. Another is direct force through existing metal. Another reshapes a hinge. Another makes the door forget it is a barrier. Those are different proposals, not merely increasingly expensive versions of the same attack.

Verb I can support direct interaction or simple force within the owned subject. Verb II can support altering eligible existing states or properties. Broader reconfiguration or fundamental changes require reading the actual higher-tier permission. A large output does not quietly turn direct force into rewriting a governing law. Conversely, turning a small hinge can involve a change of state without needing enormous force.

Domain names also need their bound meaning. Grounded Metal I is a material subject. “Authority over the gate” is an abstract relationship, not every benefit implied by the word metal in a ceremonial badge. A gatekeeper might have both subjects, but buying one is not buying the other. Write the subject in the ledger rather than relying on a poetic title.

**Try the distinction.** Ask what a failed attempt would leave behind. A failed forced opening might damage the lock. A failed reshaping might leave a hinge distorted. A failed negotiation might leave the door perfectly intact and the keeper unwilling to help. Different proposed changes support different stakes.

### Lesson: an arrangement is not a subject

Nami knows Air I and Verb II. Adding Structure II at the reference 8-BU anchor permits basic spatial or multi-target arrangements within those actual permissions. A small field that directs existing air can now be discussed coherently. It does not give Nami Water, knowledge of hidden creatures, or a rule that distinguishes allies from enemies without an actual selection arrangement.

Compare a **line**, a **zone**, and a **named group**. A line follows a spatial path and can encounter whoever is there. A zone describes a bounded place. A named-group rule describes eligibility, which requires knowing what makes a member qualify. All can be useful, but their words determine different risks. “Everybody who needs help” is not a mechanically self-explanatory group.

For a simple single-target use, a separate high structure acquisition may be unnecessary. The free self/touch baseline and the actual access can support narrow workings. Do not buy a grand arrangement because a composer has an empty field. Identify which proposed behavior calls for it.

**Review question.** If two people exchange positions inside the field, does the rule follow the location or the person? If the author cannot answer, clarify the arrangement before the first urgent use. That one question can expose a moving-zone rule hidden inside an apparently stationary radius.

### Lesson: reach and a route solve different problems

Near grants up to 30 ft. It tells you a maximum eligible distance, not whether a projectile can pass through a closed shutter. A projected working travels through its actual path; a direct manifestation has its own supported delivery. Both still need legitimate targeting.

Imagine a visible hand above a wall at 25 ft. A traveling bolt must contend with the actual cover. A supported direct manifestation can act at a located hand without importing those intervening projected-cover penalties. Now change the scene: the hand lowers and the person moves behind an opaque wall. Near still exists, but ordinary sight no longer supplies their current location.

A farther range acquisition changes one of those facts. It cannot fix the unknown target or a blocked projectile path by itself. A suitable sense, another route, or an explicitly authored targeting exception changes different facts. This is why “give the beam more range” is often the wrong answer to a concealment problem.

**Useful notation.** On a paper capability, write separate lines for maximum reach, target-location method, and delivery. “30 ft; located through ordinary sight; projected through space” is more useful than “ranged.” It gives the table three terms to check instead of one ambiguous label.

### Lesson: output permission is not an output promise

The d8 reference purchase permits that die type for a suitable damage or healing construction. It does not guarantee a particular number of dice, automatically provide healing, or make every use harmful. The chosen expression still states its behavior, source, domain, recipients, and actual pressure.

A Water Impact can deal 1d8 on a hit. If the player wants 3d8 against several people, discuss the larger output and arrangement. That discussion does not necessarily require buying the d8 permission three times. Nor does it mean 3d8 is effortless because the character owns the die type. Access and execution appraisal answer different questions.

Compare two actually rolled examples: one d8 produces 6; two d4s produce 1 and 4, totaling 5. Those numbers are damage output only if the construction says so and its resolution succeeds. They are not attack totals, Strain grades, or acquisition costs. Keeping different dice roles separate prevents an output permission from becoming an unexplained universal bonus.

**Question before rolling.** “Does this number decide whether I hit, or what happens after I hit?” Ask it whenever a new player reaches for a die. A d20 check and a d8 output can both occur in an attack, but they do different jobs. A secure water transfer can occur without either.

### Lesson: improve the sheet at the narrowest useful level

A +1 attribute grant and a +1 bonus to a particular check are not interchangeable. The attribute can contribute to several practices, an eligible save, the chosen DC, and formulas that actually use it. The narrow bonus applies only to its stated field and condition. Decide which behavior you intend before choosing an operation.

Eren's Mental 5 and Knowledge expertise produce +9. Their conditional route-reading rule raises a qualifying Knowledge check to +11. An unrelated Reason check remains +7. If they instead acquired an accepted +1 Mental increment, those Mental-derived numbers would change according to the current formulas. That broader effect justifies a different review than a map-specific +2.

A Minimum rule needs a stage. “Treat the final total of a qualifying Knowledge check as at least 10” constrains the final check. It does not raise Knowledge's displayed bonus to +10 or the Mental attribute to 10. A result of 7 becomes 10 under that exact authored floor; a result of 16 remains 16. An author can choose another stage, but must identify it.

Training is a grant, not a pile of repeated additions. Two references to one proficiency do not add PB twice. Expertise uses twice PB when actually granted. A separate numerical bonus can stack at its actual scope under the default stacking rule. Do not label all three “another training point.” Their different operations matter.

### Lesson: distinguish durable capacity from current state

At level 2 with PB 2, baseline maximum Vitality is 24. Suppose an accepted authored rule adds 5 to maximum Vitality, making 29. A character currently at 20 remains at 20 unless a real healing rule or agreed adjustment changes current Vitality. “More room for Vitality” and “recover Vitality now” are different statements.

The ordinary fresh short-rest allowance based on maximum 29 is ceil(29/2), or 15. That identifies the baseline for a fresh allowance; it does not mean changing a build repeatedly refreshes an already partly used pool. Retain the actual recovery record and discuss a legitimate adjustment when the maximum changes. The agreed long-rest procedure refreshes the pool.

Other capacities need the same precision. An extra equipment slot does not increase Load capacity. A higher Load capacity does not supply a new item or increase its Item BU. A resource maximum is not necessarily its current amount. “Expand” is a theme; the field being expanded is the rule.

**Use a before-and-after pair.** Show maximum, current, and remaining recovery on separate lines. A reviewer can then see whether the proposed rule changes one value or several. This is clearer than a single Vitality number that sometimes means health and sometimes means capacity.

### Lesson: give mobility a mode and a boundary

A Small character's ordinary walking Speed is 25 ft. Default swimming and climbing use half base speed rounded up, giving 13 ft. A proposed rule adding 10 ft specifically to walking can make their walking allowance 35 ft; it should say whether it also alters other modes. Do not silently rewrite every mode when the wording names only one.

A flight permission needs an actual flying speed and limits. “Can fly” leaves carrying, altitude, continuation, and landing questions to settle. You need not supply a universal aviation system, but you should know enough to handle the next scene. If the character must keep both hands free, carrying a person can threaten that condition. If wind affects them, say how the table assesses it.

Burrowing is not automatically phasing. A rule that moves through loose soil does not establish passage through a metal vault. Teleportation is another movement behavior with different targeting and destination needs. A great destination description does not supply knowledge of a hidden landing place.

Track the mode actually used within the whole-round allowance. If a complicated mode switch makes the accounting unclear, settle the particular route with the DM before moving. Do not claim a complete fresh walking allowance and a complete fresh flying allowance simply because both speeds appear on the sheet.

### Lesson: know what a defense covers

A resistance can identify a source, a domain, or an actual combination. Those distinctions determine whether it applies. A defense against Magical Cold need not cover Physical Cold. A defense against all Cold is broader. Read the complete qualifier rather than only the word “resistant.”

An execution in this example deals 7 Physical Cold and 5 Magical Cold as separately defined portions. Magical-only resistance changes the latter portion to ceil(5/2) = 3. Total Vitality loss is 7 +3 = 10. It does not halve the full 12 merely because both portions came from the same named capability. Another construction might define a mixed instance differently; establish that identity before defenses.

Damage immunity negates covered damage, not every associated problem. A character immune to the heat's damage can still lose their ordinary paper map in a fire if no actual protection covers it. A numb hand can prevent feeling pain without preventing an injury. A defense should identify what is protected and what is outside its scope.

**Authoring test.** Present one matching instance and one neighboring nonmatching instance. If the author intends both to be covered, broaden the rule openly and review its price. If they intend only one, keep the qualifier visible. This makes a defensive creation useful at the table rather than a debate about its title.

### Lesson: ask a sense what information it provides

A sense should say what can be detected, at what reach, through which obstacles, and under what conditions. “Hear footsteps through a shared stone floor” can establish a particular kind of information. It is not automatically knowledge of the walker's name, thoughts, carried objects, or exact condition.

For an authored example, a player proposes: “While standing on exposed stone, distinguish the location of a moving human-sized creature contacting that same continuous surface within 30 ft; airborne creatures and gaps in the surface do not qualify.” This can help locate a target, subject to actual resolution. Its proposed price is reviewed separately. It is not granted free by acquiring Stone I.

The DM describes what the information means in the scene. An uncertain noise might require interpretation. A stationary target may not qualify at all. The sense can remain useful without becoming an answer to every mystery. An informational horizon is valuable precisely because the group knows where it ends.

Detection qualifiers can be narrower than sensory channels. Recognizing a magical trace does not necessarily locate its source now. Reading a material's temperature does not necessarily see a person's outline behind it. Decide whether the creation gives a warning, a location, an identity, or another kind of information.

### Lesson: state changes need an end as well as a name

Suppose a capability places a target in a state called **ice-bound**. Does that reduce Speed, prevent movement, impose disadvantage, or merely coat clothing in ice? Who can remove it, through what action, and under which circumstances? The label itself answers none of those questions.

An example definition might read: “The target's feet are held to this patch of visible ice; while the attachment remains, they cannot walk away without breaking it or receiving help. Hands and speech remain available. The table resolves an attempt to break or melt the attachment using actual access and opposition. It ends when that attachment is removed.” This is a restrictive record, not a imported frozen-condition package.

A fixed duration could be added, but it would be another explicit detail. Upkeep could be added, but duration does not automatically imply it. The attachment might last as ordinary material after a working ends if that result is actually established; another version might vanish when maintenance stops. Choose the version you mean.

This precision protects everyone. The affected player can choose useful actions instead of discovering that a familiar word secretly removed them all. The creator can price the actual limitation rather than a vague label. The DM can handle interference without inventing a new end condition under pressure.

### Lesson: triggers and quickening do not erase dependencies

A trigger names an event. Quickening changes eligible timing. An action-economy grant supplies an actual additional opportunity. They can interact, but none is an automatic synonym for the others. “When the gate opens, strike” may use a reserved reaction or a clearly defined intent. It does not create unlimited free attacks whenever someone jiggles the latch.

A quickened bridge cannot be used before it exists. If its construction depends on an ally moving a support, that dependency remains even when a timing rule normally makes it faster. The table can agree a different construction or explicit exception; do not pretend that a label rewrites the scene's order by itself.

Temporal ordering and stasis deserve the same attention. “Suspend this falling object until the end of Heavy” identifies a subject and an ending. It does not give the caster three turns, refresh everyone's movement, or stop the whole world unless the actual rule says those things. Read exactly what is suspended and what continues.

Against an unusual opponent, ask which extra opportunities are actually shown or established. A creature described as a boss may have authored extra intents or triggers. Its title alone grants none. As a player, understand the visible rhythm so you can make choices; you need not memorize a compulsory boss subsystem.

### Lesson: change a form without copying an entire creature

A player wants Eren to turn into a small moth. The concept suggests flight, small size, different senses, delicate limbs, and a way to carry or lose gear. Those are separate mechanical implications. A form can be cosmetic, a set of explicit grants, or another carefully scoped transformation. Decide which it is.

If the transformation genuinely changes size, apply its actual defined effects and agreed scope. If it grants flight, identify flight speed and limits. If it removes hands, decide which ordinary tasks become unavailable. If equipment remains unchanged, describe how it is carried; if it falls away, keep the items in the scene. A transformation should not silently heal the character or produce a free copy of their possessions.

Agency and information changes also need clear terms. A person who appears as a moth is not automatically believed to be one by every observer. A convincing disguise can depend on position, behavior, evidence, and checks. Supernatural alteration of another person's recognition or decision is a different behavior, with different boundaries.

**Start with one reliable promise.** An initial form might only change appearance while retaining the same numbers. Another might grant a narrow actual flight mode with clear limitations. Both can be satisfying. A short complete record is usually easier to learn than a dramatic form whose missing mechanics must be invented every time it appears.

### Lesson: a custom resource needs a real procedure

SwordWeave does not require a separate mana pool. An author can create a resource when it helps a particular concept, but must explain what gains it, what spends it, its limit, and its relationship to the action rhythm. Do not use a new counter merely to conceal an unresolved consequence.

For example, a character might propose a reviewed rule involving prepared wax seals: each relevant use physically consumes one named seal; preparing replacements requires specified materials and an agreed opportunity. That can be an intelligible resource requirement. It does not make every seal an automatic BU purchase, and a full pouch does not automatically grant extra reactions.

Distinguish the reusable acquisition from the consumable state. Acquiring the rule permits eligible use; the sheet or notes track the actual seals present. Item mechanics, ordinary material availability, and execution consequences still need their proper accounting. An elaborate counter is optional authoring, not a hidden requirement for all supernatural characters.

When a resource becomes tiresome, discuss revising it between scenes. Preserve the meaningful decisions and simplify bookkeeping that contributes nothing. The table can agree changes; do not delete a real limitation after benefiting from its accepted price without explaining the new record.

## Test targeting and conditions at the table

These short situations let you practice reading the actual terms. Read each setup, state an intended result, and identify which facts matter before choosing a roll. The suggested resolutions are examples of applying the framework. They do not establish automatic DCs for every similar scene.

### The shutter: located does not mean reachable

Sella sees a guard through a narrow opening 25 ft away. They can locate the guard's shoulder. Their Stone Tap is projected, and a shutter crosses its path. If the opening amounts to ordinary half cover in this agreed scene, the projected accuracy penalty is −4. With a +7 attack bonus and a die result of 12, the total is 12 +7 −4 = 15. Against an agreed target DC 16, it misses.

If the obstruction is minor instead, the −2 adjustment gives 17, which hits DC 16. The difference is the actual cover, not a random extra penalty applied because the working is supernatural. With total cover blocking the path, a projected strike cannot pass through by default. An attack bonus does not buy a trajectory through a solid shutter.

Now close the shutter and move the guard out of view. The original sighting is not permanent tracking. A supported alternate sense can establish a current location if its rule applies. A supported direct manifestation can change the delivery problem, but does not automatically fix the location problem. Keep those two questions separate.

### The blind corner: an area is an honest uncertainty

Mira hears movement behind a low corner, but the table has not established a precise specific target location. She can propose redirecting existing water into a known stretch of passage. A suitable arrangement and owned access can support that attempt. She must describe the area and its effects, rather than pretend she named a creature through the wall.

The DM can say what Mira knows about the passage and what she cannot know. There may be nobody in the chosen area by the time the flow arrives. There may be a friend there. Selecting a known location can be valid without guaranteeing that the desired recipient occupies it. Resolve uncertainty from the actual timing and circumstances.

A player can choose a safer aim: close the route rather than harm an unknown occupant, call out before acting, or wait for a clearer signal. The choice belongs to the player after the stakes are intelligible. An area working is not a loophole for infallible targeting.

### The shared floor: a sense can be enough

A character has the authored stone-contact sense described in the family lesson. They stand on the same continuous exposed floor as a moving creature within 30 ft. The sense identifies the current location under its rule even though a screen blocks ordinary sight. A specific-target proposal can therefore be possible.

The player still needs eligible behavior, reach, and delivery. A projected stone chip can be blocked by the screen even though the location is known. A supported direct manifestation could have another route. If the target leaps off the floor, the contact condition changes. The sense is not ordinary sight, but it is an applicable way to locate a target when its terms are satisfied.

Record what the sense actually supplies. The player knows a moving creature's location, not necessarily whether it is the missing merchant. That uncertainty can matter in deciding whether to attack or speak.

### The ally-only field: selection must mean something

Nami wants a field that clears smoke around allies but leaves enemies coughing. Air and Structure II support grounded air and basic arrangements, not automatic allegiance detection. Ask how the field selects its recipients. It might follow visible named companions, protect marked stations, or clear one safe corridor through which anyone can move.

Each alternative changes the scene. Named companions can move apart; marked stations can be occupied by opponents; a clear corridor can help anyone who reaches it. A more selective information-driven rule can be authored if desired, but its behavior must be explicit and reviewed. The word ally should not silently become a perfect moral sensor.

This also helps communication. If the protected corridor is visible, companions can use it deliberately. If a zone follows a person, the others know when leaving that person changes protection. The rule creates understandable choices instead of invisible favoritism.

### The conditional bonus: eligibility comes first

Quarry Footing adds +2 to qualifying Finesse checks on exposed natural stone while both feet can contact it. Sella stands on a wooden plank fixed over stone. Their ordinary Finesse bonus is +7. Because the described contact condition is absent, the conditional +2 does not apply. It does not matter that stone is nearby or that the scene takes place in a quarry.

If Sella steps off the plank onto exposed stone and the task concerns balancing under the defined condition, the bonus can apply. A die result of 9 then gives 9 +7 +2 = 18. This is a transparent application of the record, not the DM arbitrarily switching off a purchased benefit.

A player who wanted the broader “anywhere in a quarry” condition should propose that broader rule. Do not repeatedly stretch a narrow accepted condition until its price and wording no longer reflect its use. A consistent boundary makes it easier to bargain for a legitimate future improvement.

### The two badges: source identity is not card count

A character owns one acquired Steady Hands and references it inside both an upbringing and a capability. The two containers can appear as two cards, but the underlying acquisition remains one. During qualifying rope work, it adds its one +2 contribution. The record does not gain another +2 merely because both card titles are visible.

Now suppose the character deliberately acquires a second occurrence from a distinct accepted source. The default stacking rule permits applicable contributions unless an authored uniqueness or limit changes it, but the table must inspect both actual rules. A “once per recipient” limitation can prevent doubling. A clearly permitted independent numerical contribution can apply. The acquisition ledger and the stacking terms answer that question; layout does not.

Probability bias is counted separately. If those actual sources each grant a permitted advantage, they contribute bias stacks rather than static +2s. Two advantages and one disadvantage leave one net advantage. Roll two d20s and keep the higher, then add the static bonus once. Never turn an advantage stack into an unexplained +2 because another rule uses that number.

### The interrupted song: interfere with the requirement

Nami authors a maintained flow that requires continuous playing. An opponent tries to break the instrument. The relevant question is whether the attempt succeeds and prevents the actual requirement from being fulfilled at the relevant point. Resolve the attack, contest, or other appropriate procedure from the actual situation.

A missed strike does not break the instrument. A wound to Nami does not universally end the song. A successful silencing effect could matter if it prevents the required playing. A held note that the table has explicitly allowed as sufficient can still satisfy its term; another record could require an uninterrupted melody. Read the actual requirement.

This is ordinary interference with a specific ongoing working. It does not settle the pending numerical incoming-damage repayment scheme for Vitality upkeep. Keep that scheme visibly unresolved. You can run maintained songs, tools, restrictions, and agreed continuation costs without inventing its final threshold policy.

### The visible mark: declare when a condition starts

A primitive says “gain a bonus against marked targets.” That sentence still needs a procedure for the mark: who applies it, what counts as marked, when it begins, how it can be detected, and when it ends. An ordinary painted mark and a supernatural state can have different applications.

For an example, the table agrees that a physical chalk symbol on clothing qualifies only while visible and intact. Applying it requires actually reaching the clothing and resolving resistance if the recipient opposes. A target cannot be assumed marked merely because the player's intention was to mark them. Once it is washed away, the condition ends.

A marker in an app is useful bookkeeping after the qualifying fact exists. Clicking it is not an automatic tabletop success against resistance. Conversely, an agreed successful mark remains real even if the person tracking the sheet has not yet clicked its counter. Play establishes the state; tools record it.

## Follow advancement through the ledger

A developing character has several totals that can increase at different times. Keep progression, positive acquisitions, ordinary unspent BU, mirror credit, Item BU, and current gameplay state intelligible. You do not need a complicated spreadsheet. You need a record that explains what changed and why.

### A reward can change the foundation before the purchases

Mira begins at level 1 with 25 progression BU, 24 positive spent, and 1 ordinary BU unspent. Suppose the table awards 10 BU for completing an agreed campaign milestone. Her cumulative progression becomes 35, the level 2 reference threshold. She now has 35 −24 = 11 ordinary acquisition BU available before making another purchase.

PB remains 2 at level 2. Baseline maximum Vitality becomes (10 +2) ×2 = 24. Her attributes and training are unchanged, so her +6 Magical practices and DC 11 remain the same. Higher level does not automatically add +1 to every check. Her current Vitality changes only through actual healing or an agreed state adjustment, not simply because the maximum increased.

Mira then acquires Structure II for the reference 8 BU, with its actual arrangement permission. Positive spent becomes 32. Ordinary unspent becomes 3. Her progression is still 35, not 27 because she spent 8. She can now discuss suitable basic spatial or multi-target arrangements within her other owned access. She has not acquired a new domain or unlimited recipients.

| Moment | Progression BU | Positive acquired BU | Ordinary unspent BU |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Before the award | 25 | 24 | 1 |
| After the 10-BU award | 35 | 24 | 11 |
| After the 8-BU acquisition | 35 | 32 | 3 |

A new capability using Structure II and her existing Water ingredients is organization of those actual purchases. It does not add the old 20-BU ingredient group to the ledger again. Write the new expression so the table understands how the arrangement differs from River Hand.

### A proficiency bracket changes several formulas

Consider a later character at the level 8 reference threshold of 99 cumulative BU. Baseline PB is 3 and baseline maximum Vitality is (10 +3) ×8 = 104. On reaching the literal level 9 threshold, 117 BU, PB becomes 4 and baseline maximum Vitality becomes (10 +4) ×9 = 126.

For a current chosen attribute of 4 and no direct DC modifiers, selected DC changes from 5 +3 +4 = 12 to 5 +4 +4 = 13. A practice trained with PB changes from attribute 4 +3 = +7 to 4 +4 = +8. Expertise changes its training contribution from twice 3 to twice 4. An untrained practice does not gain PB merely because the bracket increased.

Review actual modified PB before applying those formulas. A primitive that changes PB can affect other derived values. A primitive that changes only one practice has a narrower effect. When the app resolves a new build, inspect the same distinctions rather than copying a remembered level-9 row over all current numbers.

The threshold table already includes its bracket adjustments. Going from 99 to 117 is an 18-BU increase, not 10 plus a second copy of a spike already inside 117. If your campaign uses another progression arrangement, label that variation clearly so new players do not mistake it for the reference.

### A mirror offers credit only with a real rule

A mirror is a compatible reversal of an eligible acquired rule. It is not an invitation to write an inconvenience no one will apply. A reversal should state who receives it, what changes, when it qualifies, and whether another authored limit prevents it from being mirrored.

Suppose a level 2 character proposes an eligible personal numerical drawback priced for 4 credit, with the table's agreement. Their ordinary budget is 35. Their debt ceiling is 4 ×ceil(2/4) = 4. If their positive acquisitions total 37, the overflow beyond ordinary budget is 2. Two of the accepted 4 credit are actually used to cover that overflow. The remaining 2 are unused potential credit, not ordinary unspent BU.

| Ordinary progression allowance | 35 |
| Positive acquisitions | 37 |

| Valid mirror credit available | 4 |

The positive acquisitions are also below the next-level creation ceiling of 45. The table explicitly approves exceeding the ordinary allowance. Having compatible credit does not bypass that conversation. A ceiling tells you a maximum accepted debt; it does not generate four credit from an empty drawback list.

Now change positive spent to 34 while leaving the accepted drawback record the same. There is 1 ordinary BU unspent, and no debt is needed to cover overflow. Do not announce “five ordinary BU left” by adding unused potential credit to that one point. Keep the figures labeled. The actual drawback still needs its established treatment if it remains part of the character; unused credit is not a license to ignore the authored rule.

### Reversing a number is not reversing its subject

An Add operation can reverse to Subtract within the same eligible scope. A personal grant can reverse to a personal revoke where the rule supports it. The reversal does not change “self” into “someone else,” erase a condition, or choose a new affected quantity merely because that would be convenient.

If a drawback restricts your own flight, granting flight to an ally does not repay it. If a drawback applies to your delicate rope work while your hands are occupied, a beneficial effect on another recipient does not cancel it. Evaluate that recipient's rules separately. Default stacking concerns applicable contributions on their actual subjects.

Minimum and Maximum, Multiply and Divide, and Grant and Revoke have supported opposite operations, but a coherent reversal still matters. Set To has no automatic mirror. A rule setting a quantity to 3 does not tell you what an “opposite 3” should mean. Do not infer a universal negative number when the source supplies none.

An authored descriptive drawback can be legitimate without a simple automated field. Explain it clearly and review its eligibility and credit. “Sometimes I am unlucky” is too unclear to apply. “This permission is unavailable while the named condition holds” is more concrete, but the condition and credit still need agreement. The table is judging an actual limitation, not rewarding the length of its prose.

### Rewrite without losing the history of play

At level 2, Mira might decide she prefers better navigation to multi-target arrangements. Her table can agree to replace the 8-BU Structure II acquisition with two 4-BU practice grants. That is an example of negotiated retraining, not a universal right to exchange any purchase at any moment. Record the accepted new acquisitions and remove obsolete references from the active build.

Keep an earlier version or a short change note. If a shared heritage includes the former ingredient, decide whether the character still references that old version or adopts a new one. A published fork changes a definition; it does not automatically update every adopter's sheet or every pinned creature in an encounter.

Also keep gameplay state distinct from the rewritten structure. A burned hand, spent recovery allowance, or outstanding promise does not disappear because a capability card moved to another container. If a build change affects a maximum or removes a needed ongoing permission, discuss the legitimate state transition rather than silently treating edit mode as a rest.

### Choose advancement from a remembered scene

Ask what you actually wished you could do. Did an unknown target defeat your plan? A relevant sense may help more than a larger die. Did the party need another person to negotiate? Training or an information tool may help. Did you run out of movement? A mobility rule might change the next chase. Did an overly complicated capability slow you down? Clearer writing may be the best improvement and require no acquisition at all.

Growth can also change your motives. Eren might stop carrying messages for a dishonest patron. Nami might learn why a melody is forbidden in a village. Sella might choose negotiation before force after an escort goes wrong. Those changes give mechanics a purpose without demanding that every relationship become a priced primitive.

Keep one short next-step list: a likely acquisition, a question for the DM, and a story choice. It helps you arrive at the next session ready to act. You can change that list as the campaign surprises you.

## Play a day without a battle

A noncombat scene can have clear stakes, meaningful uncertainty, consequences, and teamwork. It does not need to imitate damage reduction with differently named counters. This chapter follows Sella, Nami, and Eren through a day of investigation, passage, and practical work. Its procedures are examples of the shared framework rather than a new downtime subsystem.

### Begin with a decision rather than a compulsory check

The party needs to deliver medicine across a disputed bridge before evening. Two offices disagree about who controls the route. One courier seal is cut; another dispatch claims the bridge is closed. The weather is worsening. Nobody knows whether the closure is a safety measure or a political excuse.

The DM describes what is immediately evident: the visible broken seal, the two offices, and the sound of work on the far bank. The players choose what to learn first. They do not have to perform a perception roll before being allowed to notice the scene's stated facts.

Eren asks to inspect the dispatch without repairing it. Preserving evidence matters here. Sella looks for workers who actually used the bridge that morning. Nami checks where the medicine can be sheltered from wind and rain. All three choices can be useful, and each player knows the proposed purpose.

### Separate material repair from authentication

Eren's Cloth I and Verb II can support stabilizing an existing seal. They cannot automatically prove who tied it or whether its dispatch is true. Eren sets the physical pieces on a clean surface and asks whether repairing them would hide the cut. The DM agrees that the tear is legible now but could become harder to interpret if merged without documentation.

Eren copies the visible condition and asks for a witness before using the material working. That ordinary preparation changes later evidence. It does not require buying a forensic primitive to take notes. They choose not to claim a supernatural answer the current build does not supply.

A clerk recognizes the official knot. Eren's trained and expert Knowledge can help understand the knot's use, but their conditional Read the Route +2 does not qualify: this is a seal, not a physical route map. With Knowledge +9, a roll of 5 totals 14 against the agreed DC 13. They identify an unusual repair thread used at the riverside office. That is a specific clue, not proof that every clerk there is lying.

### Let a clue open a new approach

Sella speaks with a worker who crossed at dawn. The worker reports a damaged plank and says a repair crew was sent. Sella asks where the damage lies and whether the crew has returned. Those questions clarify the route. Their Knowledge training might help interpret a familiar construction detail, while Influence might matter if the worker is reluctant to give a statement publicly.

The worker is willing to explain privately. Under these circumstances, the DM does not require a roll for every sentence. Sella offers to accompany them to the disputed office rather than turn a successful conversation into a universal coercion effect. The witness can still decline a risky public confrontation.

Meanwhile, Nami diverts rain from the sheltered table using located existing air, within the actual narrow scope the table accepts. This protects the legible record from further soaking, but sends the runoff beside a neighbor's doorway. Nami asks about the outlet first and moves the table to a better location. An extraordinary use can be minor and secure while its practical route still matters.

### Make an offer a person could accept

The riverside clerk fears authorizing a crossing that injures someone. Eren offers a limited solution: the party will inspect the route with a worker, cross only if the damaged section can be bypassed, and bring a written report back. The intended outcome is temporary permission under those terms, not permanent control of the office.

They agree an Influence check at +7 against DC 13. Eren rolls 4, totaling 11. The clerk does not authorize the party alone. The failure changes the offer: a repair foreman must accompany them or inspect first. This is the example's pre-agreed limited refusal; it is not a rule that every social failure produces a helpful alternative.

The players can accept the escort, negotiate another guarantee, find a lawful different route, or choose an unauthorized crossing with its understood risks. Eren does not repeat the identical check until the die is higher. The unresolved issue is the clerk's confidence and authority, so a changed offer is more relevant than a changed performance of the same words.

### Read a map for the problem it can answer

The foreman provides a physical route map. Eren can inspect it, so Read the Route qualifies. Their Knowledge expertise +9 and conditional +2 give +11. A die result of 5 totals 16 against DC 14 for identifying a service path around the damaged span. That map-based conclusion is within the check's stated question.

It does not establish that the service path is still intact today. The party has a route to examine, not certainty about every current hazard. Sella compares the map's bridge supports to the foreman's report. Nami asks whether the service tunnel has an outlet suitable for air circulation. The party carries different evidence into the next decision.

A map can solve one uncertainty and create a better next question. This is satisfying investigation: information gives the players choices. It need not reveal the entire adventure at once, and it need not withhold an obvious fact until three separate checks have been paid.

### Use travel tools without importing a secret schedule

The service route is short but slippery. The group chooses a slower supported crossing with rope and a foreman rather than rushing across the damaged span. The table describes the likely time and risk before commitment. There is no universal ration deduction or fixed travel interval imposed by this chapter.

Sella can use Quarry Footing on a qualifying exposed natural-stone balance check. On a timber platform, the conditional bonus does not qualify. The player can choose a stone route if it is actually available, but cannot relabel wood as stone for the bonus. Their ordinary Finesse remains useful either way.

Nami can redirect a narrow obstructing draft, but does not automatically grant the whole party flight or protection from every weather consequence. Eren can preserve a cloth dispatch, but not a glass medicine vial merely because it travels in the same bag. Ordinary padding, careful handling, and the actual container are relevant tools.

### Decide which uncertainty deserves a roll

The group reaches a place where a damaged support can be inspected from safety. The foreman knows the construction and points out the usable bracket. With time, tools, and no immediate interference, fitting a temporary handrail can be secure. The DM need not add a check solely because the party has a strong Prowess bonus.

The uncertain part is whether a hanging medicine crate can be moved before the next gust. The players propose lowering it to the protected path using a braced rope. They discuss the possible loss of a vial and how to reduce that risk. A suitable Prowess or Finesse procedure then answers the actual question of controlling the movement under pressure.

A check is useful because success and failure lead to different circumstances. If the route were calm and they had ample time, the same physical job could resolve without a roll. Different circumstances can change the procedure without changing a character's owned access.

### Record success that does not look like treasure

The medicine arrives, the foreman writes a report, and the offices agree to mark the damaged span clearly. The party has kept a promise and gained a witness. That can matter next time they need passage. The DM portrays the actual relationship, not a universal reputation bonus supplied by this chapter.

The group can separately agree a BU award for the campaign milestone. Ordinary money, supplies, and favors remain setting rewards with their own meaning. Do not convert every friendly exchange into a hidden acquisition price. A grateful person is not a free capability you can activate without regard to their situation.

The players make three short notes: what they delivered, whose promise they made, and which damaged route remains unsafe. Their characters' Vitality may be unchanged because this day did not require damage. Any accepted restrictive or environmental consequences still use their actual ending terms.

### Bring this approach to a different genre

Replace the medicine with a data archive, the bridge with an orbital docking route, and the foreman with a station inspector. The central questions remain: what do you know, what can you actually affect, what route is possible, and what terms could a person accept? A futuristic title does not grant new mechanical behavior automatically.

Replace the route with a court ceremony. Ordinary influence and evidence can matter; an actual information or agency override is a distinct extraordinary proposal. Replace the dispatch with an ancient living tree. The relevant material or conceptual subject can change; Cloth I does not become Life access merely because the paper looks organic.

SwordWeave lets a table author the ingredients suitable to its world. The framework does not require every genre to be translated into a fixed list of fantasy classes. Preserve readable permissions and stakes while allowing the images, motives, and ordinary resources to change.

## Practice with answers

Use these exercises alone, with a friend, or before a first session. They test distinctions rather than memorizing a catalogue. Read the situation, make your own answer, then compare it with the explanation. An answer about table agreement can be correct without selecting a compulsory DC.

### Exercise: choose the combined bonus

A character has Mental 4, PB 3, and Knowledge proficiency. The sheet displays Knowledge +7. The player rolls 8 against DC 14. What total applies? What if a separately accepted map-specific +2 qualifies?

**Answer.** Add the displayed +7 once: 8 +7 = 15, meeting DC 14. Do not add Mental and PB again. If the distinct +2 genuinely applies and was not already included in the displayed total, the result is 17. The conditional modifier's eligibility is a separate question from the practice's training.

### Exercise: cancel the bias before rolling

A qualifying check has two advantage stacks and three disadvantage stacks. Its static combined bonus is +9. What dice are rolled, which are selected, and how many times is +9 added?

**Answer.** Two opposing pairs cancel. One disadvantage stack remains, so roll 2d20 and keep the lower. Add +9 once. If the dice show 17 and 6, the selected result is 6 and the total is 15. Five named sources do not mean rolling six dice after cancellation; calculate the net first.

### Exercise: inspect a missing permission

A character owns Water I, Verb II, Near, and d8 output. They propose restoring a drowned person's life with three d8s. Which part of the proposal needs another explanation?

**Answer.** The healing or revival behavior and its eligible subject need actual access or an explicit agreed exception. Grounded Water and d8 do not establish life restoration. More dice do not supply it. Ordinary rescue remains possible, and suitable future ingredients can be authored, but the current ledger does not silently grant the result.

### Exercise: pay for acquisitions only once

One 4-BU domain acquisition is referenced in an effect, two capabilities, and an upbringing. No additional occurrence was acquired. How many BU does this underlying domain add to the character's positive ledger?

**Answer.** Four. The containers reference one acquisition. They do not produce four purchases totaling 16 or four automatic simultaneous contributions. Any other acquired ingredients are counted separately. The description and ownership identity matter more than the number of cards showing the icon.

### Exercise: keep equipment outside the character ledger

A level 2 character has 35 progression BU and 34 positive character acquisitions. Their equipped tool contains a reviewed 4-Item-BU augmentation. What are the relevant acquisition totals and ordinary character budget left?

**Answer.** Character progression remains 35; positive character spent is 34; ordinary character BU left is 1. The tool's Item BU is 4 in its separate ledger. The tool can affect the resolved sheet under its actual equipped rule, but its accounting is not added to the 34 or subtracted from the character's one unspent point.

### Exercise: check slots and carrying separately

A character has six equipped slots. They propose equipping an ordinary one-slot item, a two-handed two-slot item, and an unusually permitted four-slot item. Is the slot requirement met? Does being under Load capacity answer that question?

**Answer.** The equipped requirement is 1 +2 +4 = 7, exceeding six. They need another real slot grant, a different equipped selection, or a changed item rule accepted by the table. Being under Load capacity does not provide the missing slot. Unequipping one item can solve the slot issue while its carried Load remains.

### Exercise: follow movement through tracks

A Medium character with Speed 30 ft walks 10 ft in Fast and 15 ft in Measured. They want to move another 10 ft during a valid Heavy-track reaction. What remains available ordinarily?

**Answer.** They have spent 25 ft and have 5 ft left. A reaction does not refresh Speed. Moving the full further 10 requires an appropriate additional permission or an agreed consequence. The response must also meet reaction access, trigger, and immediate-scope requirements; leftover movement alone does not make every reaction valid.

### Exercise: distinguish a target from a guessed area

A character knows a corridor is 20 ft away but cannot locate the person hiding there. They have a suitable area arrangement and actual access for a nonselective flow. Can they propose acting on the corridor? Does that guarantee they hit the hidden person?

**Answer.** They can propose the known area within actual access and agreed scope. It does not guarantee the person is inside or remains there. A specific-target working needs legitimate location or an authored alternative method. The area may also affect other eligible occupants, so discuss its actual recipients and consequences.

### Exercise: calculate covered damage

An agreed instance deals 17 Magical Fire damage. The target has matching resistance. What damage remains? Now add matching vulnerability on that same instance.

**Answer.** Resistance alone gives ceil(17/2) = 9. Matching resistance and vulnerability cancel, leaving 17. Multiple resistances do not quarter it by default. These are damage results; an associated restriction or destroyed object needs its own actual rule and scope.

### Exercise: preserve the rest allowance

A character's maximum Vitality is 13 and their fresh ordinary short-rest allowance is 7. At 11/13, an agreed short rest restores them to full. Later they reach 8/13 and earn another short rest. How much can each rest restore, and what remains afterward?

**Answer.** The first restores 2 and leaves 5. The second can restore 5, reaching 13, and leaves 0. Only actual healing consumes the allowance. Another short pause does not refresh it. An agreed completed long rest normally heals and refreshes the allowance; its healing is independent of this pool.

### Exercise: inspect used debt

A level 2 character has ordinary allowance 35, positive acquired BU 37, and 4 valid accepted mirror credit. Their debt ceiling is 4. How much credit is used to cover ordinary overflow, and how much remains unused?

**Answer.** The positive overflow is 37 −35 = 2. Two credit are used; two remain unused potential credit. Ordinary unspent BU is 0. The positive total is below the next-level creation ceiling of 45, and the table must still approve exceeding the ordinary allowance. Four valid credit are not the same as four actual used debt.

### Exercise: read an ongoing requirement

A capability contains three effects and requires continuous singing. Its player takes a small wound while maintaining it. How many upkeep tracks does this one capability normally have? Does the wound automatically end it or establish a new numerical repayment?

**Answer.** One maintained capability has one upkeep track, even with three effects. Determine whether the wound actually prevents the singing requirement; a hit does not universally end a working. The damage-triggered Vitality repayment scheme remains pending in this alpha and must not be invented from this exercise. Other actual consequences still apply at their agreed points.

### Exercise: distinguish stabilization from recovery

An ally at zero Vitality is successfully stabilized through an agreed procedure. Are they automatically awake at one Vitality? What if suitable healing subsequently restores 2?

**Answer.** Stabilization halts the rescue countdown but does not grant Vitality or consciousness. Suitable healing above zero ends the dying state and normally restores consciousness unless another actual consequence prevents it. A lingering injury can remain even when the ally is awake. Treat each state by its own rule.

### Exercise: check the next proficiency bracket

A character moves from the level 8 threshold of 99 BU to level 9 at 117. Their chosen current attribute is 4 and there are no direct modifiers. What are the new PB, selected DC, and baseline maximum Vitality? Does current Vitality automatically become that maximum?

**Answer.** PB is 4. DC is 5 +4 +4 = 13. Baseline maximum Vitality is (10 +4) ×9 = 126. The cumulative threshold already contains its adjustment. Current Vitality does not automatically refill; actual healing or an agreed state adjustment must establish it.

## Carry a useful table record

Your record is a tool for making decisions together. It should tell you what you can propose, which number to add once, what you have already spent, and what still matters from earlier play. Ornate names can make it memorable, but clear clauses make it usable.

### Write one ready intent on a small card

Keep the intended result above the ingredients. Then write the terms you repeatedly need. A player should be able to read the card aloud without explaining their whole nested build every time. Keep the complete ingredient definitions nearby for difficult questions.

| Card line | What to record |
| --- | --- |
| Intended result | The change you want to produce, in ordinary words |
| Eligible subject | Actual bound domain or ordinary object/person |
| Permission | Actual verb or other behavior grant |
| Targeting | How you locate a specific target or choose a known area |
| Delivery and reach | Actual route/method and maximum permitted distance |
| Normal expression | A reviewed useful example, not a guarantee of every scale |
| Resolution | The appropriate combined bonus or recipient's save for that expression |
| Continuing terms | Duration, actual maintenance, and ending if relevant |
| Scope reminder | One important thing the working does not establish |

For River Hand, a normal expression can be moving a small located stream through an available path. Its scope reminder might be “does not revive the dead or reveal an unknown target.” A normal example helps a beginner begin; it should not imprison future eligible creativity. New actual scope is appraised when proposed.

### Keep a round line rather than a second character sheet

Record the facts likely to change this round: position, movement spent, Main Intent, independent reaction availability, current Vitality, ongoing requirements, and new consequences. Put persistent purchases on the character record, not in the round line. This keeps the temporary state short.

At the next Council, refresh the ordinary movement allowance and Reaction Slot, review the actual continuing requirements, and begin the next plan. Do not automatically erase consequences or heal. An app's phase control supports those decisions; advancing the marker is not a hidden instruction to apply damage or end every effect.

If you make a mistake, state it plainly. “I counted ten feet twice; I am actually at the wheel, not the landing” lets the table repair the situation. Quietly moving a token to the preferred destination creates a harder problem than an honest correction.

### Give a consequence its own readable line

Use cause, actual change, affected subject, start, and ending. Add a numerical value only when the consequence is numerical. “Cannot maintain sung workings until the named throat care and agreed rest” can be clearer than a row of invented penalty icons. “Owes the lock keeper a report before evening” can be a complete narrative obligation.

When an ending occurs, mark the consequence resolved rather than deleting its history if the cause may matter later. A healed hand and a repaired relationship are different outcomes. The group can keep a simple shared note without turning every obligation into a new compulsory subsystem.

### Finish the session in five minutes

Before stopping, record where the party is, what they are trying next, current Vitality and remaining short-rest recovery, active consequences and workings, and any new acquisitions or gear. Identify one unresolved rule question separately from the accepted events. A question is not automatically a retroactive veto of everything that happened.

If the sheet is shared, agree who can change gameplay state while someone is absent. Viewing a character or linking its budget to an encounter does not itself authorize editing. If you use paper, agree which record is current; if you use digital synchronization, resolve a conflict before overwriting another person's update.

The next session can begin with a short recap: “We preserved the bed, rescued the crew, and owe repairs for the lost packet.” That is enough to restore the meaningful choices. Your character's detailed ingredient library remains available whenever a new idea needs it.
