# SwordWeave System Reference Document Draft

Rules edition: **v0.1 alpha**. Working reference; incomplete tables and the deferred upkeep policy still prevent a final release.

## Core procedure

### Participants and intent

Players portray their characters. The Dungeon Master or Game Master describes the world, portrays other participants and adjudicates outcomes with the table. The group agrees the intended setting, starting budget and relevant expectations before play.

An intent states the result an actor wants. Before commitment, establish:

1. The intended result.
2. Applicable ordinary circumstances and extraordinary access.
3. Scope, targets, reach, output, timing and persistence.
4. Stakes, cost and consequences.
5. Resolution procedure.

The actor can accept, narrow or revise the attempt. Resolve uncertainty when needed, apply the agreed result and consequences, then continue from the changed situation.

### Rule of Cool

**Core rule.** Give exciting ideas a chance. Discuss what makes the attempt possible, what it risks and how it will resolve. Use the actual situation to support a coherent, engaging and cinematic scene.

The table can agree an exception or variation. State its scope and consequences explicitly. An exceptional outcome does not silently grant unrelated permanent permissions or erase an existing consequence.

### Rules and examples

**Core rule** identifies the framework. **Default** identifies the supplied procedure. **Authored rule** identifies a specific component's permission, condition, limit or exception. **Optional guidance** identifies a treatment the table may select. **Example** illustrates a case. **App note** explains a digital operation.

Use the agreed procedure for the actual situation. Published examples are not universal prices or consequences.

## Character foundation and construction

### Attributes and practices

**Default.** Base Physical, Mental and Magical total 10; each is from −1 to +5. Relevant primitives can modify the resulting values. Use current numerical attribute values directly rather than converting them into another modifier scale.

| Attribute | Practices |
| --- | --- |
| Physical | Prowess, Finesse, Fieldcraft |
| Mental | Awareness, Reason, Knowledge, Influence |
| Magical | Mysticism, Communion, Intuition |

Ordinary attempts do not require training. Relevant full attribute applies to a practice check. Training adds PB where applicable; expertise uses twice PB where granted. Grants that establish the same training do not automatically add PB repeatedly. An explicit numerical bonus follows its own authored rule.

### Build Units and components

BU pays for acquisition of reusable primitives. A default level 1 foundation has 25 BU. Item BU is separate. Agree custom or higher-level starting budgets with the DM.

A primitive establishes a reusable permission or rule. Effects group ingredients or outcomes; capabilities organize complete workings. Lineage, upbringing and manifest organize ancestry, experience and current expression. Their names do not grant mechanics beyond their stated content.

A character foundation requires owned positive verb and domain access in the character build; items do not satisfy this requirement. Missing range and output categories receive the zero-BU Touch and 1d4 baselines.

Reusing an owned primitive in another composition does not repurchase it. Deliberately acquiring additional occurrences, acquiring new ingredients and accepting permitted mirrored drawbacks require their own accounting.

### Authored prices and stacking

Library/builder prices and tiers are guidance. Authors select BU prices; the DM may accept them or request adjustments. Scope, conditions, restrictions and stacking can affect the price.

Applicable contributions stack by default unless the authored stacking rules state otherwise. Enforce conditions, uniqueness and scope. Permission grants, numerical contributions and roll behavior are distinct; a counter is not automatically a static +1.

## Construction reference

### Component records

**Authored rule.** Record the name, readable rule, actual ingredients, recipients/targets, applicable conditions, operations and values, stacking/uniqueness restrictions and relevant ending conditions. State a proposed BU price for an acquisition. A label or descriptive name grants only the rules actually recorded.

A primitive may grant permission, contribute a numerical change or define a descriptive rule. An effect groups related ingredients or outcomes. A capability organizes a working or talent and may reference effects and primitives. Lineage, Upbringing and Manifest organize character heritage. Items carry their own ingredients and accounting. These containers do not automatically create additional acquisitions or permission grants.

Reusing the same acquired primitive in several compositions does not repurchase it. Deliberately acquired additional occurrences follow their authored rules and acquisition costs. Resolve overlapping references against the actual acquisitions rather than treating every appearance as another free contribution.

### Verb, domain and structure access

**Default reference.** The base access families use I–IV tiers. Their current suggested acquisition anchors are 4, 8, 12 and 16 BU respectively. Family meanings remain distinct; a tier in one family does not grant another family's access.

| Tier | Verb access: kind of change | Domain access: scope of subject | Structure: arrangement |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| I — 4 BU | Direct physical/perceptual interaction and simple creation/destruction or force application within access | Grounded, directly observable subjects such as water, fire, earth or ordinary local weather | Single-point/direct application, such as self, touch, a fixed object or one target |
| II — 8 BU | Change existing states and properties: reshape, redirect, combine, separate, transfer or stabilize | Physical/conceptual systems such as life, growth, memory, emotion or bounded local space/time | Basic spatial or multi-target arrangements, such as small groups, lines, cones, radii or fields |
| III — 12 BU | Reconfigure internal systems, impose constraints or redirect bounded causal chains | Abstract/systemic subjects such as consciousness, identity, information, bounded causality or symbolic systems | Adaptive spatial arrangements, such as moving/expanding zones, branching chains or conditional patterns |
| IV — 16 BU | Interact with governing logic, define exceptions or alter fundamental constraints within agreed scope | Fundamental subjects such as existence, reality, global causality or foundational laws | Systemic arrangements, such as scene-wide, inclusion/exclusion or state-driven target rules |

Read and bind the actual subject or arrangement when composing a working. Access does not by itself establish unlimited output, guaranteed success, complete knowledge or exemption from consequences. A structure describes permitted arrangement; actual targets, area, timing and selectivity still enter the appraisal.

### Construct an effect or capability

1. Describe the intended result.
2. Identify the ordinary circumstances and extraordinary access supporting it.
3. Select acquired primitives and, where useful, related effects.
4. Specify actual grants/changes and applicable recipients, conditions, stacking and limits.
5. Describe the normal use or trigger and any duration/continuation requirements.
6. Identify which details are fixed by the construction and which are appraised for each attempted use.
7. Review the definition and acquisition accounting with the table; test contrasting uses.

A capability need not contain a separately named effect. Neither container grants mechanics absent from its actual ingredients or descriptive rules. A new arrangement of existing purchases is not itself a new purchase of those ingredients.

### Construct a heritage or character

For a heritage, choose Lineage, Upbringing or Manifest according to its narrative purpose. Describe the heritage, select actual ingredients/capabilities, identify acquisitions and record any genuine drawbacks. Review the resulting scope and suggested cost. A descriptive history need not grant mechanical benefits.

For a character, agree the starting budget/level, allocate base attributes, choose the proficient attribute and practice training, establish owned positive verb/domain access, acquire the chosen ingredients, organize heritage/capabilities, then resolve the final sheet. Keep Item BU and equipment accounting separate. Check derived formulas, active modifiers and consequences. A player may use an existing creation, adapt it or author one.

**Checked example.** Mira's upbringing organizes Verb II (8), Water domain I (4) and Knowledge proficiency (4): 16 BU. Near Range (4) and d8 (4) bring her total to 24/25 BU. Organizing Water/Verb/Near in Guided Water and River Hand does not change that acquisition total. Her attribute allocation and derived values appear in the PHB character example.

### Construct creatures and encounters

**Default creature foundation — current builder reference.** Choose a positive base creature budget B. Rank r = sqrt(B / 25); base attribute points = ceil(3r); PB = 1 + ceil(r); baseline Vitality = max(1, ceil(B / 2)). Allocate the available nonnegative attribute points, select the proficient attribute and distribute practice slices consistently with the practice-allocation rules. Authored modifiers and an explicit agreed Vitality baseline can change the resolved sheet. These are creature baselines, not the player character's starting allocation or Vitality formula.

Describe the creature's encounter purpose, senses, movement and behavior. Select and review actual primitives, effects, capabilities, heritages or items that support its abilities. Resolve active modifiers, conditions, practices, output and Vitality. Show base budget, acquired/spent creature BU, permitted mirror credit and remaining creature BU separately; equipment contributes separate Item BU. Shared ingredients are accounted against their acquisitions rather than repeatedly charged through containers. A higher maximum does not automatically restore current Vitality.

**Worked construction.** A 25-BU Canal Watcher has r = 1, three base attribute points, PB 2 and baseline Vitality 13. Allocate Physical 2, Mental 0 and Magical 1. Verb II (8), Water domain I (4), Near Range (4) and d8 (4) spend 20 creature BU, leaving 5; this example has no mirror credit and no equipment. Its water manipulation is appraised through the recorded permissions. A title such as “guardian” grants no armor, extra actions or area attack.

For an encounter, record the objective/situation, party BU and separate Party Item BU, any known party size, the chosen creatures and quantities. Multiply chosen base creature budgets and resolved Item BU by quantity. Compare totals, counts and concentration of budget where those facts are available. Mirror credit is not another creature's budget. Spent/remaining creature BU describes a build; encounter appraisal uses its chosen base budget.

**Worked appraisal.** Two Canal Watchers contribute 2 × 25 = 50 enemy creature BU and 0 enemy Item BU. A party with 50 Party BU and 8 Party Item BU has a combined 58; the enemy combined total is 50. Enemy minus party is −8, and the enemy/party ratio is 50/58. If party size is two, counts match; each Watcher contains half the enemy creature budget. None of these facts establishes a difficulty rating.

Comparable BU does not promise comparable difficulty. Objectives, terrain, information, control, area effects, support, burst damage and how the table applies the rules affect play. Manual party totals are sufficient; linked sheets are optional budget references. Run the encounter with the shared Council → Fast → Measured → Heavy rhythm, current creature state and agreed consequences.

**App note.** Catalogue templates, immutable versions, independent play copies and encounter runs organize digital reuse and tracking. Publication links add convenience; offline construction requires the actual relevant definitions. Starting a digital run creates private play copies; choosing or previewing a template does not modify another user's creature or character.

## Resolution

### Progression and mirrored acquisitions

**Default.** Acquisition BU, Item BU and execution Cost are separate. Unspent BU may acquire or invent ingredients during play with table agreement. Spending acquisition budget does not undo progression. The DM/table establishes rewards and pacing; there is no compulsory XP or per-session award schedule.

The cumulative table includes bracket spikes. Add them only through the progression procedure, never again to a listed cumulative threshold. Apply active PB/Vitality modifiers after these baselines.

Compatible operations can be mirrored within their actual scope and access: Add ↔ Subtract; Multiply ↔ Divide; Minimum ↔ Maximum; Grant ↔ Revoke. Set To has no automatic opposite. Descriptive reversals require a coherent agreed meaning. An accepted personal drawback remains in effect and in the acquisition accounting even when a separate action proposes another direction.

The default debt ceiling is 4 × ceil(level / 4): 4 at levels 1–4, 8 at 5–8, 12 at 9–12, and onward. Ordinary remaining BU, available drawback credit and actual used debt are distinct. Creation fills ordinary budget, then eligible credit, then any remaining overflow; exceeding the ordinary allowance requires DM agreement and cannot exceed the next-level creation ceiling or permitted drawback debt. Later sheet warnings are app behavior, not permission to ignore those agreed constraints.

### Checks and formulas

**Default check:** d20 + relevant current attribute + applicable training + applicable modifiers. Meet or beat the agreed DC. A displayed combined bonus is added once.

| Quantity | Baseline |
| --- | --- |
| PB | 2 + floor((level − 1) / 4), before active PB modifiers |
| Character DC | 5 + current PB + chosen eligible current modified attribute + direct DC modifiers |
| Maximum Vitality | (10 + current PB) × level, then active maximum-Vitality rules |

One selected-attribute DC is distinct from Physical, Mental and Magical saving throws. An attack and a saving throw are different resolution methods; a working does not require both by default.

Use applicable combined bonuses once. Check/roll result floors, ceilings and fixed results apply at their authored scope, distinct from attribute/stat bounds. No automatic natural-20 success, natural-1 failure or critical-damage multiplier is imported; an authored rule or agreed table variation can establish its own effect.

Ordinary rounding is upward. The explicit floor in the PB formula specifies its own operation. Authored result limits and other exceptions state their own scope.

### Advantage and disadvantage

**Default.** Apply authored stacking restrictions, then cancel opposing advantage/disadvantage stacks one-for-one. Let N be the absolute remaining count.

- Net advantage: roll N + 1 resolution dice; keep the highest.
- Net disadvantage: roll N + 1 resolution dice; keep the lowest.
- Zero net bias: roll one resolution die.

**Example.** Two advantages and one disadvantage leave one advantage: 2d20, highest. Two advantages with no opposing source: 3d20, highest. The applicable static bonus is then added once.

## Scope and targeting

### Access and use

Verb and domain permissions determine eligible changes and subjects. Range and output permissions determine access to reach and die types. Selected dice count, area, targets, duration and other declared scaling can increase pressure without each requiring another purchase by default. Extra behaviors can require additional access.

### Sensory location

**Default.** Targeting a specific creature or object requires locating it through an applicable sense. Ordinary vision, granted blindsight, tremorsense or another suitable sensory permission can provide that location.

Without it, an actor can propose targeting a known spot or area and resolve uncertainty at the table. A chosen area does not guarantee a desired creature is present.

**Authored rule.** A working such as scrying, widespread weather influence or “any qualifying target within range” can establish a different targeting method. Range alone does not grant information or override the working's targeting requirements.

### Range permissions

Default reference maximums are Near 30 ft, Far 60 ft and Very Far 120 ft. Extreme permits continuous reach up to approximately three miles, including nearer targets. The table can agree broader scope, including Extreme beyond three miles, with appropriate access and consequences. These permissions do not reveal targets or remove sensory/targeting requirements.

## Combat and movement

### Shared rhythm

Council coordinates intent. Fast resolves Complexity 0–1, Measured resolves 2–3 and Heavy resolves 4+. Complexity describes timing dependencies rather than damage or power. There is no default initiative roll.

One independent Reaction Slot resets at Council. A default reaction has a valid trigger, immediate Complexity 0–1 and self/touch or a single-target scope. Relevant access and actual timing still apply.

Describe one coherent Main Intent and establish dependent actions. A Reaction Clash resolves colliding opposing intents within a track when timing matters: d20 + relevant attribute + applicable training/modifiers; higher acts first. On a tie the GM resolves simultaneously or follows the stronger fictional position. An Active Contest resolves a direct struggle using each side's relevant approach; higher wins the immediate struggle. Use combined practice totals once. Losing a clash does not automatically erase the Main Intent; pivot when changed circumstances require it.

For a valid reaction, pause the triggering event, resolve the response, then continue. It may occur before or after the Main Intent and does not automatically cancel its trigger.

### Movement allowance

**Default.** Speed is ordinary movement available across the whole round. Spend some or all and divide it among tracks and valid reactions. It can accompany an intent or be used independently where the situation permits. Spending a Reaction Slot does not provide a new movement allowance.

Moving farther requires appropriate speed access or an agreed cost/consequence. Actual routes, hazards, interference and the Rule of Cool guide the ruling.

**Example.** Speed 30 ft permits 10 ft during Fast and 20 ft during Heavy. Additional movement requires a further permission or table agreement.

| Size | Base walking Speed | Default swimming/climbing |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Tiny | 15 ft | 8 ft |
| Small | 25 ft | 13 ft |
| Medium | 30 ft | 15 ft |
| Large | 40 ft | 20 ft |
| Huge | 60 ft | 30 ft |
| Gargantuan | 90 ft | 45 ft |

Swimming/climbing use half base speed rounded up. Apply authored changes; flight and burrowing require granted access.

### Surprise

**Optional guidance.** There is no universal surprise mechanic. An ambush can justify the prepared side completing one round before surprised participants respond. Circumstances or an agreed d20 check can change this treatment.

## Costs and persistence

### Contextual consequences

Strain describes how an attempt pushes the actor and situation. Agree costs and their timing before commitment. Costs can be Vitality, resources, numerical penalties, restrictions, temporary loss of access, tasks, environmental effects or narrative consequences; combinations and negligible costs are possible.

Maintaining a working generally spreads pressure out or lowers upfront demand compared with forcing an increased expression in one round. There is no universal discount or conversion into damage. Specify the continuation requirements and the duration/ending of any consequence.

The 0–6 Strain grades guide appraisal: effortless, light effort, noticeable strain, heavy burden, dangerous instability, critical risk and catastrophic consequence. They are not automatic bills or fixed success probabilities. Consider scale, impact, dependencies/complexity, environment, opposition and urgency. Declared dice count, area, targets or duration can add pressure without each requiring another purchase by default; new behaviors may require additional access.

**Optional guidance.** Let B be the minimum evaluated composition and A the selected evaluated use. ΔCV = A − B; total CV = B + ΔCV = A. With B = 12 and A = 30, increase is 18 and total is 30. These illustrate arithmetic, not a universal spell price. CV is neither a roll bonus nor acquisition BU paid again, and numerical scaling cannot capture every contextual demand.

**Core rule.** A consequence records its cause, actual change, timing/trigger and ending/recovery terms. Names do not import universal numerical packages. Agree when Cost applies and what failure means; interruption has no universal refund.

### Upkeep

Track, duration and upkeep are separate properties. A maintained capability has one upkeep track even when it contains multiple effects. Several capabilities can be maintained; their agreed requirements accumulate. Continuing and ending follow the working's actual conditions.

Agree an explicit continuation point in the shared round, such as Council. A recurring 4-Vitality requirement can be due again next round; a loss of access follows its own duration/ending condition. Interference needs actual access, position, timing and resolution. A hit alone is not a universal interruption. An actor unable to perform a required task cannot continue it. Instant actions cannot be interrupted retroactively after resolution; resulting ongoing effects may be separately disrupted through appropriate access.

**Editorial placeholder — creator decision deferred.** The Vitality-only damage threshold, immediate extra repayment and damage-counter reset/excess policy are awaiting a ruling. Do not treat an example proposal as settled, or apply that numerical scheme to non-Vitality upkeep. Tables agree a procedure pending final wording.

## Recovery and equipment

### Agreed rests

A short rest is a short pause, perhaps 15 or 30 minutes. A long rest is overnight sleep or equivalent recovery, not necessarily eight hours. DM and players agree whether their effects apply; elapsed time never applies recovery automatically.

**Default short rest.** Allow recovery equal to half maximum Vitality, rounded up, between completed long rests. Only actual Vitality restored consumes the allowance. Unused recovery carries into subsequent short rests; it cannot heal above maximum.

**Default long rest.** Restore to maximum and refresh the short-rest allowance. The table can agree partial healing after a completed long rest. Long-rest healing is independent of the short-rest allowance. Injuries and other lasting consequences use their stated recovery requirements.

**Example.** Maximum 13 gives allowance 7. At 10/13, heal 3; 4 remains for later short rests. A completed long rest refreshes that allowance.

### Stabilization and healing

At zero Vitality, the character collapses, unconscious and incapacitated. Collapse uses a contextual rescue countdown; roughly a minute is guidance, not ten guaranteed safe rounds. Dedicate an action track to Fieldcraft stabilization or suitable owned primitives, with agreed access, resolution and Cost; no universal stabilization DC is prescribed. Success halts the countdown without granting Vitality or consciousness. Healing above zero ends the dying state and restores consciousness unless another consequence prevents it. A stabilized character can recover through assisted rest; active dying requires rescue first.

### Damage and lethal thresholds

Physical, Magical and Psychic are execution sources; domains identify the output's nature. Mental is an attribute. Read actual source/domain scope: resistance halves matching damage rounded up, vulnerability doubles it, immunity negates covered damage. Multiple resistances do not quarter damage by default. Applicable resistance/vulnerability on an instance cancel. Damage immunity alone does not negate every associated condition.

Mixed output needs an explicit source/domain breakdown and identification of split versus separate instances. Apply final post-defense damage as Vitality loss. Healing is capped by current maximum; increasing maximum alone does not heal.

**Examples.** Nine Magical Fire becomes 5 under matching resistance, 18 under vulnerability or 9 with both. Six Physical Fire + eight Magical Fire totals 10 under Magical-only resistance (6 + 4).

One execution dealing at least twice maximum Vitality, or taking current Vitality to −maximum Vitality, can cause immediate death under the massive-damage rule. Establish lethal stakes beforehand. Use current Vitality before the hit and final post-defense damage rather than a sheet's stored zero clamp. Maximum 13/current 4/final damage 17 reaches −13; final damage 26 independently reaches twice maximum. The collapse clock is distinct from an outright lethal result.

### Equipped slots

**Default.** Six universal equipment slots. An ordinary equipped item occupies one; a two-handed item occupies two. A higher authored requirement can represent unusual gear, and primitives can extend available slots. Size alone does not multiply the final requirement.

Load, Item BU and equipped slots are distinct measures. Equipped items still contribute carried Load. A small character's unusually large sword may be permitted with an explicit three/four-slot requirement or another agreed consequence.

### Load and delivery paths

Capacity = size base + 5 × current Physical + applicable bonuses. Load above capacity is encumbered; the GM/table determines penalties and complications. Equipped items still count toward carried Load. Explicit authored Load rules can change an item's contribution. A backpack organizes contents without automatically adding capacity.

| Size | Base capacity | Default Load per item |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Tiny | 10 | Pouch rule |
| Small | 20 | 1 |
| Medium | 40 | 2 |
| Large | 80 | 4 |
| Huge | 160 | 8 |
| Gargantuan | 320 | 16 |

One pouch holds up to 1,000 tiny objects and contributes 1 Load. Item BU, ordinary value and Load remain independent.

Projected vectors travel through intervening space. Minor obstruction imposes −2 accuracy; standard half cover −4; total cover blocks the trajectory. Evaluate actual angle/path. Direct manifestation appears at a target and ignores those intervening projected-cover penalties, but still requires sensory location or an authored targeting method. Cover does not automatically grant knowledge of a hidden target.
