# SwordWeave Player's Handbook Draft

Rules edition: **v0.1 alpha**. Working manuscript; incomplete and not yet a final PDF release.

## Begin a shared story

SwordWeave is a tabletop roleplaying game. Each player portrays a character: someone with a place in the world, things they can do and something they want. The Dungeon Master, also called the Game Master, describes the surroundings and portrays the other people and creatures you meet. Your choices and their consequences create the adventure together.

You can play around a table or meet online. Bring a character sheet, a way to roll dice and a way to record changes. The website can help build and track a character, but you can also play with a printed sheet and notes.

### Agree what you are playing

Before making characters, talk about the adventure. Are you explorers trying to reach a lost city, troublesome apprentices, people defending their home, or something else? Agree what kinds of situations you want to explore and which subjects should stay outside the game. Anyone can ask to pause or change an uncomfortable scene.

Ask the DM which starting level or Build Unit budget to use, which existing creations fit the setting and whether equipment has a separate allowance. A simple beginning helps a new group learn; a higher starting budget gives a more developed character. The table chooses the starting point together.

### Describe what you want to accomplish

The DM presents a situation. Describe your character's intent in ordinary words. You can ask what they see, hear or already know before committing to an attempt.

> **Example**
>
> DM: “Smoke rises through the stairwell. From upstairs, you hear someone coughing. The staircase is beginning to collapse.”
>
> Mira: “Where are the windows? Is there water nearby? I want to make a way for them to get out.”

An intent gives the conversation direction. You do not need a saved ability whose title describes the exact situation. Ordinary actions depend on the circumstances and your character's competence. Extraordinary workings use the permissions your character owns.

A **primitive** is a reusable rule or permission. A water-shaping character might own a domain permission for Water and a verb permission for changing its form. Those ingredients can support several ideas: draw water along a ledge, shape a barrier or redirect a stream. Each attempt still needs a workable scope and an agreed resolution.

### Give exciting ideas a chance

**Core rule — the Rule of Cool.** Give an exciting idea a fair hearing. Talk through what makes it possible, what it risks and how it will resolve. The rules help everyone understand the attempt and its consequences.

The table can agree an unusual application or exception when it makes the scene coherent and engaging. State that agreement clearly. Being able to reach a window through a collapsing roof can depend on the route, timing and danger, even if a similar jump was easy elsewhere.

Mira's group might decide that a rushing channel of water can support a brief rescue. Holding it open could restrict her other workings, require continuous attention or leave her unable to shape water for a while afterward. The cost can be mechanical, narrative or both. Vitality damage is one possible consequence among many.

### Agree the attempt before rolling

Work through these questions together:

1. **What is the intended result?** Name what you are trying to change or achieve.
2. **What makes it possible?** Check ordinary circumstances and any required permissions.
3. **What is the actual scope?** Describe the target, reach, output, timing and persistence.
4. **What is at stake?** Agree the difficulty, cost and relevant consequences, including what failure can mean.
5. **How will it resolve?** Decide whether it happens directly, needs a check or needs another suitable procedure.

You may accept those terms, narrow the attempt, spend more time or try another approach. Useful questions can prevent an unnecessary roll or reveal a better plan.

> **Example**
>
> Mira: “I want to pull water from the trough into a passage leading to the upstairs window.”
>
> DM: “Your Water and verb permissions support shaping the channel. Make it narrow enough for one person, and you can hold it while Sol helps them across. You will need to keep directing it; if you stop, it falls apart.”
>
> Mira: “Could I leave it standing and go inside?”
>
> DM: “That is a different working. We can discuss a lasting structure, or you can keep this version small and maintained.”

Neither working needs to match a catalogue title. Their differences affect access, effort and consequences.

### Read a die roll

Dice answer uncertain questions. A **d20** is a twenty-sided die. **1d4** means one four-sided die; **4d10** means roll four ten-sided dice and add their results.

For an ordinary check, roll a d20 and add the relevant bonus. A **DC**, or Difficulty Class, is the number the total must meet or beat. The DM chooses a suitable check and establishes what the result means. Straightforward, uncontested actions may happen without a roll.

Your check generally includes the relevant current attribute, training where it applies and other applicable modifiers. The sheet can display their combined bonus. Add that displayed total once.

> **Example**
>
> The DM asks Sol to cross an unstable ledge with a displayed bonus of +5 against DC 12. Sol rolls 9. The total is 14, so the attempt succeeds under the agreed terms.
>
> Sol does not add another proficiency bonus already included in that +5.

**Default — advantage and disadvantage.** Applicable sources stack according to their authored rules. Cancel opposing stacks one-for-one. Each remaining stack adds one resolution die: with two advantages, roll three dice and keep the highest; with two disadvantages, keep the lowest. If they cancel completely, roll one die. A particular primitive may state different stacking behavior.

### Carry the result forward

Apply the agreed outcome and describe the changed scene. Spend resources, change current Vitality or record a consequence when needed. A consequence should say what it does and what ends it. “Exhausted voice: cannot maintain sung workings until recovered” tells the table more than an unexplained condition name.

Other characters act from that new situation. In combat, the group coordinates a shared round through **Council, Fast, Measured and Heavy**. The combat chapter explains that rhythm; ordinary conversation and exploration do not need to be forced into a combat round.

### Recognize the kinds of guidance

Throughout this handbook, **Core rule** identifies the shared framework, and **Default** identifies the supplied procedure. **Authored rule** belongs to a particular primitive, item or working. **Optional guidance** offers a treatment your table can choose. An **Example** illustrates one situation, and an **App note** explains a digital control.

Tables can agree variations. Keep the actual agreement clear, especially when someone is learning the game or joining an established group.

## Make someone you want to play

### Begin with a person and a purpose

Write a short concept: who the character is, something they can do and what draws them into the adventure. Their history can grow through play.

Mira is a curious courier who learned to shape water while travelling with a river caravan. Her teacher has disappeared. She wants to find them and keeps getting distracted by people who need help.

That concept suggests useful permissions and a reason to act. It does not require a fixed class or a prescribed sequence of abilities.

### Choose the foundation

Your three attributes are **Physical**, **Mental** and **Magical**. Physical describes bodily approaches; Mental describes perception, thought and interaction; Magical describes extraordinary understanding and expression. Their numerical values are used directly in relevant checks and formulas.

**Default — starting attributes.** Allocate a total of 10 among the three base attributes, with each from −1 to +5. Later primitives can change the resulting values. Keep the base allocation distinct from those contributions.

A proficient attribute supports its associated practices and saves. Particular practice training can also be acquired through authored rules. Your choices should fit the concept; the foundation does not impose a class.

The ten practices are:

| Attribute | Practices |
| --- | --- |
| Physical | Prowess, Finesse, Fieldcraft |
| Mental | Awareness, Reason, Knowledge, Influence |
| Magical | Mysticism, Communion, Intuition |

You can attempt ordinary tasks without training. Training adds the applicable Proficiency Bonus; it is not permission to try the task at all. Relevant extraordinary permissions still matter.

### Buy useful permissions

**Build Units**, abbreviated **BU**, are the purchase budget for reusable primitives. A default level 1 foundation has 25 BU. Agree a different starting level or budget with the DM when appropriate.

A character foundation needs owned verb and domain access in its character build. A **verb tier** establishes the kind of change you can attempt; a **domain** establishes the subject you can work with. Read their actual definitions. Equipment cannot substitute for those foundation requirements.

When the corresponding categories are absent, Touch range and 1d4 output are free baselines. Further reach and different output dice can be acquired through suitable primitives. Larger output, several targets or unusual selectivity can still change an attempt's pressure and cost.

Prices in the Library and builder are suggestions and authored choices. You may invent a primitive, choose a price and discuss it with the DM. Conditions, limits and stacking can justify different prices. The DM can accept the proposal or ask you to adjust it before using it at their table.

> **Example — Mira's purchases**
>
> Mira's player chooses base attributes Physical 3, Mental 3 and Magical 4, with Magical as the proficient attribute. The player then builds this ledger using the current canonical entries:
>
> | Primitive | BU |
> | --- | ---: |
> | Verb Access Tier II | 8 |
> | Water through Domain Access Tier I | 4 |
> | Near Range | 4 |
> | d8 output permission | 4 |
> | Knowledge proficiency | 4 |
> | **Total purchased** | **24** |
> | **Unspent from 25** | **1** |
>
> The scope of the Water domain and verb permission still governs her workings. Near reaches up to 30 ft. Owning d8 permits that die type; the chosen number of dice and the particular result are discussed when she describes an action.

Mira's level 1 Proficiency Bonus is 2. Her trained Knowledge bonus is Mental 3 plus PB 2, for +5. If Magical is selected for her DC, the baseline is 5 + PB 2 + Magical 4 = **11**. Her baseline maximum Vitality is (10 + PB 2) × level 1 = **12**, before relevant modifiers.

This ledger teaches the accounting. Your character's prices and rules need the same table agreement.

### Organize your history and abilities

**Lineage** describes ancestry or species. **Upbringing** describes background and training. **Manifest** describes a role or current expression of the character. These heritages can organize primitives into a coherent foundation.

You can develop your own heritages, adapt an existing one or use several where the concept and table agreement support them. A heritage name alone grants no additional mechanics. Check the actual ingredients and count purchases appropriately.

An **effect** groups related outcomes or ingredients. A **capability** organizes a complete working or ready intent. Once you own its ingredients, describing another arrangement does not buy the same primitives again. A new arrangement still needs suitable permissions, scope and resolution.

### Keep items separate

Items have their own **Item BU** accounting. Agree an equipment allowance with the DM rather than assuming it is included in the character's 25 BU.

**Default — equipped slots.** You have six universal equipment slots. An ordinary equipped item uses one; a two-handed item uses two. A particular item can have a higher agreed requirement, and primitives can extend your available slots. Size does not automatically multiply that requirement.

A small courier might be allowed to wield an unusually large sword with a three-slot requirement or another consequence. Describe the adjustment on that item so everyone knows how to apply it.

Load tracks what you carry and remains separate from equipped slots and Item BU. An unequipped item can still contribute Load.

### Check that the character is ready

Before the first scene, make sure you can explain:

- Your character's purpose and how they connect to the party.
- Their base attributes, training and current check bonuses.
- The purchased verb and domain permissions that support their special actions.
- Purchased BU, unspent BU and separate equipment accounting.
- Current and maximum Vitality, equipped gear and any actual drawbacks.

Drawbacks are optional. Accept their real consequences before using any permitted mirror credit; a descriptive personality flaw does not automatically produce BU. Detailed mirroring and progression rules appear in the budget chapter.

**App note.** Quickbuild and complete creation both produce characters you can keep developing. A form may offer one slot for each heritage type as a convenience. That form does not replace the table's agreement about the character's concept.

## Read and compose your abilities

You have a concept and a purchase ledger. Now turn those ingredients into things you can explain and use. You can use something from the Library, adapt it or build your own. All three approaches use the same construction rules.

### Start with the intended result

Describe what you want in ordinary words before choosing a name or mechanical format. “Guide water from one vessel into another” gives the table something to judge. A title such as River Hand helps you find the working again, but its description and ingredients establish what it does.

Ask which parts of the result need extraordinary access. Moving an ordinary bucket can be an ordinary action. Shaping its contents from across a room requires the relevant permissions. A primitive can grant a permission, change a number or establish a particular rule; read its definition rather than relying on its icon or family name.

### Read the main building blocks

The Market organizes base primitives into families. Begin with the following questions:

| Question | Relevant foundations |
| --- | --- |
| What kind of change can I make? | Verb access |
| What can I work with? | Domain access |
| How is the working arranged or delivered? | Structure and targeting |
| How far does it reach? | Range |
| What output can I express? | Intensity and dice |
| How does it persist or respond? | Duration, triggers and particular authored rules |
| What changes on my sheet? | Attribute/practice grants, numerical modifiers, senses, mobility, defenses and other augmentations |

These questions are a way to read a working, not a requirement to purchase one primitive from every family. Use your baselines and owned rules, then identify any additional permission the proposed result actually needs.

**Default reference — access tiers.** The base verb, domain and structure families use four access tiers with suggested anchors of 4, 8, 12 and 16 BU. Their meanings differ by family. Verb Tier II describes changing existing states; Domain Tier II describes a different breadth of subjects. Owning one does not grant the other.

Range and output use their own ladders. Near Range permits 30 ft with a current suggested cost of 4 BU. The d8 output permission also has a current suggested cost of 4 BU, but it grants a die type, not distance. A shared price does not make two primitives interchangeable. The reference tables collect these base definitions; authors and the table can agree different prices for their actual rules.

### Make an effect, then a capability when useful

An **effect** groups related ingredients or outcomes so you can reuse them. A **capability** organizes a working, talent or ready intent. A capability can contain primitives directly and can also use effects. An effect need not be created as a separate card before every capability.

For each construction, record:

1. Its name and intended result.
2. The ingredients and what each contributes.
3. The recipient or target, scope and applicable conditions.
4. Any numerical change, output, trigger or duration it actually establishes.
5. Stacking, limits and ending conditions where they matter.
6. How it is proposed and resolved in play, including any agreed continuation requirement.

Keep a permanent acquired rule distinct from the temporary result of using it. Owning a water-shaping permission does not mean every water structure is permanent. Owning a die type does not automatically heal every injured target or establish resistance to incoming damage.

> **Worked construction — River Hand**
>
> Mira's player uses her Water domain and Verb Access Tier II to describe guiding and reshaping existing water. Near Range supports attempts up to 30 ft where she can locate the target. They organize these ingredients as an effect called Guided Water, then use it in a capability called River Hand.
>
> The capability says what Mira can propose: redirect a stream or guide water between vessels within her access and reach. It does not promise that every quantity, route or situation succeeds without a check. At the table, they establish the actual volume, obstacles, timing and consequences.
>
> Her d8 permission remains available for an appropriate damage or healing working; simply including it in a ledger does not turn moving water into an automatic attack or cure. She may compose another capability when she wants such a result.

### Keep one purchase ledger

Organizing the same acquired ingredient in several effects, capabilities or heritages does not repurchase it. Count the underlying acquisitions, including any deliberately acquired additional occurrences. Read each rule's restrictions before deciding that several appearances create several contributions.

Mira already owns all five purchases in her 24-BU ledger. Guided Water and River Hand organize some of those ingredients; they do not increase her total to 48 BU or produce extra free copies of the rules. If she later acquires a new ingredient, record that acquisition and its cost.

### Build a heritage around the person

A heritage connects mechanics to ancestry, experience or current expression. Choose **Lineage**, **Upbringing** or **Manifest** according to what the creation represents. Include the rules and capabilities that support its description, and identify the actual acquisitions it contains.

> **Worked construction — River Caravan Upbringing**
>
> Mira's player groups Verb Access Tier II (8 BU), Water domain access (4 BU) and Knowledge proficiency (4 BU) into an upbringing inspired by her training: **16 BU** of acquired primitives. Near Range and d8 output remain other purchases: **4 + 4 BU**. The whole character still costs **24 BU**, leaving **1** from her 25-BU budget.
>
> Another river-caravan character can choose different training and permissions. The heritage name does not make every member a water shaper or grant unlisted abilities.

You can adapt this upbringing or write a new one. A compelling biography can remain descriptive; mechanical benefits need their actual rules. Likewise, a descriptive flaw becomes a purchased drawback only through its agreed mechanical or narrative terms and the applicable mirror rules.

### Write a new primitive when your idea needs one

First state the rule in readable language. Identify who receives it, what it permits or changes, when it applies, how it stacks and any limit. Propose a BU price and explain the scope or restrictions behind it. Review it with the DM before relying on it in a build.

A useful review asks whether two readers would apply the rule the same way. “See heat” needs a scope: what sense is granted, what it can distinguish, what blocks it and whether it locates a target well enough for a working. “Gain a bonus while wet” needs the affected check or metric, the amount, the meaning of wet and its stacking treatment.

You do not need a catalogue entry whose name exactly matches your concept. The base families provide vocabulary and examples for authoring. Their suggested prices help the conversation; the author and table settle the actual rule and price.

### Agree consequences as part of the working

Consequences are part of play and can also be part of an authored rule. They can change numbers, restrict an action, consume a resource, affect the surroundings or create a narrative obligation. The same condition name can describe different consequences in different situations.

Before committing, agree what the consequence means and when it applies. A clear record includes:

| Detail | Example of an agreed consequence |
| --- | --- |
| Source and affected subject | Mira maintains a difficult water channel. |
| Actual change | She must keep directing it and cannot use this same working for a second channel at the same time. |
| When it applies | While she continues maintaining this channel. |
| How it ends | Ending the working releases the restriction and the unsupported water falls away. |
| Recovery or follow-up | If the attempt causes another lasting consequence, record its own ending or recovery terms. |

This is an example agreement, not a universal limit of one channel or one maintained capability. Other circumstances can support another scope, cost or continuation requirement.

If the agreed cost is Vitality, record the amount and timing. If it is lost access until a task is completed, record that task. A new round or rest does not erase a consequence whose stated ending has not occurred. Agree lasting injuries and other special recovery needs explicitly.

An attempt can have a small or negligible cost when the situation supports it. Ambition, urgency and danger affect the appraisal. **BU buys reusable mechanics; execution has its own agreed consequences.** Do not spend BU every time you use a purchased primitive unless a particular agreed rule establishes a different purchase or consumption arrangement.

### Try the construction in a scene

Test more than its most favorable use. Ask what happens when the target is hidden, the proposed result is larger, the actor is interrupted or two rules contribute at once. Clarify missing terms before the construction becomes hard to apply during a tense scene.

Mira can guide nearby water in a calm room without assuming the same outcome for a flooded, collapsing stairwell. The latter adds moving obstacles, frightened people and urgency. Her access remains the foundation; the table appraises this attempt and agrees its resolution.

**App note.** The Library provides examples and the Atelier helps organize and inspect creations. Keep the actual ingredient definitions with an offline character record. A saved card is convenient, but the table's agreed rules and the character's acquisitions determine what is available.

## Resolve uncertainty

You do not roll because you named a practice. You roll when the result is uncertain and the difference matters. Explain how you approach the problem. The DM and players establish the relevant attribute or practice, the opposition or DC, and what the possible outcomes mean.

**Default check:** roll d20 + the relevant current attribute + applicable training + applicable modifiers. Meet or beat the agreed DC. Use the attribute's actual value; Physical 3 contributes +3, not a converted score modifier.

If your sheet already shows a combined practice bonus, add that bonus once. Mira's trained Knowledge is Mental 3 + PB 2 = +5. A roll of 9 gives 14. Against DC 12, that succeeds. Adding Mental and PB again would incorrectly give 19.

### Ten practices, many approaches

| Attribute | Practice | Ways it might matter |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Physical | Prowess | Force, exertion or holding a contested position. |
| Physical | Finesse | Precise movement, careful manipulation or concealment through movement. |
| Physical | Fieldcraft | Practical survival, handling hazards or stabilizing an injured companion. |
| Mental | Awareness | Noticing relevant details or following a visible development. |
| Mental | Reason | Analysis, deduction or understanding a mechanism. |
| Mental | Knowledge | Recalling learned information or applying a familiar discipline. |
| Mental | Influence | Persuading, negotiating or expressing a credible threat. |
| Magical | Mysticism | Understanding or applying extraordinary workings. |
| Magical | Communion | Relating to or communicating through an appropriate extraordinary connection. |
| Magical | Intuition | Interpreting an extraordinary impression or choosing through granted insight. |

These are examples of approaches, not exhaustive permission lists. Ordinary attempts remain available without training. Extraordinary results require their actual access. A social check does not automatically give control over another character's decisions.

**Default.** Proficiency adds PB where applicable. Expertise uses twice PB when granted. A repeated grant of the same training does not itself add another PB. A separate explicit numerical bonus follows its own conditions and stacking rules.

PB starts at 2. Its baseline is 2 + floor((level − 1) / 4), before active modifiers. The explicit floor is part of this formula; the ordinary rounding rule elsewhere is upward.

### Attacks, saves and your DC

An attack tests whether an attempted attack succeeds. A saving throw tests an actor's resistance to an effect. Your working's actual delivery and the table's resolution determine which is appropriate; using a capability does not demand both an attack and a saving throw by default.

**Default.** Your selected-attribute DC is 5 + current PB + the chosen eligible current modified attribute + direct DC modifiers. Mira's Magical 4 and PB 2 give DC 11. Physical, Mental and Magical saving throws remain separate values. They do not imply three different character DCs. Use the relevant save and its actual granted training or modifiers.

Read authored result floors, ceilings and fixed-result rules at their stated scope. A minimum check result does not raise the attribute itself. An attribute increase can instead affect every derived number based on it. Do not add an automatic natural-20 success, natural-1 failure or critical-damage multiplier unless the table or an authored rule has established one.

### Advantage can stack

**Default.** Apply the sources' conditions and uniqueness rules, then cancel advantage and disadvantage one-for-one. Each remaining stack adds one resolution die. Roll N + 1 dice for N net stacks: keep the highest for advantage and the lowest for disadvantage. With none remaining, roll one die. Add the applicable static bonus once after selecting the die.

**Examples.** Two advantages and one disadvantage leave one advantage: roll 2d20, highest. Two advantages without opposition give 3d20, highest. Two disadvantages give 3d20, lowest. Stacking does not bypass a primitive that expressly forbids repeated contributions.

## Act together in combat

A combat round is a shared problem. Your party can coordinate a plan, resolve simpler actions before dependent ones and respond when the situation changes. SwordWeave uses **Council → Fast → Measured → Heavy**. There is no default initiative roll or imported bonus-action sequence.

### Council: make the plan understandable

Describe one coherent Main Intent. Coordinate with your allies and establish which actions depend on another result. The DM describes what the opposition is visibly attempting. Check access, timing, scope and stakes before commitment.

**Default tracks:** Fast resolves Complexity 0–1; Measured resolves 2–3; Heavy resolves 4+. Complexity concerns timing dependencies, not a ranking of damage or power. A straightforward powerful strike need not become Heavy merely because it has large output. Agree a rushed or altered timing with its actual access and consequences.

Track where the round leaves everyone. When a path collapses in Fast, an action planned for Heavy must use the changed route rather than an imaginary unchanged battlefield.

### Clashes and contests

Use a **Reaction Clash** when opposing intents meet within a track and arriving first matters. Roll d20 + relevant attribute + PB if trained, with applicable rules. The higher result acts first. On a tie, the GM resolves simultaneously or follows the stronger fictional position.

Use an **Active Contest** when efforts directly oppose: holding a door against someone forcing it open, or slipping past a watchful opponent. Agree a suitable approach for each side. Use the relevant combined practice bonus once where a practice applies. The higher result wins the immediate struggle.

Losing timing does not automatically erase your Main Intent. If a guard rings a bell before you seize their arm, the alarm has sounded; seizing the arm may still be possible. If your plan has become impossible, pivot: redirect it, reduce the scope or accept another sensible consequence before resolution.

### One independent reaction

**Default.** You have one Reaction Slot each round, reset at Council. A reaction needs a clear event you can perceive and an available response you can perform. Its default scope is immediate, Complexity 0–1, and self/touch or one target. Relevant access and position still matter. A larger response needs a specific permission or explicit table ruling.

Pause the triggering event, resolve the reaction, then continue. A reaction can occur before or after your Main Intent. It does not automatically cancel the event it responds to.

**Example.** Neri catches an ally slipping beside them. Being nearby and able to catch them establishes the opportunity. Saving everyone falling across a distant broken bridge is a different scope; spending a Reaction Slot alone does not grant that rescue.

### Move across the round

**Default.** Your Speed is movement available across the whole round. Spend some or all, divide it among tracks or valid reactions, and include it in an intent or move independently where the situation permits. Beginning a later track or using a reaction does not give a fresh allowance.

**Example.** Speed 30 ft supports moving 10 ft in Fast and 20 ft in Heavy. Moving farther needs an appropriate speed primitive or an agreed Cost/consequence. Actual routes, interference and hazards still apply; reach is not permission to pass through a wall.

| Size | Walking Speed | Default swimming/climbing |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Tiny | 15 ft | 8 ft |
| Small | 25 ft | 13 ft |
| Medium | 30 ft | 15 ft |
| Large | 40 ft | 20 ft |
| Huge | 60 ft | 30 ft |
| Gargantuan | 90 ft | 45 ft |

Swimming/climbing default to half base speed rounded up. Authored rules can change these values. Flight and burrowing require their relevant grants.

### A bridge round

**Example.** Mira will restrain a guard's alarm arm; Sol will build a supported path across a gap; Neri will move an injured friend once the path exists. At Council they agree access, movement, timing and what failure risks. The guard reaches for the bell while another opponent attacks the support.

In Fast, Mira clashes with the guard. Suppose the guard wins timing: the bell rings, and Mira can still try to restrain them if possible. Damage to the support changes Sol's construction. In Measured, Sol completes a smaller path at the agreed continuing cost. Neri uses that path in the agreed order. A nearby slip can create a valid reaction opportunity; a more elaborate dependent attempt resolves in Heavy.

Before the next Council, record damage, costs, current positions, continuing requirements and unresolved consequences. The party crossed, but pursuers now know where to look. Success at the crossing did not erase the alarm.

**Optional guidance: surprise.** There is no universal surprise subsystem. A table might let prepared enemies complete a round before surprised participants respond. The circumstances or an agreed d20 check may change that treatment. An ambush does not automatically entitle every enemy to a free round.

## Push the limits

Owned access establishes what you can propose. The particular attempt establishes how much pressure it creates. d10 output and Very Far reach do not make four dice against a distant crowd as effortless as one die at Touch range. Describe the chosen output, recipients, area, distance, duration and timing; the table appraises the whole result.

### Scale, impact and complexity

Scale asks how much is affected. Impact asks how strongly the result changes it. Complexity asks how the working's parts depend on each other. Environment, opposition, available support and urgency matter too.

Raising dice count, area, targets or duration does not require a separate acquisition for every dial by default. It can increase Strain and Cost. Adding a new behavior, such as healing allies inside an enemy-harming blast, may require additional access and a different construction. Neither a bigger die nor a higher payment silently grants the behavior.

**Core rule.** Locate a specific target through a relevant sense. Vision, granted blindsight, tremorsense or another suitable method can establish its location. Range alone does not reveal it. Without that location, propose targeting a known spot or area and resolve uncertainty; the desired creature might not be there.

An authored working can establish another targeting method, including scrying, broad weather control or any qualifying target within a stated range. Agree its actual scope and exceptions. Extreme reach can extend by DM agreement, but extra distance does not supply knowledge of the target.

### Agree a Cost before committing

Strain is a 0–6 appraisal guide, from effortless through light effort, noticeable strain, heavy burden, dangerous instability, critical risk and catastrophic consequence. A grade is not an automatic Vitality bill or a fixed chance of success.

Cost can be Vitality, a resource, a numerical penalty, exposure, a restriction, environmental change or a narrative consequence. It may be negligible. Agree what applies, when it applies and what failure means. Interruption has no universal refund rule.

**Example.** Heating a wet lock can scorch a tool or exhaust the actor. Setting a roof ablaze can spread fire into neighboring homes. Smaller scope, help or more time can change the proposed stakes. Ask for those alternatives before accepting a cost you did not intend.

**Optional guidance: CV.** A table can compare evaluated construction values. If minimum composition value B is 12 and the selected use A is 30, the scaling increase is A − B = 18; total CV is 30. These illustrate arithmetic, not a universal spell price. CV is not added to a roll and is not acquisition BU paid again. Numerical comparison supports appraisal; it does not capture every difference in environment or behavior.

## Keep a working alive

When a working resolves, how long its result lasts, and what sustains it are three separate questions. Track answers the first, duration the second, upkeep the third. A fixed duration does not automatically require active maintenance.

**Example.** A flame can flash and end, remain for two rounds without further effort, or last while the actor sustains it. Write which construction applies. Continuing a working can spread its pressure over time; there is no compulsory numerical discount from doing so.

### Ongoing consequences

Agree the maintenance requirement, its payment point within the shared rhythm and what happens if it cannot be met. Council is a useful agreed continuation point. A 4-Vitality requirement paid this round can be due again next round while the working continues. A loss of access until a task is completed instead lasts according to that ending condition. New rounds do not silently remove it.

**Default.** One maintained capability has one execution, one Strain appraisal and one upkeep track, even if it contains multiple effects. You may maintain several capabilities; their actual requirements accumulate. There is no default single-concentration limit.

A hit does not universally stop a working. Singing continuously may be threatened by silence. Burning incense may be threatened by losing the incense. An unconscious actor cannot continue a task they can no longer perform. Determine interference from the actual requirement, position, timing and consequence.

**Draft decision pending.** Additional damage-triggered repayment when upkeep is measured in Vitality is awaiting the creator's decision. No damage-counter reset or excess-damage policy is adopted in this draft. Ordinary continuation requirements remain available by table agreement.

### Interfere with unfinished work

Describe a real way to interfere and establish the opportunity before the action resolves. Reach the actor, break an essential support, disrupt a required circle or use a suitable countering working. A reaction also needs its available slot and valid scope. Resolve the relevant attack, save, clash or contest; apply the agreed failure, partial result or continued execution.

**Example.** A gate needs an intact chalk circle. Scattering it before completion can threaten the actual construction. Missing the attempt does not stop the gate. Hitting its maker need not stop it if no agreed requirement is disrupted. Once an instant flash has resolved, its execution can no longer be interrupted retroactively; a resulting ongoing effect may still be disrupted through relevant access.

## Survive and carry consequences

Vitality measures health, stamina and exertion. There is no universal separate mana pool or spell-slot track. Player baseline maximum Vitality is **(10 + current PB) × level**, then active maximum-Vitality rules. Current Vitality is a separate value; raising the maximum does not itself grant healing.

### Damage has a source and domain

Physical, Magical and Psychic describe execution sources; fire, cold or another domain describes identity. Mental is an attribute, not an interchangeable name for Psychic source. Read the actual defense's source/domain scope.

**Default.** Resistance halves covered damage, rounded up; vulnerability doubles it; immunity negates damage within its stated scope. Multiple resistances do not quarter damage by default. Applicable resistance and vulnerability on the same instance cancel. Damage immunity does not automatically negate every associated consequence.

**Example.** Nine Magical Fire damage becomes 5 with matching resistance, 18 with matching vulnerability, or 9 when both apply. A mixed execution dealing 6 Physical Fire and 8 Magical Fire loses only 4 from its Magical portion under Magical-only resistance: total loss is 10. Establish whether mixed domains split one output or produce separate instances before applying defenses.

Apply the final damage as Vitality loss. Healing restores Vitality within the current maximum. **App note.** Manual damage fields do not automatically apply typed defenses; resolve them first.

### Collapse is a rescue problem

**Default.** At zero Vitality, collapse leaves the character unconscious and incapacitated. The GM establishes contextual rescue stakes. Roughly a minute is guidance, not ten guaranteed safe rounds. Severe wounds or a hostile environment can shorten the opportunity.

Allies can dedicate an action track to Fieldcraft stabilization or a suitable creative use of owned primitives. The table agrees access, resolution and Cost; there is no universal stabilization DC. Successful stabilization halts the rescue countdown. It does not itself restore Vitality or wake the character. Healing above zero ends the dying state and restores consciousness unless another consequence prevents it. A stabilized character can recover through assisted rest; an actively dying character needs rescue first.

**Massive damage.** A single execution dealing at least twice maximum Vitality, or taking current Vitality to −maximum Vitality, can cause immediate death under the massive-damage rule. Establish lethal stakes before resolution. Use final damage after defenses and the current value before the hit; a sheet storing zero must not hide the overrun.

**Example.** Maximum 13, current 4 and final damage 17 gives 4 − 17 = −13. This reaches the negative-maximum boundary even though the blow is below 26. A blow of 26 reaches twice maximum independently. The contextual collapse rescue clock does not reverse an outright lethal result.

### Recovery is agreed, not automatic

A short rest is a pause, perhaps 15 or 30 minutes. A long rest is overnight sleep or an equivalent meaningful recovery period, not a mandatory eight-hour timer. DM and players agree whether the circumstances earn either rest's effects. Elapsed time alone does not apply them.

**Default.** Short rests share a finite allowance of half maximum Vitality rounded up between completed long rests. Only actual points restored consume it. Retain unused points for later agreed short rests. A completed long rest normally restores Vitality to maximum and refreshes that allowance; its healing is not charged against the short-rest pool.

| Example: maximum 13 | Actual recovery | Short-rest allowance left |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Begin after a long rest | — | ceil(13 / 2) = 7 |
| At 10/13, agree a short rest | 3, reaching 13/13 | 4 |
| Later at 9/13, agree another short rest | 4, reaching 13/13 | 0 |
| Complete the next long rest | Normal full recovery | 7, refreshed |

The DM can adjust recovery by agreement. After poor sleep the table might allow 70% rather than full restoration; apply ordinary upward rounding when the chosen calculation needs it. A completed long rest still refreshes the short-rest allowance. Record the agreed amount rather than quietly assuming every rest is equally restorative.

Injuries, curses, access restrictions and other lasting consequences keep their own recovery requirements. Healing a burned character's Vitality does not automatically repair the destroyed map, remove a specific injury or satisfy an obligation. State what remains and how it can change.

## Equipment and growth

An item can be an ordinary tool, a carrier for capabilities or a source of active modifications. Record what it actually provides, what must be equipped, its Item BU, Load and slot requirement. Describing a sword as enormous does not by itself write all those rules.

**Default.** You have six universal equipment slots. An ordinary equipped item uses one, a two-handed item uses two. A primitive can extend your slots. An agreed unusual item may require more or impose another consequence: a small character using an oversized sword might accept a 3–4-slot requirement. Size alone is not an automatic slot multiplier.

Load measures carrying; slots measure use. Equipped items still contribute Load. Capacity is size base + 5 × current Physical + applicable capacity bonuses. Load above capacity is encumbered; agree the resulting penalties or complications.

| Size | Base capacity | Ordinary Load per item |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Tiny | 10 | Pouch rule |
| Small | 20 | 1 |
| Medium | 40 | 2 |
| Large | 80 | 4 |
| Huge | 160 | 8 |
| Gargantuan | 320 | 16 |

One pouch holds up to 1,000 tiny items and contributes 1 Load. A backpack organizes contents; changing capacity requires an actual rule. Use an item's explicit Load rule where granted. Keep purchase price or treasure value separate from Item BU and Load.

### How an attack reaches a target

Projected vectors travel through space: arrows, beams or thrown blasts. Minor obstruction imposes −2 accuracy; ordinary half cover −4; total cover blocks the trajectory. Decide from the actual angle and path.

A direct manifestation appears at its target and ignores those intervening projected-cover penalties. It still needs a legitimate sensory location or authored targeting method. A wall does not become permission to locate an unseen foe. A visible head above cover may be reachable by a direct manifestation while the same barrier interferes with a traveling bolt.
