# SwordWeave Game Master's Guide Draft

Rules edition: **v0.1 alpha**. Working manuscript. This chapter begins the guide; it is not the complete book or a final PDF.

## Review creations and choose consequences

SwordWeave gives players a construction language. Your job is to help the table understand what a creation does, how it fits the world and what a particular attempt puts at risk. An unusual idea is an invitation to examine the situation. Begin with the result the player wants.

**Core rule.** Establish access, actual scope, stakes, resolution and consequences before commitment. Let the player clarify or revise their intent. Apply the agreed outcome, then continue from the changed situation. The Rule of Cool supports coherent and exciting exceptions; state their scope so an exceptional moment does not accidentally become an unrelated permanent ability.

### A review you can use at the table

Read a proposed component with its author. Ask these questions of the definition; do not make a player fill out a form to perform every ordinary action.

| Review | What you need to understand |
| --- | --- |
| Result | What changes, who benefits and who or what can be affected? |
| Permission | Which owned ingredients support the change? Is any proposed behavior absent from them? |
| Conditions | When does it apply? What prevents it? What does a broad phrase such as “protected” mean here? |
| Composition | Which ingredients are actual acquisitions and which are references to purchases already counted? |
| Stacking | Do additional occurrences apply? Do sources share a uniqueness restriction? Is a grant being confused with another numerical bonus? |
| Use | How does the actor perform it, and what uncertainty requires resolution? |
| Continuation | Does it end immediately, last for a stated duration, or require maintenance? What can interfere? |
| Price | What suggested BU acquisition price fits this table's scope and restrictions? Keep Item BU separate. |

An author's price is a proposal. Library and builder suggestions are useful comparisons. You may accept the proposal or ask for an adjustment, including a cheaper version if its limits make the original price excessive. Explain which part creates the concern. “It affects every target without discrimination or effort” is actionable feedback; “that sounds overpowered” leaves the author guessing.

Keep acquisition and execution separate. A purchased ingredient can support many uses without being bought again. A broad or hurried use can still incur Strain and agreed Cost. Buying another ingredient is one way to establish missing access; increasing effort alone does not silently grant a new behavior.

### Review a water-working

**Example.** Mira owns Verb II, a Water domain permission, Near Range and d8 output. She proposes redirecting ordinary water away from a broken canal gate. Read the actual permissions. Check the water's quantity, distance, resistance and where it will go. Moving water is one question; creating a new permanent gate, locating an unseen saboteur or healing a wounded worker are different results.

For a small flow with time to work, you might agree negligible Cost and no roll. In a collapsing sluice, redirecting the whole surge before it reaches the workers is a demanding attempt. Make its reach, recipients, timing and stakes explicit. Mira can narrow the channel she controls, enlist another character or accept a continuing restriction instead of forcing the whole result at once.

Do not charge her again for the Water permission because it appears in both an effect and its parent capability. Do not assume the d8 ingredient makes every manipulation deal damage. Output applies when an appropriate authored construction and the proposed use establish damage or healing.

### Write a consequence people can use

A consequence should change decisions at the table. It can be damage, a mechanical penalty, a restriction, lost access, an obligation, exposure, an environmental change or another narrative result. A number is useful where the result is numerical. An understandable restriction is useful where a number would hide what happened.

**Core rule.** Record the actual consequence, its cause, applicable timing or trigger and how it ends. A familiar name does not import another game's condition package.

| Record | Example: holding the diverted channel |
| --- | --- |
| Cause | Mira keeps the surge diverted while the workers repair the gate. |
| Change | She must keep directing that channel and cannot use this working for a separate water manipulation at the same time. |
| Applies | While she continues the channel; the table agrees its place in the shared round. |
| Interference | Losing the necessary access or being unable to continue the directing task threatens it. A wound alone is not a universal cancellation rule. |
| End/recovery | The restriction ends when she releases the channel or the repair removes the need for it. |

This is a possible ruling for this scene. It does not establish a universal one-capability limit. Several maintained capabilities can coexist; their agreed requirements and costs accumulate. Multiple effects inside one maintained capability share its one upkeep track and one Strain appraisal.

Contrast three possible costs before choosing one:

- **Mechanical:** an agreed amount of Vitality or a stated numerical penalty.
- **Restrictive:** hands, voice, attention or access must remain committed to the task.
- **Narrative:** the diversion floods a storehouse, draws attention or leaves a debt to the workers whose route is blocked.

These may combine, but piling on every category is not an automatic measure of fairness. Match the actual effort and scene. Offer a smaller scope or a different approach when a player dislikes the proposed trade-off. Maintaining a working can spread pressure across time and generally be cheaper upfront than forcing a larger result in one execution; there is no compulsory discount formula.

**Draft decision pending.** The creator is still considering additional repayment triggered by incoming damage when upkeep is paid in Vitality. Do not infer a damage counter/reset policy from these examples. Establish ordinary ongoing requirements with the table; the deferred numerical policy must be resolved before the final core release.

### Construct opposition for a purpose

Start with the situation the creature changes. A guard needs a reason to stand in the way. A predator needs a method of hunting. A support creature needs allies and an opportunity to support them. Numerical budget alone does not supply those choices.

**Default creature foundation.** For base creature budget B, rank is sqrt(B / 25), base attribute points are ceil(3 × rank), PB is 1 + ceil(rank), and baseline Vitality is max(1, ceil(B / 2)). These are creature foundations; do not substitute them for player character formulas. Resolve authored modifiers, practice allocation and explicit agreed baselines after establishing the foundation.

**Example construction: Canal Watcher.** A watcher protects the sluice against deliberate damage. At B = 25 it has rank 1, three attribute points, PB 2 and baseline Vitality 13. Assign Physical 2, Mental 0 and Magical 1. Verb II (8), Water domain I (4), Near Range (4) and d8 (4) spend 20 creature BU, leaving 5. This example has no equipment and no mirror credit. It redirects water within the actual recorded permissions. Decide its practice allocation and record complete bound definitions before treating this ledger as a ready-to-run creature sheet.

The remaining 5 is unused budget, not an unnamed attack or defense. Its guardian identity supplies motivation; it does not silently add armor, immunity, area damage or another turn. Give it understandable senses, movement and behavior supported by the record or the table's agreed ordinary circumstances.

### Prepare an encounter people can resolve several ways

**Example situation: the jammed sluice.** Two Canal Watchers believe opening the damaged gate will flood their protected spawning beds. Workers believe leaving it shut will send water through the settlement. The party needs to release the pressure and get the workers clear.

Prepare the following facts:

- The watchers protect the beds; they will hear a credible repair proposal.
- The stuck mechanism can be reached from the bank, while approaching across the wet channel is exposed.
- Workers know where the maintenance route leads. Asking them gives the party a practical alternative.
- Rising water changes available routes. Describe visible warning signs before a route becomes unusable.
- Success might be a repair, a negotiated diversion, evacuation or defeating the watchers. Damage is one possible tool.

Two 25-BU watchers give 50 enemy creature BU. Their Item BU is zero. A party with 50 Party BU and 8 Party Item BU totals 58. The difference is −8 from enemy to party; the ratio is 50/58. If there are two party members, counts match. Half the enemy creature budget is concentrated in each watcher.

Those facts are useful. They do not establish an easy or fair fight. A water specialist guarding a narrow channel may have a strong position against creatures that cannot cross it. An agreement about the spawning beds may make combat unnecessary. Review control, area effects, support, burst output, information, terrain and objectives rather than attaching an invented difficulty label to the ratio.

### Run and record the changed situation

Use Council → Fast → Measured → Heavy. At Council, make opposition intent legible and let the party coordinate. Place actions by their timing dependencies, using Complexity 0–1 for Fast, 2–3 for Measured and 4+ for Heavy. A heavier output is not automatically a later track.

When opposing intents meet, choose the relevant resolution: a clash for whose action arrives first, a contest for whose effort prevails, or another agreed check/attack/save. A valid reaction can intervene through its actual trigger, scope and available slot; it does not automatically cancel an event.

Record current/maximum Vitality, movement used, reaction availability, active consequences, continuing requirements and the resulting positions. Review what actually changed before the next Council. Advancing a digital phase does not itself deal damage, clear a consequence or expire a working.

If the watchers are driven back, they may retreat toward the beds. If Mira redirects the channel, ask where the water now goes. If the party evacuates the workers, the gate problem still remains. Consequences keep a scene moving; they need not close every possible approach.

**App note.** A template is a reusable definition. A private play copy records one creature in play. Encounter runs can reuse a prepared lineup without sharing those creatures' current damage. Linked player sheets provide budget references and authorized sheet links; reading a sheet does not give the GM editing permission.
