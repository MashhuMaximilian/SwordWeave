# SwordWeave book content and shared coverage

The three books share rules but serve different reading needs. The PHB teaches a player to build and play. The GM Guide teaches running, preparing and creating. The SRD supplies a complete, reusable reference to the designated open system: precise mechanics, base primitive families/tiers and construction procedures.

Creator clarification, 10 October 2026: the current books focus on building your own content. The 100-monster appendix and large ready-made heritage/item catalogues are not required deliverables. This supersedes the earlier appendix proposal. See [the comparison of Pathfinder, Call of Cthulhu/BRP, Daggerheart, Fate and Cairn](comparative-publication-research.md).

## Published examples of SRD scope

Checked 10 October 2026:

- The official [D&D SRD page](https://www.dndbeyond.com/srd) describes an openly licensed foundation for creators. Its [SRD 5.2.1](https://media.dndbeyond.com/compendium-images/srd/5.2/SRD_CC_v5.2.1.pdf) includes classes, origins, feats, equipment, spells, GM procedures, magic items and monster statblocks. It is more than a synopsis of the rules.
- In D&D's core-book arrangement, classes are principally in the [Player's Handbook](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/phb-2024); the broad creature catalogue is in the [Monster Manual](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/mm-2024); the [Dungeon Master's Guide](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/dmg-2024) covers running games, creating adventures/campaigns and related tools. The SRD draws reusable content from across that arrangement rather than serving as a fourth unrelated system.
- [Fate Core's SRD](https://fate-srd.com/fate-core) includes character creation, actions/conflicts, running the game, scenarios and advancement. An SRD need not copy D&D's class/bestiary model to be a complete reference.

These examples inform scope and organization. SwordWeave keeps its own mechanics, terminology and free-book policy.

## SwordWeave coverage

| Content | Player's Handbook | GM Guide | SRD |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Character creation and progression | Full player procedure and a few worked constructions | Custom starting conditions, pacing and rewards | Exact rules, tables, base definitions and checked examples |
| Primitives, effects, capabilities and heritages | Base primitive families/tiers, their meanings and flexible composition | Authoring/reviewing components, suggested pricing and judging their use | Base family/tier reference, definitions, construction grammar and a few examples |
| Checks, targeting, combat, movement and reactions | All player procedures with examples | Coordinating scenes and adjudicating opposition | Same procedures in concise reference form |
| Strain, cost, upkeep and consequences | How to propose, agree and track them | Detailed situational rulings and contrasting examples | Framework and defaults sufficient to run play |
| Damage, rescue, healing and rests | Complete player rules | Contextual rescue/recovery situations | Same mechanics and recovery definitions |
| Equipment | Item construction, use/accounting and a worked example | Creating scenario equipment and useful rewards | Item construction/accounting rules and necessary base definitions |
| Creatures and encounters | Reading relevant creature rules and a few play examples | Building your own creatures, tactics and encounter situations | Construction formulas, encounter rules, a reusable record structure and a few checked examples |
| Stories and campaign advice | Player-facing examples | Extended preparation and optional tools | Essential adjudication plus sufficient examples; identify advice clearly |

No player rule is exclusive to the SRD. A player using only the PHB must have the necessary procedure. The SRD can be shorter in its explanations while containing broader reference content for creators and GMs. The GM Guide's essential adjudication is also available in the open reference.

## Base primitives and release boundaries

Publish the base construction vocabulary with actual definitions and tier meanings, not only category names or links to the Library. Show how to combine it into effects, capabilities, lineages/upbringings/manifests, items and creatures. Use a few checked examples with enough detail to reproduce and adapt them; a large catalogue of finished options is outside this task.

The current Market source groups 29 families into construction language, resolution/expression, character foundation, system/reality and additional families. This is an inventory starting point, not a mandate to print every bound expression. Explain the principal categories and include the canonical base definitions necessary for readers to build their own content. Treat “classes/families” here as primitive classifications, not fixed character classes.

For example, verb/domain/structure access uses I–IV tiers and current 4/8/12/16-BU anchors. Range and dice have their own ladders; they do not inherit one universal tier/price progression. Preserve the difference between access, output, scope and authoring cost guidance. Reconcile the accepted range clarification with older source wording before publication; do not silently rewrite pinned catalogue versions.

The 100 seeded monsters and existing heritages remain optional platform resources. Use selected examples where they teach a construction step; no Monster Manual, exhaustive heritage collection or community-fork catalogue is required for the PHB/GM/SRD release.

Worked examples must identify the source versions used for verification, ingredient definitions, actual suggested BU costs, conditions and stacking. Published prices are authored suggestions subject to the table's approval. Do not invent resolved numbers by copying an incomplete UI card. Platform links add convenience; they do not replace necessary base definitions in the books.

Only use material with established publication/reuse rights. The publicly readable community catalogue is not automatically a pool of text/art we can relicense. State designated open rules/content and separate artwork/asset notices in each release. The three core digital books and their necessary content remain free.
