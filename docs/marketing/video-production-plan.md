# SwordWeave video production plan

**Prepared:** 20 October 2026
**Status:** Preproduction brief and shooting guide. A short synthetic-voice audition was prepared separately at the founder's request; it is not a finished video or approved website audio. No actual tutorial footage, full edit or timed captions exists yet.
**Quality decision:** The founder rejects videos that look or sound like generic AI output. A tool or installed skill cannot establish that a video is good enough to publish.

## Voice auditions · 20 October

A different expressive speech engine was tested using existing free workspace credits. Its first conversational take still measured about 147 words per minute and failed the pace check. ## The first deliverable is a finished sample

Make a **20–30 second finished excerpt from the primitive tutorial** before producing a full video. It must use actual SwordWeave footage, the proposed voice, the final caption treatment and a representative explanation. The separately requested synthetic-voice audition can test a sound preference; it does not approve the finished production or a website release. A rough storyboard or silent animatic does not approve the edit.

The sample should show **Result → Rolls & checks → Action Roll → Attack Roll**, explain why this is narrower than all rolls, and hold the selected result long enough to read. It tests the difficult work: making a dense editor understandable. Use the sample script in [Create a primitive](video-scripts/create-a-primitive.md).

The founder reviews the sample for voice, pacing, legibility and tone. One unfamiliar reader checks whether they can identify the selected result without coaching. Revise the sample in one consolidated pass. If it still feels artificial or confusing, stop that production approach and change it before recording the full series. Nothing goes on the website merely because it has been generated or exported.

## Voice and performance

**Founder preference:** Record their own voice using **talking points and the real workflow**, rather than reading a fixed script aloud. Prepare a few key facts for each shot, let the founder explain them naturally, then edit for clarity. A natural accent is welcome; do not erase it to imitate an advertisement. Exact sample dialogue is an optional editing aid, not founder-approved wording.

| Route | What it requires | Review condition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Founder voice — primary | Numbered talking points, a quiet recording, pronunciation decisions and a few pickups | Founder is comfortable with the performance; an unfamiliar listener can follow it |
| Another human narrator | Explicit participation and agreed rights, pronunciation direction, pickups | Founder approves the narrator and any cost before commissioning |
| Synthetic voice test | Explicit founder choice of a service/voice and any account or cost; honest disclosure | Sample passes the same quality review; it is not represented as the founder speaking |

No voice cloning, imitation of a real person, invented founder quotation, paid subscription or service signup is authorized by this plan. Synthetic narration must not imply a person endorsed or performed it. AI assistance in scripting/editing should be described accurately; it is separate from the choice of narrator. The founder specifically requested a short synthetic-voice test, but has not approved synthetic voice for website use. Short demonstrations may be caption-only when the task stays understandable without speech. The complex mechanical-rule tutorial should use the founder's explanation unless another approach passes an explicit review; keep the written tutorial available while that recording is pending.

Direct the performance sentence by sentence:

- Speak to one curious player, with a reason for each click. Prefer “This changes attack rolls” to a string of menu labels without explanation.
- Finish a thought, leave room for the viewer to inspect it, then move the cursor. Keep useful pauses and natural breaths.
- Avoid a trailer voice, forced excitement, identical emphasis on every sentence, synthetic chuckles, and artificially added mistakes intended to simulate a person.
- Record unfamiliar terms and numbers twice. Listen for “not,” “plus,” “self,” “target,” BU and Cost: a small error can reverse the lesson.
- Rehearse the talking points before capture. The founder chooses their own words; keep the essential distinctions and correct visible labels. Do not accelerate an explanation to fit an arbitrary runtime.

## Capture and editing tools

The proposed local stack is **OBS Studio for capture, Shotcut for the edit, and Audacity for voice cleanup when needed**. These avoid a new hosted-video production account. This is a tool choice for the sample, not an instruction to install three applications immediately. [OBS recording guidance](https://obsproject.com/kb/standard-recording-output-guide), [Shotcut features](https://shotcut.org/features/) and [Audacity recording guidance](https://manual.audacityteam.org/man/tutorial_recording_multi_track_overdubs.html) describe the relevant capabilities.

The installed **Remotion best-practices skill** can guide repeatable chapter cards, caption overlays and later exports. It does not record a human explanation or make an edit convincing automatically. No Remotion project, dependencies, Studio server or render has been created for this planning work. If used later, keep the production project outside the website application's dependency tree and check its applicable license before selecting it for the production workflow.

Start with one clean capture/editor workflow. Do not build a video automation framework before approving the sample. Do not send raw private footage or voice to a transcription/voice service without choosing that route explicitly.

## Prepare the scene before recording

1. Freeze the relevant application commit, public deployment, fixture records and rule-source version in a shot manifest. Book consistency remains ongoing; identify the exact source used rather than claim every edition already matches.
2. Review the example's scope and acquisition price with the founder. The editor's starter is an authoring example, not an automatic price or a balanced product. Resolve any disagreement before recording the priced Identity fields.
3. Use a dedicated project demonstration account with no personal messages, account menus, bookmarks or private character notes visible. Keep demonstration saves private. Reading a public sheet and saving/forking are different states; show the actual sign-in requirement at the relevant point.
4. Rehearse the entire workflow, including save, success feedback and reopen. An edited recording may remove waiting time, but it cannot invent a successful save, a target update or automated resolution.
5. Use the fixed editor theme and viewport agreed in the sample. Confirm selected gold controls remain readable after the contrast fix. Hide notifications and unrelated browser chrome before capture; remove personal information in the source scene, not just with a last-minute blur.
6. Prepare a take list and a safe reset path. A failed take should not alter an existing founder draft or public Library record.

## Recording targets

These are proposed production settings, not accessibility standards or platform requirements. Change them if the actual sample looks better with another setup.

| Item | Starting target | Acceptance evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Master | 16:9, 1920×1080, 30 fps; higher-resolution source capture if the machine handles it smoothly | Editor labels stay crisp at ordinary playback; no frame drops during the demonstrated action |
| Browser scale | Choose a comfortable zoom before capture; record the setting | Main controls readable without full-screen playback on a laptop; intended phone crop also readable |
| Capture container | OBS MKV where practical; remux a working copy for the editor | A crash-resistant original is retained, and the editor opens the copy correctly |
| Voice | Separate clean mono track; 48 kHz recording where supported; retain the unprocessed source | No clipped samples, distracting room echo, hum, plosives or unstable volume |
| Delivery | A tested web-compatible MP4 plus poster and captions; final encoding chosen after sample inspection | Playback, seek, sound and captions work in the actual website player on desktop and phone |

OBS recommends MKV to reduce the damage from an interrupted recording and provides remuxing; this fits the recent PC crashes. Remuxing changes the container, not the underlying recorded demonstration. See [OBS's recording guide](https://obsproject.com/kb/standard-recording-output-guide).

Capture several seconds of stillness before and after each action. Make one deliberate cursor movement, click, then pause. Do not wiggle the cursor to fill silence. Keep the pointer beside the label after a click so it does not cover the selected text. Record the whole panel for context; create close crops from that real footage during editing.

## Edit the explanation, then polish

**Assembly:** Put correct actions in order; remove false starts and long loading waits; retain the cause and result of each edit. Mark a time skip when it could otherwise imply immediate processing or a completed step. Keep the first meaningful result near the beginning.

**Teaching edit:** Alternate the full panel with restrained close views of the control being explained. A zoom follows an actual decision, then returns to context. Keep persistent orientation labels such as “3 · Value” when cropping a dense editor. Do not pan while the viewer needs to read. Prefer clean cuts to ornamental transitions.

**Voice edit:** Start with the founder's natural explanation, not a forced word-for-word match to a draft. Use phrase-length pickups, not a splice on every word. Keep enough breaths and room sound to avoid choppy syllables or a gate that snaps open. Apply cleanup only to a demonstrated problem: aggressive denoising can sound watery, and heavy compression can flatten emphasis. Compare the cleaned voice with the original. Record again if processing cannot make a sentence clear naturally.

**Sound mix:** Begin the sample without background music. If music is later requested, it needs appropriate reuse rights and must stay unobtrusive beneath speech; it is not a substitute for pacing. Check the complete edit with headphones and ordinary laptop/phone speakers at a consistent comfortable volume. Confirm no clipping, abrupt loudness jump, missing syllable, distracting mouth noise or audible processing artifact. Set a measured loudness/peak target for the chosen delivery destination at mastering time; this plan does not invent one universal platform target.

**Finishing:** Use existing SwordWeave typography and restrained title/section cards. No stock AI presenter, generated UI, morphing controls, fake testimonial, synthetic cursor “proof,” or decorative fantasy B-roll hiding the actual interaction. A diagram may explain the five choices, but it must be clearly an explanatory graphic beside real evidence.

## Captions, transcript and visual explanation

Create captions **from the locked spoken edit**, then correct every word against the audio and the UI. A speaking script is not a finished transcript, and planned shot times are not caption timestamps. No final `.srt` or `.vtt` should be fabricated before a recording exists.

Supply an English WebVTT track for the website, SRT where distribution needs it, and an adjacent HTML/Markdown transcript including necessary descriptions of visual changes. Include meaningful non-speech audio if it conveys information. Explain crucial visuals in the narration itself: “Choose Attack Roll in Rolls & checks,” not “click here.” [W3C's captions guidance](https://www.w3.org/WAI/media/av/captions/) explains why automatically generated captions need human correction and how captions relate to transcripts.

Use short, phrase-based lines with comfortable reading time; normally one or two lines without covering the selected control or validation message. Do not split names, a number from its unit, or a negation from the phrase it changes. Check captions with sound muted and at the smallest intended player size. Decorative word-by-word karaoke is a poor fit for this tutorial. An optional social excerpt can carry burned-in captions, while the website version should retain user-toggleable captions.

If Remotion is selected, follow its current caption skill: keep reviewed timing in `Caption` JSON, and keep each clip and its captions in the same sequence so trims and speed edits cannot desynchronize them. Use the restrained Basic Captions treatment unless the sample justifies another choice. Export and verify sidecar tracks as well; a burned-in overlay is not a substitute for the website caption track. See [Remotion captions documentation](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/captions/).

## Roles, reviews and stop conditions

| Responsibility | Owner for this phase | Concrete output |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Product/rules correctness | Founder, with agent source comparison | Approved wording, fixture, price, limits and source references |
| Talking points, shot list and production assembly | Agent; founder explains in their own words | Reviewable shooting guide and sample edit |
| Voice | Founder or explicitly selected narrator | Approved raw voice and pickups, with usage permission |
| Comprehension check | Someone unfamiliar with this particular workflow | Can identify Result/Operation/Value/Recipient/Trigger and repeat the small task without coaching |
| Technical/player QA | Agent, then founder playback review | Caption, readability, sound, link and privacy checks against the exported file |
| Website/public release | Founder review of the actual finished asset | Explicit ready-to-publish decision; correct current destination and disclosure |

Rules/source checks can be delegated in parallel with capture preparation. A second agent should review a script against source without having written that same script. Recording, narration alignment and final timing remain one coordinated edit; parallel agents must not independently change the same timeline or caption file.

Reject an export if any of the following remains: an incorrect mechanic, a misleading automation claim, an unexplained scope/price assumption, unreadable selected controls, a voice the founder dislikes, caption/narration disagreement, exposed private data, wrong edition claims, or a task that a new viewer cannot follow. Log the defect against a shot and replace that section. Do not release while calling a substantive defect “alpha polish.”

## Versioned deliverables and maintenance

For each approved video retain the source recordings, clean and edited voice, editable project, master export, web export, poster, corrected caption source, VTT/SRT, transcript, chapter list, sources and review decisions. Large/raw files stay outside normal Git history; keep the lightweight script and manifest in the repository. Choose storage and hosting before uploading anything; no paid storage or account is implied here.

Suggested final file family: `create-a-primitive-v01-<release-date>-<app-commit>.*`. The manifest should record:

```text
Asset ID / editorial version:
App commit / production URL / capture date:
Rules source and exact edition:
Fixture names and public URLs where applicable:
Narrator / voice route / permission / disclosure:
Capture viewport / zoom / theme / fps:
Editor project and raw-source locations:
Caption source / transcript / export hashes:
Rules review / new-viewer review / founder decision:
Publication URL and destination page:
Known limitations / replace-when trigger:
```

Before replacing a video, preserve its version record and update its adjacent transcript, captions, thumbnail, description and source label together. Re-record only affected chapters when possible. Changes to editor labels, save flow, rule behavior or the demonstrated fixture trigger review; a cosmetic unrelated change does not require a full series remake.

No creator outreach, video hosting signup, channel publication, account creation, new application install, voice purchase or video render is performed by this document. The immediate next production step is reviewing the separate voice audition, then recording a representative founder-led excerpt using the talking points. The written tutorial and press materials can progress independently.
